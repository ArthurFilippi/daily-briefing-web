Russia achieved minimal territorial gains of 14 km² in May 2026 while suffering ~34,000 killed and wounded—approximately 1,100 daily—marking the slowest advance since autumn 2023. The attrition rate appears unsustainable relative to battlefield progress, contradicting Kremlin narratives of imminent victory. Source: Le Grand Continent

Ukraine intensifies drone strikes deep into Russian territory

Kiev is multiplying unmanned attacks across Russian soil, compounding military reverses with economic and morale damage. Combined with stalled offensive progress, these strikes feed domestic discontent in Russia and challenge Putin's strategic coherence. Source: Le Monde

President Trump stated Israel and Hezbollah have agreed to halt new military action, though Israeli PM Netanyahu did not explicitly endorse a cease-fire. Lebanese officials indicated one was taking shape. The arrangement remains fragile: Israeli forces intercepted projectiles from Lebanon hours after the truce announcement. Source: BBC / NYT

🔍 Signal of the Day

Why Schneider Electric's AI-augmentation strategy matters more than the layoff panic

The fact: French multinational Schneider Electric chose to deploy AI in manufacturing to amplify worker productivity rather than replace headcount—a deliberate counterweight to the narrative of AI-driven job destruction. The company redesigned workflows so AI handles repetitive tasks while humans focus on exception-handling and optimization.

The broader pattern: This sits within a larger tension emerging across 2026 earnings cycles. While Australian firms (per Mercer survey) anticipate up to 20% job cuts within two years, and short-seller sentiment assumes AI = layoffs, the productivity-augmentation model shows an alternative path. Companies like Schneider are discovering that AI's real value isn't labor arbitrage; it's throughput multiplication. A factory worker paired with AI can supervise 3x the output with equal cognitive load.

Implication for a future entrepreneur: As an engineering founder at Polytechnique, this signals a beachhead for deeptech in industrial ops. The winners won't be those who pitch "replace workers with AI"—regulatory and social backlash will crush that narrative. Instead, build for the "AI augmentation" use case: tools that let human teams do their jobs 3–5x faster, cut error rates, and unlock higher-margin work. Schneider's bet proves that CFOs will fund this, and workers will adopt it. Your deeptech moat lies in domain specificity (manufacturing scheduling, materials science, predictive maintenance) paired with human-in-the-loop design. The global economy doesn't need fewer workers; it needs workers who can handle exponentially more complex problems. Build for that.
