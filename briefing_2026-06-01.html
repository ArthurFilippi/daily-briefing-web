🌍 Geopolitics & Policy

Russia's Industrial Production in Sharp Decline Outside Military Sector

Russian non-military industry is collapsing across most sectors, with recent data from Rosstat showing accelerating declines that now extend to oil and gas—historically Russia's economic bedrock. The contraction reflects combined impact of sanctions, Western technology restrictions, and capital flight. This industrial hollowing will constrain Moscow's long-term capacity for sustained military spending, creating a potential strategic ceiling within 12–24 months. Source: Le Grand Continent

Israel Seizes Beaufort Castle in Southern Lebanon; Negotiations Resume

Israeli forces captured the Crusader fortress overlooking the Litani River on May 31, marking their first major territorial gain in Lebanon since withdrawing 26 years ago. The seizure provides negotiating leverage ahead of US-brokered talks scheduled for Washington on June 2–3. Both sides view the castle—strategically dominant but politically loaded—as a test of cease-fire credibility. Source: Le Monde

Nicaragua: Indigenous Leader Brooklyn Rivera Dies in Detention

Brooklyn Rivera, an Indigenous rights activist and former lawmaker, died while detained by Nicaragua's authoritarian government since 2023 on undisclosed charges. His death exemplifies regional judicial capture and suppression of dissent, signaling escalating human rights deterioration in Central America. Source: DW

Turkey: 262 Writers Denounce Judicial System as Instruments of Political Control

Including novelist Asli Erdogan and others, Turkish authors issued a joint statement condemning the removal of opposition leader Özgür Özel via judicial decision, framing it as evidence of captured courts serving executive power. This collective action signals rare unified resistance among Turkey's intellectual class against judicial politicization. Source: Le Monde

🔍 Signal of the Day

The Geopolitical Weaponization of AI Compute Capacity

Russia's industrial production is collapsing outside the military sector—a brutal reversal of supposed "sanctions resistance." Simultaneously, SoftBank overtakes Toyota to become Japan's most valuable company, powered by AI momentum. And Nvidia is consolidating chip dominance across consumer PCs, data centers, and inference via RTX Spark and Vera. These three events are not separate: they reveal that AI compute infrastructure is now the strategic asset of great-power competition.

Russia cannot sustain militarization without an industrial base; losing non-military production means losing the economic surplus needed to fund long-term military campaigns. Japan's corporate hierarchy pivot—from cars to semiconductors—reflects capital's verdict that whoever controls AI compute controls 21st-century economic rents. And Nvidia's dual expansion (consumer + enterprise) shows that whoever controls the silicon controls the entire value chain.

For a Polytechnique founder with deeptech ambitions, this has three implications: (1) compute is not a commodity—it's becoming a protected, strategic industry tier like nuclear or space; (2) geographic arbitrage is eroding—European startups cannot escape the US-China semiconductor duopoly via clever regulation alone; (3) allied access to chips will stratify opportunities—founders building on European soil will face longer latency to cutting-edge inference capacity than US-based counterparts, unless SoftBank's commitments translate into actual parity (uncertain). Start planning for compute constraints now, not later.
