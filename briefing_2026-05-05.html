🌍 Geopolitics & Policy

Hormuz Closure Forces 74 Nations Into Fragmented Energy Response

Over two months of Iran–US conflict have triggered uncoordinated energy contingency measures across 74 countries as ~20% of global crude flows remain disrupted. Each nation acting independently rather than via multilateral coordination, creating a chaotic "geopolitics of scarcity" with inflation risks spreading unevenly. This decentralised response signals breakdown of post-Cold War energy governance frameworks. Source: Le Grand Continent

US Navy Intercepts Iranian Missiles; Maersk Resumes Escorted Transit

US warships shot down Iranian missiles and drones targeting vessels in the Strait of Hormuz on May 4; Maersk's US-flagged commercial vessel exited under military protection after weeks of stranding. Iranian fast boats engaged by US forces simultaneously. Ceasefire appears fractured; shipping companies remain uncertain whether to resume transits. Source: BBC

Trump's 'Project Freedom' Operation Triggers Fresh Escalation

Trump administration declared military operation to reopen Hormuz, immediately provoking Iranian response. German Chancellor Merz urged Tehran back to negotiating table after drone/missile strikes on UAE oil infrastructure. Geopolitical risk premium now embedded in oil markets; no diplomatic off-ramp visible. Source: Le Monde, DW

NATO's Article 5 Credibility Crumbling; Europeans Forced to Rearm Independently

German withdrawal of 5,000 US troops announced; European leaders gathering in Yerevan (May 5) questioning whether US would defend NATO allies if attacked. Trump's public doubts about alliance commitment forcing EU to design autonomous defence posture—first time since 1970s that European rearmament proceeds without US reassurance. Poland already scaling civil defence training; massive capital reallocation toward indigenous weapons programmes inevitable. Source: Le Monde

🔍 Signal of the Day

The Critical Minerals Bottleneck Is Becoming the Gating Factor for AI & Green Energy

The Fact: Lithosquare's €22M seed round—the largest AI/deeptech critical minerals play in Europe—closes a glaring gap in the value chain. Lithium, copper, nickel discoveries are now rate-limiting steps for EV electrification and AI infrastructure (data centres consume ~10% of electricity in major grids; each facility needs ~3,000 tonnes of copper). Traditional geological exploration takes 7–10 years; Lithosquare applies machine learning to compress timelines and reduce dry-hole risk.

The Broader Pattern: China's dominance in wind turbines (via subsidies + import restrictions) is being replicated by Europe in resource mapping. Simultaneously, the Hormuz closure forced 74 nations into fragmented contingency plans, revealing the cost of supply-chain concentration. Critical minerals are the new chokepoint—more strategically important than oil in a decarbonised world. Every major VC and strategic investor is now scanning the critical minerals stack (mining tech, refining, recycling).

Implication for You: If you're building a deeptech startup, the killer advantage is now in enabling scarcity mitigation—not in consumer applications. Geopolitical fragmentation + the energy transition create structural demand for tools that find, process, or recycle constrained resources. Lithosquare validates the thesis: capital flows toward infrastructure that solves bottlenecks, not toward optimising existing abundance. Consider how your technical edge (AI, biotech, advanced materials) unlocks access to resource constraints.
