🌍 Geopolitics & Policy

OpenAI's Latest Model Operates Fully Autonomous Surveillance Drones

GPT-6 Astra has become the first AI model to pass Andon Labs' autonomous drone operation benchmark, enabling independent surveillance of individuals without human oversight. The capability represents a threshold breach in weaponisable AI—moving from assisted to fully automated decision-making in sensitive domains. This development arrives as US-China treasury talks position AI itself as a bilateral security concern requiring real-time warning systems, not products. Source: Le Grand Continent

Germany's Regional Defeats Deepen Chancellor Merz's Crisis

Chancellor Friedrich Merz's centre-right CDU crashed out of parliament in Mecklenburg-Vorpommern state elections on Sunday, while the far-right AfD surged; Berlin elections delivered parallel losses. Merz publicly pledged to remain in post, but German political precedent suggests deeply unpopular leaders rarely recover once public confidence fractures. His weakness now invites pressure from both flanks—labour-left and nationalist right—weakening EU coordination on defence and energy policy precisely as Trump-Xi tensions escalate. Source: Financial Times

France-Canada Defence Alignment Signals North Atlantic Recalibration

Prime Minister Mark Carney visited Saint-Pierre-et-Miquelon on Sunday to meet President Macron, immediately after the EU offered Canada "associate member" status—a diplomatic first. The visit was explicitly framed as a tactical and symbolic response to Trump's expansionist rhetoric (his recent interest in acquiring Greenland and Canada). Both nations now signal deepening defence and economic ties, cementing a secondary North Atlantic axis independent of Washington. Source: Financial Times

Taiwan Presses $14B US Arms Sale Amid Trump-Beijing Negotiations

As the Trump administration faces direct pressure from Beijing to freeze a proposed $14 billion weapons package to Taiwan, the island is demonstrating that its military security serves broader US strategic interests. Beijing's pushback signals willingness to trade on defence commitments during upcoming Trump-Xi talks, raising near-term risk of concessions on Taiwan's defensive capacity in exchange for AI or trade agreements. Source: The New York Times

🇫🇷 🇷 French/European Entrepreneurship

French Tech Raises €42.2M in Week; AI Captures 82% of Capital

Between September 14–18, nine French-based startups announced €42.2 million in total funding, with six firms deploying AI at their core capturing 82% of all capital deployed. The figure follows Mistral AI's outsize round the prior week, signalling a normalisation to tighter ticket sizes (€4–7M median range) while AI-first positioning remains a hard prerequisite for institutional cheques. This concentration reflects both genuine capability gaps in EU deeptech outside LLMs and investor herd behaviour following OpenAI and Anthropic valuations. Source: FrenchWeb

💼 Business & Finance

SoftBank Issues $11B Junk Bond for OpenAI Investment Surge

SoftBank Group announced a junk bond issuance exceeding $11 billion to fund accelerated investments in OpenAI, marking one of the largest leveraged bets on a single AI platform by a public conglomerate. The scale of financing—tied to a company that remains unprofitable and requires massive GPU capital expenditure—suggests SoftBank believes OpenAI's valuation trajectory justifies extreme leverage. Bond investors are effectively betting on rapid commercialisation of reasoning models and agent automation by 2027–28. Source: Bloomberg

US-China AI Talks Frame Technology as Bilateral Security Threat

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent described Sunday's talks with Chinese counterparts as "very successful," emphasising a shared vision on AI's strategic risks and benefits. Both sides pledged to develop early-warning systems for AI-driven national security threats. The dialogue reframes AI from a competitive market domain into a geopolitical stability issue, implying future bilateral agreements will constrain rather than accelerate deployment in sensitive sectors (defence, critical infrastructure, surveillance). Source: Bloomberg

Wheat Rallies on Constructive US-China Trade Signals

Wheat and corn futures jumped following positive initial US-China trade talks, with both sides describing discussions as substantive ahead of a Trump-Xi summit later this week. China has purchased over halfway to its 25-million-ton soybean commitment for 2026, though broader commitments on US agricultural imports remain stalled. The commodity move signals cautious risk-on sentiment, though persistence of trade tensions beyond commodities could quickly reverse gains. Source: Bloomberg

Firmus Grid Targets $5B in Landmark Australian Data Center IPO

Firmus Grid Ltd., an Australian data center operator, is seeking to raise at least $5 billion in what would rank as one of the nation's largest-ever initial public offerings. The IPO capitalises on surging AI infrastructure demand and undersupply of power-adjacent data facilities in the Asia-Pacific region. Successful pricing would validate investor appetite for utility-scale AI compute infrastructure as a distinct asset class, separate from cloud software. Source: Bloomberg

A top-performing T. The thesis implicitly accepts US-China decoupling as permanent and positions on the assumption that China's domestic AI hyperscaler demand will drive ancillary component suppliers regardless of Western sanctions. CATL Reports Limited Impact From China Battery Consumption Tax

Contemporary Amperex Technology Co. acknowledged volatility in domestic orders after China's new battery consumption tax took effect this month, but characterised the overall effect as contained. The company's resilience suggests either tax pass-through to consumers or sufficient margin to absorb levies—indicating China's battery supply chain remains structurally sound despite policy friction. EV volumes and energy storage deployments will likely continue absorbing CATL's near-term production. Source: Bloomberg

Australia Anchors Long-Term Growth on AI Development Amid Geopolitical Fragmentation

Australia's government has signalled that AI rollout and capability development will be the primary driver of economic growth over the next 40 years, a bet made explicit as global tensions deepen and regional supply chains fragment. The strategy implicitly concedes that traditional comparative advantages (agriculture, resources) will decline in real terms and that computational capacity is now the binding constraint on prosperity. It also positions Australia as a potential AI hardware and infrastructure hub if US-China restrictions tighten further. Source: Bloomberg

🔍 Signal of the Day

When Autonomous Weapons Enter the Venture Narrative

OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra passing the Andon Labs drone benchmark is not a product release—it is a clarification. For two years, AI safety discourse existed in a quarantine between academia and policy forums, rarely intersecting with VC incentives. Today that wall has dissolved. An AI system capable of autonomous human surveillance now exists; it was built by a private company; and it will be monetised. Simultaneously, US treasury officials are negotiating with Beijing on bilateral AI warning systems, tacitly acknowledging that neither government controls the commercial AI capability that matters most.

Why this matters: The venture logic of "first mover advantage" collides headlong with state-level security imperatives. A startup that trains a reasoning model for medical diagnostics faces the same API as a model trained for autonomous targeting. Founders seeking Series B capital in 2027 will inherit a regulatory inheritance: government pre-approval for any model above a capability threshold. This is not new (defence contractors have navigated this for decades), but it is new for software. The implication is a bifurcation: consumer AI remains in open competition; foundational AI (reasoning, embodied agents, real-world control) moves into a licensing regime similar to cryptography in the 1990s.

For your portfolio: This favours large incumbents (OpenAI, Anthropic) with sufficient capital and compliance infrastructure to weather licensing friction, and defence-adjacent compute makers. It punishes moonshot autonomous robotics startups betting on unregulated deployment. The most valuable AI ventures in 18 months will be those with tacit government approval or those narrowly scoped to non-dual-use verticals (diagnostics, materials science, climate modelling).
