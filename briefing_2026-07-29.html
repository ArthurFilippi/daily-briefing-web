At least 13 deaths confirmed; magnitude reported as high as 7.1 in some accounts, with significant infrastructure damage. Emergency response is active but casualty count may rise as debris clearance continues. Source: BBC / NYT

      🔍 Signal of the Day

      SK Hynix earnings miss reveals AI capex/revenue decoupling—and capital is running for the exits.

      The South Korean chip giant's 557% profit surge should have been celebratory. Instead, it missed consensus by enough to halt trading across Korean exchanges and trigger $60M in liquidated crypto leveraged positions. The paradox: record profit + disappointed investors = a market recognizing that AI capex is outpacing actual workload monetization.

      What this reveals: The AI infrastructure stack is displaying classic overinvestment dynamics. Chip makers like SK Hynix are burning cash ($31B capex guidance from Hynix alone this year) to expand production capacity for AI chips that are arriving into a market where end-user demand (cloud providers, enterprise deployments) is not yet at velocity to absorb supply. This is the "build it and they will come" problem. Investors are repricing around the likelihood that memory oversupply will compress margins before 2027.

      The implication for you: Watch where capital is flowing instead. European deeptech is seeing diversification into quantum (ZuriQ), regenerative medtech (Xeltis), and waste-AI (Greyparrot)—all with 6-36 month funding windows but fundamentally different risk/return profiles. If you're building in deeptech, the signal is clear: pure AI infrastructure plays face margin compression risk. Capital is rotating toward verticals with durable unit economics (circular economy, quantum, medtech, robotics) and away from commodity chip oversupply. The "AI everywhere" thesis is fracturing into "AI in things with real margins."
