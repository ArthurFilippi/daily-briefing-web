🔍 Signal of the Day

The Anthropic Export Ban: When Geopolitics Eats AI Sovereignty

The Trump administration's Friday evening export control directive forcing Anthropic to disable Claude Mythos 5 for foreign nationals marks the first major AI model weaponization by a US government—not as regulation, but as tactical control. The move was framed as cybersecurity precaution, but insiders and analysts agree it signals retaliatory control over an AI firm perceived as insufficiently aligned with administration priorities.

This sits within a broader pattern: China exports the PCBs that power AI (Kingboard +550% YTD); the US controls the models and now the export terms; Europe controls neither but claims AI sovereignty while its deeptech companies chase €30M Series Bs in quantum and biotech. The US has weaponized its AI lead into a geopolitical tool in real time—forcing allied companies into compliance on non-technical grounds.

For a 21-year-old entrepreneur reading this: export controls on frontier AI are now permanent policy, regardless of administration. If you build AI infrastructure, assume your TAM shrinks by 70% on day one (exclude China, Russia, and now potentially allied nations under secondary sanctions). The path forward is either (a) go where the US lets you (Israel, Japan, UK), or (b) build EU-native alternatives where regulatory capture is slower but funding is 3x harder. French/European deeptech startups sidestep this by focusing on physical substrates, biotech, and quantum—areas where US control is weaker. Plan accordingly.
