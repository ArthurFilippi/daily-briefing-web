🔍 Signal of the Day

When AI Agents Become the Unit of Capital Allocation

MoEngage's acquisition of agentic-AI technology—deploying millions of personalized agents—signals a structural shift in how software companies monetize scale. Instead of seat licenses or per-user pricing, the future unit is the agent: autonomous decision-makers optimizing customer interactions in parallel. This represents the inflection where AI moves from centralized language models (Zhipu, OpenAI) to distributed, task-specific micro-agents embedded in enterprise workflows.

The pattern: every vertically-integrated SaaS play (marketing, HR, finance, supply chain) is now racing to deploy agent swarms rather than rule-based automation. Profitability follows: higher customer LTV, lower churn (agents learn and improve over time), and defensible moat (agent training becomes proprietary data). Yet this also creates winner-take-most dynamics—the company with the best training data and lowest inference cost wins the vertical.

For founders: The next wave of venture returns will accrue to teams that (1) identify a fragmented vertical with high-touch labor (e.g., field sales, compliance, clinical notes), (2) deploy agent swarms that mimic that labor at 10-30% of human cost, and (3) iterate on agent performance via customer data loops. The €500M+ rounds going to Stark (drone swarms) and Astral (precision manufacturing) are betting the same way—distributed agents optimizing complex decisions at scale. For a 21-year-old building in deeptech, this means: your unfair advantage is domain expertise in a human-labor-intensive vertical, not raw AI capability.
