      🌍 Geopolitics & Policy

      Fed Raises Rates; Trump's Pressure Fails

      Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh led a unanimous decision to raise interest rates for the first time since July 2023, rejecting explicit White House pressure to cut. The move occurs less than two months before midterm elections and reflects determination to contain inflation despite Trump's threats to halt trade if rates were not reduced. Source: NYT, FT

      China's Pinglu Canal: Four-Year Build, 77-Year Gap

      Beijing inaugurated a 134-kilometer canal—its first major water infrastructure since 1949—completed in just four years, signaling accelerated state capacity for megaprojects. The canal addresses both agricultural water security and regional development ambitions across central provinces. Source: Le Grand Continent

      EU-Canada Associate Membership Rattles Trump

      The EU's proposal to grant Canada "associate member" status triggered threats of "very serious" tariffs from Trump, who called it a "hostile act." Berlin already opposes the fast-track approach, warning Ottawa risks alienating other EU partners. The clash exposes fractures in Western alliance architecture as the bloc attempts closer integration without US approval. Source: BBC, FT

      Ukraine Sustains Civilian Toll; Corruption Surfaces

      Russian airstrikes injured at least 12 people including two children in Kyiv overnight, with a 19-story residential building damaged in Odesa. Separately, an undercover recording exposed kickback schemes affecting Ukraine's defense procurement, highlighting corruption risks to Western military aid. Source: Le Monde, NYT

      🇫🇷 🇷 French/European Entrepreneurship

      Hackuity Lands €16M for AI Vulnerability Management

      Lyon-based cybersecurity startup Hackuity secured €16 million ($19M) led by Forgepoint Capital to accelerate its AI-powered Vulnerability Operations Center and international expansion. The funding reflects acute market demand: the firm positions itself against rising AI-driven security threats and what it calls the "vulnerability tsunami." Source: EU-Startups

      Integral (Berlin) Raises €18M Series A for AI Finance

      Berlin-based Integral announced a €18 million Series A co-led by Mosaic Ventures for AI-native accounting, tax, and payroll services targeting SMEs. The company has raised over €30 million in less than two years, validating rapid product-market fit in backend fintech. Source: EU-Startups

      Nearby Computing Secures €680K for Cloud-to-Edge Orchestration

      The Amsterdam-based deeptech startup raised €680,000 to scale its platform for orchestrating and automating distributed cloud-to-edge environments across networks, applications, and AI workloads. The round reflects growing infrastructure demand as enterprises shift compute beyond centralized data centers. Source: Tech.eu

      Veridion (Bucharest) Raises €17.5M for Business Intelligence

      The AI-powered startup announced Series A funding to expand its continuously updated digital replica covering 642 million global businesses. Hoxton Ventures led the round, emphasizing early-stage VC appetite for real-time intelligence infrastructure in Eastern Europe. Source: EU-Startups

      🔍 Signal of the Day

      Infrastructure Races Mirror Geopolitical Fragmentation

      China's four-year completion of the 134-km Pinglu Canal and Hong Kong's doubled innovation spending coincide with India's $30 billion chipmaking bet and the EU's acceleration of AI-native finance and cyber remediation infrastructure. What unites these? Each bloc is building independent operational capacity—water systems, chip fabs, clearing networks, and vulnerability detection—to reduce reliance on external actors. This is not duplication; it is deliberate strategic redundancy.

      The pattern extends to venture capital: French Hackuity and Berlin's Integral each raised €16–18M in rounds 2–3, not venture explosions, but sufficient velocity to hit profitability and customer lock-in faster than US incumbents can respond. Nearby Computing's €680K round is lean by comparison, yet it targets the physical-logical junction where cloud and edge converge—the infrastructure blindspot of centralized models.

      For entrepreneurs, the implication is directional: the next decade rewards builders who treat geopolitical fragmentation as a feature, not a bug. Firms that can operate across EU regulatory frameworks while delivering supply-chain redundancy (e.g., dual-region chip validation, multi-currency settlement) will capture premium valuations. The era of global optimization via moat-building in a single region is ending. Winners will be those who internalize friction—compliance complexity, latency, currency volatility—as customer value.
