🌍 Geopolitics & Policy

Putin and Xi Signal Deepening Russia-China Alliance Amid US Uncertainty

Russian president Putin met Xi Jinping in Beijing on May 20, lauding "unprecedented" and "strong positive" cooperation between Moscow and Beijing—a high-stakes diplomatic moment occurring just days after Trump hosted Xi and amid stalled US-led Ukraine negotiations. The timing underscores Moscow's pivot eastward as Western sanctions persist and suggests China may be incrementally upgrading military and economic commitments to Russia, even as public pressure for ceasefire emerges. This triangulation reflects a fundamental reordering: with US engagement unpredictable under Trump's "America First" doctrine, Russia and China are cementing alternatives to Western-led order. Source: Financial Times, Le Monde

Pentagon Cuts 4,000 Troops from Europe; US Reverts to Pre-Ukraine Deployment Levels

The US Department of Defense announced reduction of its European military presence by 4,000 personnel, bringing troop strength to 2021 baseline (pre-full invasion levels) and cutting from four to three brigades stationed on the continent. The withdrawal has already delayed Polish deployment timelines, signaling a concrete shift from post-2022 deterrence posture toward fiscal retrenchment aligned with Trump's campaign promises. This move, paired with ongoing Ukraine stalemate, weakens NATO's forward-positioned defense capacity and invites further Russian adventurism in Baltic and Eastern European theaters. Source: Deutsche Welle, Le Monde

Iran Leverages "Triangular Coercion"—Strait of Hormuz Closure and Gulf Attacks Signal Long-Term US Vulnerability

Despite military disadvantage, Iran has employed coordinated attacks on Gulf states and closure of the Strait of Hormuz to extract diplomatic concessions, revealing structural weaknesses in US regional dominance. Analysis indicates this "triangular coercion" strategy—simultaneous multi-vector pressure—may foreshadow prolonged confrontation and supply-chain vulnerabilities that markets have yet fully to price in. Simultaneously, VP Vance signaled "good progress" in ongoing negotiations and claimed Trump called off an attack at three Gulf nations' request, introducing ambiguity into US commitment levels. Source: New York Times, Deutsche Welle

EU Agrees Provisional Trade Deal with US Ahead of July 4 Tariff Deadline

The European Union secured tentative agreement on removal of import duties on American goods, positioning the bloc to meet Trump's July 4 ratification deadline and avoid escalatory 25% tariffs on European exports. The provisional framework reflects last-minute diplomatic coordination but underscores continued transactional vulnerability: EU credibility now depends on rapid parliamentary ratification, and Trump retains veto-level authority over tariff schedules. This trade uncertainty, combined with military retrenchment, signals gradual decoupling of US security and economic commitments to Europe. Source: Deutsche Welle, Le Monde

🇫🇷 🇷 French/European Entrepreneurship

EQT Deploys €5 Billion Superfund; "Over 100 Companies in Pipeline"

Swedish VC powerhouse EQT won the EU's €5 billion superfund competition and announced over 100 portfolio companies already in pipeline for scaling across Series C–exit stages. The superfund's capital density and long-duration thesis signal EU determination to build indigenous alternatives to US-dominated late-stage venture, with particular focus on deeptech and industrial software. Success hinges on execution velocity—deployment targets likely 18–24 months—and ability to retain talent and capital in face of continued US venture dominance. Source: Sifted

Payments Infrastructure Consolidates: Primer Raises $100M Series C; Balderton-Backed, Positioned for Expansion

European payments startup Primer, backed by tier-1 VC Balderton, secured $100 million Series C funding to accelerate developer adoption and geographic expansion in B2B fintech. The round validates market thesis that abstraction layers atop fragmented payment rails justify significant venture capital; Primer's trajectory mirrors broader consolidation in European fintech infrastructure where winners capture durable margin by reducing merchant friction. Capital deployment will likely focus on enterprise sales and API ecosystem deepening. Source: Sifted

French AI Consultancy Singulier Merges with German Ommax; Building "European Giant" in AI Services

French AI consulting firm Singulier merged with German counterpart Ommax to create an integrated European player positioned for enterprise scaling and cross-border service delivery. The merger reflects fragmentation in European AI advisory—no single firm has yet achieved incumbency comparable to US consultancies—and suggests consolidation will accelerate as clients demand full-stack capability. Combined entity targets rapid scale-up through integrated sales infrastructure and 24/7 linguistic/regulatory coverage. Source: Maddyness

RemotePass Raises $17.4M Series B; EBRD Participation Signals Emerging Market Expansion

Global payroll and hiring platform RemotePass closed $17.4 million Series B led by EBRD Venture Capital, with participation from 500 Global and existing backers. The EBRD's involvement explicitly targets Central/Eastern European expansion—zones of high talent cost-advantage and regulatory experimentation—and signals institutional capital alignment toward solving diaspora hiring and compliance friction. Series B deployment likely targets geographic expansion and localization across 15+ new markets. Source: Tech.eu

💼 Business & Finance

Bond Market Rout Threatens "Cheap Funds" Era; Central Banks May Turn Hawkish

Global fixed-income volatility and yield steepening across major sovereigns—exacerbated by Middle East war inflation expectations—are signaling end of multi-year low-rate regime that underpinned cheap venture and private equity capital. Bloomberg analysis suggests parallels to 2006–2007 pre-GFC dynamics, with compressed risk premia now vulnerable to sudden repricing. For founders and operators: valuations justified by 2% discount rates will face rapid recalibration if central banks shift stance. Source: Bloomberg Opinion

China Bonds Decouple from Global Rout; 9-Month Yield Lows Reflect Fragile Recovery and Ample Liquidity

Chinese sovereign bonds bucked global selloff, hitting 9-month yield lows amid PBOC's accommodative stance and economic recovery fragility—signaling capital flight hedging and reduced confidence in international growth. The divergence suggests China's zero-rate liquidity stance persists despite global inflation, creating arbitrage and cross-border imbalances. For European deeptech founders: limited access to Chinese concessional capital despite proximity means reliance on EU/US sources remains structural. Source: Bloomberg

Nvidia's $90 Billion Deal Spree Rivals Big Tech VC Operations; Ties Customers to Chipmaker Ecosystem

Nvidia has deployed $90 billion in acquisition and investment activity, effectively functioning as a quasi-venture fund that finances AI startups while securing lock-in to its GPU architecture and software stack. This vertical integration strategy—reminiscent of Intel's 1990s dominance plays—redistributes venture capital decision-making from independent GPs to chipmakers and creates existential risk for non-Nvidia-native stacks. Implication: founders must evaluate cloud infrastructure vendor lock-in as structural cost, not convenience. Source: Financial Times

Indonesian Export Controls Trigger Palm Oil and Coal Stock Collapse; Commodity Nationalism Escalates

President Prabowo's government announced tightened state control over commodity exports, immediately triggering sell-offs in Indonesian palm and coal equities on repricing of sovereign intervention risk. The move signals broader trend toward resource nationalism in Southeast Asia and signals that macroeconomic stability (inflation control, fiscal discipline) may now subordinate to political patronage and protectionism. For European manufacturers with commodity supply chains: Indonesia now poses higher policy risk and requires hedging. Source: Bloomberg

🔍 Signal of the Day

The US-China-Europe Triangle Is Reshaping Venture Capital Gravity

The past 48 hours have crystallized a geopolitical realignment visible in venture and M&A patterns: Putin's Beijing visit on May 20 (touting deepened Sino-Russian economic ties), combined with OpenAI's $234M Singapore commitment and concurrent US troop withdrawal from Europe, signals that US capital and talent concentration is diffusing across three poles—Chinese government-backed tech (Alibaba, Huawei), APAC regulatory havens (Singapore, Dubai), and EU-focused infrastructure plays (EQT's €5B superfund, Singulier-Ommax merger).

This fragmentation reflects underlying macro forces: (1) US venture returns compressed by geopolitical risk and higher rates; (2) China pursuing vertical integration to escape US export controls; (3) EU using public capital (superfunds, strategic funds) to build indigenous champions in deeptech and payments. The pattern is visible in today's news: Primer's $100M raise, RemotePass's EBRD backing, and CircuitHub's $28M for manufacturing all target European consolidation around supply-chain resilience, not US-style venture scale.

For an aspiring deeptech founder at Polytechnique: the implication is stark. The 2014–2021 thesis—build in Europe, raise from US funds, scale globally—is obsolete. Today's winning founders must choose their pole explicitly: (1) China-aligned stack (Alibaba, ByteDance infrastructure, SMIC fabs); (2) US-native (NVIDIA stack, Silicon Valley capital, but with geopolitical exit risk); or (3) EU-strategic (regulatory capture, public capital, but slower rounds and lower velocity). There is no neutral position. Your technology choice (chip architecture, data residency, talent geography) now is your geopolitical bet.
