    🌍 Geopolitics & Policy

    US Global Image Collapses; China Gains Diplomatic Ground

    For the first time since the early 2000s, international opinion surveys show China rated more favorably than the United States—a direct consequence of Trump's first year emphasizing tariffs, withdrawal, and nationalist rhetoric over alliance maintenance. This perception shift arrives amid concrete diplomatic friction: the US ambassador to Rome summering on a superyacht while Trump sparred with Meloni; bilateral tensions with Canada over wildfire smoke; and India's arrest of activist Sonam Wangchuk after a 20-day hunger strike protesting education policy. The symbolic distance between US commitments and actual behavior is widening faster than Beijing's messaging can fill it. Source: Le Grand Continent

    Iran Conflict Enters Sustained Escalation Phase

    The US conducted its seventh consecutive night of strikes against Iranian infrastructure—bridges, water plants, power facilities—as Iranian generals threatened "full-scale" retaliation if bombing continues beyond the next few days. Shipping through the Strait of Hormuz has dropped to seven-week lows; hedge funds added bullish oil bets at their fastest pace in a decade; and reports indicate US allies in the region also faced Iranian counter-strikes. The targeting of civilian-use infrastructure has drawn war crimes allegations from analysts. The conflict now appears structurally embedded rather than cyclical. Sources: BBC, NYT, DW, Foreign Policy

    Ukraine's Young Defense Innovation Cohort Faces Old-Guard Pushback

    Ukraine's drone-forward defense minister, who accelerated weapons innovation by cutting bureaucratic red tape and leveraging 20-something engineers, has been sidelined after military brass reasserted control. The pivot reflects institutional tension: technological speed versus procurement discipline. Meanwhile, the EU-Ukraine Drone Alliance formally launched, with DefenceTech startups Quantum Systems and Destinus as founding members, signaling that European defense innovation now runs through startup corridors as much as traditional primes. Source: NYT

    🇫🇷 🇷 French/European Entrepreneurship

    European Defense VC Reaches Record Scale; Lockheed Opens London Hub

    Lakestar closed its Resilience I fund at €262.2 million—oversubscribed—positioning it as Europe's largest institutional venture fund dedicated to defense and dual-use technology. Separately, US defense contractor Lockheed Martin Ventures committed at least €87 million to European DefenceTech as it opened a new London office, tapping into a €874 million global fund. This capital concentration signals a structural shift: French and European startups now compete for deployment alongside NATO and EU procurement cycles, not just product-market validation. Sources: eu-startups

    French Startups Rally €56M in Single Week; ENGO Raises €5.1M for AR Sports Eyewear

    French startups closed €56 million across multiple rounds in a single week. Grenoble-based ENGO, developing lightweight AR glasses for athletes, raised €5.1 million led by Ventech to accelerate miniaturization R&D and international expansion. The raise illustrates persistent European strength in hardware-software fusion and spatial computing—categories where US capital remains scarcer than software-only SaaS. Sources: Maddyness, eu-startups

    Prior Labs Exits to SAP for €1B+ Only 18 Months Post-Founding

    Berlin's frontier AI lab Prior Labs announced SAP's completed acquisition, backed by over €1 billion in investment from SAP for infrastructure, hiring, and frontier research. The speed—18 months from founding to €1B+ exit—underscores both the scale of AI infrastructure capital and the consolidation of frontier AI under incumbent enterprise software. Prior Labs will operate as a SAP subsidiary, suggesting that European frontier AI may increasingly become a research appendage to larger tech conglomerates rather than an independent competitive layer. Source: eu-startups

    Biotech Momentum: Sightera Closes €3M Pre-Seed for AI Drug Discovery

    Antwerp-based Sightera Biosciences, a University of Antwerp spin-off using generative AI for small-molecule drug design, raised €3 million in pre-seed funding led by Entourage, Anacura, and QBIC. The capital will fund team expansion. European biotech remains fragmented but increasingly AI-accelerated, with policy support (EQT securing €25M from the British Business Bank for life sciences) signaling institutional backing for the sector. Source: eu-startups

    💼 Business & Finance

    China's Moonshot AI Unveils Kimi Model; Markets React With Sell-Off

    Chinese AI startup Moonshot released a freely available large language model (Kimi) that appears to narrow the performance gap with US-built systems like OpenAI's. The announcement triggered sharp declines in AI and semiconductor equities on Friday, reviving memories of last year's DeepSeek shock. Oracle's credit risk metric hit an all-time high amid concerns about aggressive AI capex burn and future profitability in a competitive landscape. Retail traders crowded into leveraged AI/tech funds faced crushing losses. This suggests market pricing for US tech dominance is no longer resilient to competitive shocks. Sources: NYT, Bloomberg

    Big Tech Turns to Bond Markets for AI Data Center Financing

    Oracle and peers are tapping debt markets for "staggering sums" to finance data centers as AI capex requirements exceed cash reserves. Baker Tilly Advisory is raising ~$3 billion in debt to refinance private credit loans. This migration from equity/private credit to public bonds signals both confidence in revenue durability and underlying concerns about profitability timelines—a subtle credit market vote that "AI returns" remain unproven. Sources: NYT, Bloomberg

    Oil Markets Rally on Iran Conflict; US Drilling Extends Longest Streak Since 2022

    Brent oil reached its highest level in a month as Strait of Hormuz shipping approached halt. US oil rig count notched the longest streak without decline in over four years, and hedge funds added bullish bets at fastest pace in a decade. Energy inflation is now structurally embedded in macro assumptions, supporting downstream cost pressures for chip fabrication, transportation, and agricultural inputs. Sources: Bloomberg, NYT

    Food Safety Crisis: Cyclospora Outbreak Spreads; Taylor Farms Pulls Iceberg Lettuce

    Iceberg lettuce from Taylor Farms (a major producer across 12 US states and Mexico/Canada) linked to cyclospora (parasitic diarrhea) outbreak affecting 1,645 people across five states. Taco Bell and distributor Sysco removed Taylor Farms lettuce; FDA investigating Mexican shredding facilities. Supply chain vulnerability in fresh produce remains acute; this recall underscores how food logistics still lack digital traceability at scale despite two decades of e-commerce. Sources: NYT, BBC

    🔍 Signal of the Day

    China's Perception Inversion: What the Global Polling Shift Actually Reveals

    For the first time in 20 years, international surveys show China perceived as more favorably aligned with global interests than the United States. This is not a reflection of China's popularity per se—Xi's openness pitch on AI cannot erase governance concerns. Rather, it reveals a vacuum: as Trump deploys tariffs, symbolic governance (superyacht ambassadors), and unilateral strikes, Washington is not defending a narrative about its role. China, by contrast, is aggressively filling the frame with statements about "global collaboration" on AI, infrastructure investment, and regional stability language.

    This pattern cuts across three domains simultaneously: (1) Geopolitical perception management — who credibly claims leadership, not just dominance; (2) VC/defense strategy — European capital is repositioning away from US-led tech dominance and toward sovereign capability (hence Lakestar, Lockheed's €87M commitment, EU-Ukraine Alliance); and (3) AI competitive dynamics — Moonshot's public model release is framed as democratizing AI, while US giants defend proprietary advantage through bond-financed infrastructure.

    For you as an emerging deeptech founder: this moment favors builders who can articulate dual-use, globally-defensible missions (defense-adjacent, but not US-branded; AI-powered, but not US-captured). Startups that position themselves as "European sovereign capability" or "global collaborative infrastructure" will access capital faster than those claiming pure US-style venture dominance. The narrative war has shifted, and capital follows narrative.
