    🔍 Signal of the Day

    Why Compute Scarcity, Not Supply, Now Sets AI Valuations

    The $35B Anthropic-Lambda deal is not a contract—it is a ransom note. Frontier AI labs can no longer outbid each other on talent or data; the constraint is GPU clusters. Lambda, until now a niche inference provider, now controls access to tens of thousands of H100s; Anthropic commits to single-provider dependency to guarantee capacity during model training windows that may last 3–6 months. This flips power dynamics: chipmakers (Nvidia) now dictate terms to AI companies through chosen cloud partners, not the reverse.

    The broader pattern: capital is abundant, but silicon is not. Grok, Claude, Gemini, and GPT-5 will not be differentiated by model scale but by whoever secures the least-contested compute. European deeptech founders face a sharp lesson—raising €2.6M for quantum hardware (S-Transistors) is strategically rational precisely because GPU capacity for classical AI is already spoken for through 2027. The next venture cycle will bifurcate: AI infrastructure (compute, chips, cloud) attracts mega-rounds ($1B+); consumer/enterprise applications face margin compression as inference costs drop. Your position should anticipate the first cohort (hardware, systems) outpacing the second (apps) through 2026–27.
