    🌍 Geopolitics & Policy

    Iran deal live; Hormuz reopens to commercial shipping

    JD Vance defended the US–Iran interim nuclear agreement at the White House, rebutting criticism from both Israel and domestic hawks, though talks in Geneva were postponed citing logistical issues. Iran's supreme leader dismissed the accord as a US capitulation born of "desperation," signalling hard-line skepticism despite Tehran's participation. The US has lifted the naval blockade, reopening the Strait of Hormuz to commercial traffic. Source: DW, Le Monde

    India's heat stress poses ₹4.5% GDP drag by 2030

    New Delhi is enduring sustained temperatures exceeding 40°C, with climate economists projecting productivity losses equivalent to 4.5% of Indian GDP by 2030 if thermal stress persists. The figure reflects compounded losses across agriculture, labour capacity, and energy demand rather than direct casualties. This compounds existing headwinds: Indian software stocks tumbled Friday after Accenture warned of revenue growth deceleration, with the sector down ~30% YTD. Source: Le Grand Continent

    UK Labour faces internal challenge; Burnham wins Makerfield seat

    Andy Burnham, Greater Manchester's mayor and Labour's highest-polling figure outside Westminster, won the Makerfield by-election decisively, beating Reform UK's populist candidate. His victory clears parliamentary entry and positions him to mount a leadership challenge against Prime Minister Keir Starmer, whom allies describe as losing northern England. Burnham is characterised as charismatic and regionally rooted—a contrast to Starmer's centrist opacity. Source: Le Monde, FT

    EU extends Russia sanctions; Zelensky pushes accession pace

    The 27 agreed Thursday to prolong economic sanctions against Russia by 12 months at their summit in Brussels, with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky in attendance. Though European leaders applauded a "special moment," Zelensky's plea to accelerate EU membership negotiations was rebuffed; the bloc signalled continued dialogue over acceleration. The decision underscores EU caution on further escalation amid Iran deal fallout. Source: Le Monde

    🇫🇷 🇷 French/European Entrepreneurship

    E2D (€500M) launches as Franco-German defence-tech wager

    Earlybird Ventures and AVP announced E2D, a €500 million growth-stage fund targeting dual-use and defence technologies across Europe, representing one of the largest Franco-German collaborative VC vehicles. The fund targets ~20 companies at €15–40M ticket sizes, addressing Europe's acknowledged scaling gap in sensitive tech. Timing reflects EU anxiety over US–China bifurcation and defence self-sufficiency gaps exposed since 2022. Source: EU-Startups, Tech.eu

    Cargofy lands €9.6M Series A for AI freight automation

    Berlin-based logistics startup Cargofy closed a €9.6 million Series A (€5.2M primary, €4.3M secondary) to deploy AI-driven "digital workers" automating freight operations. The round represents accelerating appetite for operational automation in last-mile logistics, a sector facing persistent labour shortages. Cargofy competes in a crowded space but benefits from EU demand for supply-chain resilience post-pandemic. Source: EU-Startups

    French aging-care fund closes at €75M; Serena, UMR, Makesense convene

    Serena, the UMR, and Makesense announced a €75 million fund targeting startups addressing France's demographic transition—elder care, social services, digital health for seniors. The vehicle formalises a long-gestating policy priority (aging population) into venture capital, signalling institutional acceptance of societal-impact theses in France. Fund size and co-sponsors (ecosystem players rather than traditional LPs) suggest patient, policy-aligned capital. Source: Maddyness

    UK business bank commits €46M to FPE Capital Fund IV

    The British Business Bank announced a €46 million (£40M) cornerstone commitment to FPE Capital's Fund IV, backing high-growth SMEs across the UK and Ireland. This follows a prior €23M commitment to Fund II in 2017, signalling sustained confidence in mid-market European growth investing. FPE's strategy reflects post-Brexit repositioning toward domestic capital flows. Source: EU-Startups

    💼 Business & Finance

    Emerging-market equities touch record high on Hormuz reopening

    A gauge of emerging-market equities reached record levels Friday as the Strait of Hormuz reopened to commercial shipping following the Iran deal, signalling potential downward pressure on global crude prices. However, the rally masks fragmentation: India's state refiners have already secured two months of crude and are in no rush to resume Middle East purchases, while currency traders simultaneously loaded dollar call options on Fed hawkishness. The divergence reflects asymmetric positioning across asset classes. Source: Bloomberg

    Fed hawkishness triggers dollar rally; gold, sterling under pressure

    Goldman Sachs cut its year-end gold price target by $500/oz, citing the Fed's decision to hold rates steady rather than cut in 2026. Simultaneously, currency traders piled into dollar call options following the Fed's recent hawkish pivot, and Goldman flagged sterling as the most overvalued G10 currency, presaging downward pressure. The confluence reflects a structural shift: higher US rates outweighing geopolitical risk-off and commodity safe-havens. Source: Bloomberg, FT

    Indian software stocks tumble as Accenture signals growth slowdown

    Indian software services firms experienced sharp declines after Accenture warned of slower-than-expected revenue expansion, accelerating a YTD sector decline of ~30%. The selloff reflects broader concerns over demand fragmentation in digital services as enterprises slow spending post-AI capital cycles. Tamil Nadu and Bangalore-listed majors absorbed the losses. Source: Bloomberg

    China trade dialogue over confrontation; Taiwan Strait posture unchanged

    The EU opted for continued dialogue with Beijing rather than immediate trade action, citing retaliation risks, even as Australia faces a 55% tariff on beef exports to China after hitting Beijing's annual quota. New Zealand's meat and dairy exports to the US surged to monthly record highs, reflecting trade diversion away from China. The asymmetry—EU caution vs. US market access gains—underscores divergent transatlantic strategies. Source: FT, Bloomberg

    🚀 Tech / Deeptech / VC

    US flags ASML's advanced lithography machine may have reached China

    US Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick warned ASML's leadership that one of its top-tier extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography machines may have been diverted to China, violating US export restrictions. The alert represents the most significant challenge to ASML's operations under the Trump administration and underscores escalating semiconductor supply-chain weaponisation. ASML has not yet publicly commented on verification or remediation steps. Source: Bloomberg

    Anthropic's Mythos AI access preserved for early testers despite US order

    Some companies selected by Anthropic for early-access testing of its Mythos AI model retained access despite a broader US government order halting other versions of the system. The preservation suggests either carveouts for national-security-cleared entities or enforcement discretion. The incident highlights tensions between US export controls and AI capability distribution. Source: Bloomberg

    Elastic acquires DeductiveAI for up to $85M in AI debugging play

    Search infrastructure company Elastic agreed to acquire DeductiveAI, a three-year-old startup using AI to identify and resolve software bugs, for up to $85 million. The acquisition reflects consolidation appetite in AI-assisted development tooling and validates DeductiveAI's CRV-backed thesis. The deal underscores Elastic's pivot toward applied-AI product layers. Source: TechCrunch

    Baseten raises $1.5B at $13B valuation; inference-scale funding continues

    AI inference infrastructure startup Baseten is finalising a $1.5 billion funding round at a $13 billion post-money valuation, months after its previous mega-round. The capital infusion reflects sustained conviction that inference-scale compute will be a long-term margin driver as models commoditize. Baseten competes with Together AI, Replicate, and other inference platforms in a congested market. Source: TechCrunch

    🔍 Signal of the Day

    Emerging-Market Bifurcation: Capital Flows Follow Geopolitics, Not Just Yields

    Emerging-market equities hit record highs Friday on Hormuz reopening and Iran deal implementation, yet India's state refiners are not rushing back to Middle East crude, preferring their pre-secured supplies. Simultaneously, currency traders loaded dollar calls on Fed hawkishness, and Thailand is attracting capital inflows away from Indonesia on perceived fiscal-strength grounds. What appears as a unified "risk-on" trade masks three distinct narratives: (1) commodities benefiting from Hormuz reopening, (2) Asia's tech exporters facing margin compression from US rate expectations, and (3) capital selecting specific emerging markets on institutional-strength metrics rather than broad commodity beta.

    This fragmentation matters for an entrepreneur with EM exposure or EM-dependent supply chains. The old playbook—"Fed cuts = EM rally"—has inverted: Fed hiking + geopolitical resolution = selective EM outperformance only where governance or growth differentiate. India's heat-stress GDP drag (4.5% by 2030) and Accenture's software-growth warning suggest that structural headwinds (climate, secular slowdown) now override tactical geopolitical relief. For deeptech or logistics plays with India operations, factor in both labour availability pressure and customer-base demand uncertainty; neither is offset by oil-price relief.
