    🌍 Geopolitics & Policy

    Houthi Escalation Over Bab el-Mandeb Threatens Red Sea Traffic

    Yemen's Houthi forces claimed responsibility for missile and drone attacks on Saudi Arabia's Riyadh and Abha airports on Wednesday, killing three foreign nationals and wounding 36 others. Separately, rival factions dispute control of the Bab el-Mandeb strait—a chokepoint through which 12% of global maritime trade flows—as Aden-backed forces contest Houthi territorial claims. Combined with recent Iranian regional posturing, the escalation is raising insurance premiums on container shipping and pushing oil futures higher. Source: BBC, Le Monde, Bloomberg

    France Braces for Second Mass Student Protest Wave

    Nationwide high school and university blockades resumed Thursday as the far-left mobilizes behind student demands for curriculum reforms and defense spending cuts. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu publicly acknowledged legitimate grievances but signaled budgetary immobility on core requests, indicating a protracted standoff. This backdrop complicates fiscal credibility just as American asset managers flag elevated French sovereign spread risk. Source: BBC, NYT, Le Monde

    Italy Debates Electoral Bonus-Seat Law; Democracy Concerns Grow

    Italy's government is advancing a controversial electoral reform granting bonus parliamentary seats to election winners—proponents cite governance stability, critics warn of democratic backsliding in a country with a 70-year history of short-lived coalitions. The proposal reflects renewed right-wing pressure to entrench governing majorities across the eurozone. Source: NYT

    IMF Approves $1.21B Pakistan Bailout Amid Middle East Exposure

    Pakistan has reached a tentative IMF agreement worth $1.21 billion as regional spillovers from the Iran–Saudi conflict drain foreign reserves and push the rupee toward record lows. Weak monsoon rains are compounding food-price inflation, adding macroeconomic pressure. Source: DW

    🔍 Signal of the Day

    Why France's Startup Funding Pipeline Is Narrowing—And What It Means for Scale-Up Ambitions

    Today's cluster of French/European founding announcements—Rivercell (€22M), VoltR (€22M), Netsec (€8.93M), CLEAVR (€8M), Konsileo (€5.9M)—reveals a structural funding ceiling that contradicts Europe's sovereign tech ambitions. Each round sits below €25M, reflecting capital discipline rather than abundance, and each required either public subsidies (VoltR, CLEAVR) or international LPs (Partech, Sequoia, HV) to close.

    Beneath this pattern lies a two-layer problem: American asset managers are reweighting eurozone sovereign exposure downward due to French fiscal stress, which tightens LP dry powder available for European fund deployment. Simultaneously, rising US interest rates and the lure of Nvidia-adjacent opportunities (memory chips, AI infrastructure) are concentrating capital in a narrow set of defensible bets—leaving most European deeptech founders in a "middle valley" where they're too expensive for seed-stage pure-plays but lack sufficient product-market validation for mega-rounds.

    For an engineering student with entrepreneurial ambitions in French/European deeptech: this moment is paradoxical. Round sizes are smaller (less dilution risk), but capital is slower to commit (longer fundraising cycles). The winners are founders solving hard infrastructure or regulatory problems with public-sector tailwinds (energy, cybersecurity, biotech) and those with prior exits or US network density. Pure-software or B2B SaaS plays without differentiated unit economics face a 24-month fundraising winter. If you're building, prioritize revenue traction and international pilot contracts over raising press releases.
