    China Moves to Regulate AI Companion Dependencies

The framework represents Beijing's pivot from growth-stage AI towards behavioral guardrails, mirroring past social-media interventions. This signals regulatory intent to prevent Western-style attention-capture models while maintaining state-level oversight of digital relationships. Source: Le Grand Continent

    US-Iran Conflict Escalates; Trump Signals Infrastructure Targeting

This divergence reflects continued reliance on external demand and signals domestic fiscal stimulus may be insufficient to reignite internal consumption. Source: FT, NYT

    🔍 Signal of the Day

    AI Regulation and Market Fragmentation: China's Companion Rules Foreshadow Jurisdictional Divergence

    Today's enforcement of China's AI companion personalization rules represents a pivot from growth-stage AI licensing toward behavioral guardrails. Beijing explicitly targets "emotional dependency" mechanisms—the engagement loops that Western AI labs optimize for. The rule covers recommendation systems, conversational continuity, and gamification features that manufacturers like OpenAI and Anthropic treat as core product vectors.

    What this reveals: AI market fragmentation is now structural, not temporary. China's regulatory approach (content control + dependency prevention) will force non-Chinese developers to maintain separate model architectures and training objectives for mainland users. This mirrors earlier fintech and social-media splits. The regulatory distance between Beijing, Brussels (AI Act), and Washington (light-touch) is widening—not converging.

    For founders: This means the "global AI model" era is closing. Teams building consumer AI (companions, dating, health coaching) will need to architect for three regulatory regimes simultaneously or accept geographic exclusion. State-sponsored defenses for critical AI (like Destinus's European missile system) signal that deep-tech founders have a structural advantage in jurisdictions willing to fund sovereignty bets. Founders whose business model depends on frictionless planetary scaling (Neko, Overtone, etc.) should begin modeling regional divergence into unit economics now.
