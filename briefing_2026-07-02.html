    🌍 Geopolitics & Policy

    US-China AI Duopoly Locks Out Global Competition

    Of the 50 most-used AI models globally in June, 28 were American-developed and 19 Chinese, totaling 94% of daily deployments. The remaining 6% comprises European, British, and other jurisdictions' offerings—a dramatic concentration that reflects both training-data advantages and hardware access. This structural dominance translates into standard-setting power: downstream platforms, regulators, and enterprises default to US or China infrastructure, making independent AI sovereignty a multi-year capex sprint for any rival nation. Source: Le Grand Continent

    Russia Intensifies Kyiv Strikes; Iran-US Nuclear Talks Stall

    Russian missiles and drones struck Ukraine's capital on July 1–2, killing at least 8 civilians and marking the largest bombardment in weeks, hours after President Zelensky warned of a "massive strike" incoming. Separately, Iran-US negotiations initiated mid-June over nuclear compliance have yet to address the Iranian program itself; Tehran insists on retaining control of the Strait of Hormuz shipping lane, a red line for Washington. Both conflicts remain kinetic and unresolved, constraining regional business activity and prolonging energy-market uncertainty. Source: BBC News / Le Monde; Source: Le Monde

    US Resumes Dollar Transfers to Iraq; Strategic Pivot

    The Trump administration restarted dollar shipments to Baghdad after a months-long suspension, signaling a recalibrated approach to Iraqi sovereignty and Iran containment. The halt had been leveraged as pressure on the Iraqi government to distance itself from Tehran; resumption suggests either tactical compromise or a shift toward stabilizing Middle East capital flows ahead of 2027. Source: The New York Times

    France Pursues War-Crimes Cases Against Israel Over Lebanon Strikes

    A second complaint was filed in Paris by a Franco-Lebanese plaintiff alleging Israeli bombing of civilian targets in Tyre (April 2026) constituted war crimes and crimes against humanity. The lawsuits exploit France's universal jurisdiction statute, creating legal exposure for Israeli military operations in the Eastern Mediterranean and signaling European civil society pressure on Tel Aviv. Source: Le Monde

    🔍 Signal of the Day

    The Profitability Trap: Why European DeepTech Wins Look Like Losses

    Withings' 18-year profitability journey and Poppins' reimbursement victory appear on the same news cycle as EquiLibre's €438M valuation for an AI trading lab with zero revenue disclosure and Ruya Ventures' €43M fund explicitly betting on lab-to-market transition failure rates. The pattern reveals a structural misalignment: European deeptech and health innovation require longer capital patience and deeper regulatory entrenchment than US/Chinese venture models reward. Withings succeeded not because it raised €500M Series D, but because it survived long enough for wearables-for-health to become standard care—a 18-year bet venture returns can't justify. Poppins' reimbursement is a 2–3 year slog per health system. Meanwhile, EquiLibre is valued like Stripe at Series A, capitalizing on raw AI agent potential unproven in production. For an École Polytechnique founder targeting France/EU markets, this creates a dual trap: pursue venture-scale growth (VC expects 10x in 5 years; deeptech scales in 10+) or accept that regulatory moats and clinical evidence—your actual defenses—take a decade to build. The implication: if your idea requires profitability proof, health reimbursement, or hardware supply chains, EU capital markets will underfund you versus US AI-native competitors. Either raise 2–3x capital from patient LPs (family offices, corporates, EIB), or pivot to AI infrastructure/software where 2-year scaling is possible.
