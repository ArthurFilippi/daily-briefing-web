SFR Telecoms Acquisition at €20.35bn Faces Antitrust Showdown

Patrick Drahi agreed to sell SFR to a consortium of Bouygues Telecom, Orange, and Free-Iliad for €20.35 billion, with approval from French and EU competition authorities critical to completion. The deal would eliminate SFR as an independent operator, reducing France's telecom competitors from four to three and sparking union concerns over 20,000+ job losses. Regulatory review will determine whether consolidation serves infrastructure efficiency or violates competition thresholds. Source: Financial Times; Le Monde

🔍 Signal of the Day

The Asymmetric Warfare Shift: Why Ukraine's Drone Strikes Matter Beyond Tactics

Ukraine's second long-range drone strike on St. Petersburg in days—forcing the city's first indoor alert since 2022—represents a fundamental shift in conflict economics that will shape geopolitics for a decade. The fact: a relatively resource-constrained military, using domestically-developed or adapted drones costing €50,000–€200,000 per unit, can now degrade the operational capacity of a peer nuclear power's second-largest city and force civilian disruption deep behind enemy lines.

The broader pattern is asymmetric warfare going mainstream across all theaters. Iranian drones threaten Gulf maritime traffic; Hezbollah deploys precision munitions from Lebanon; North Korea refuses to negotiate weapons; Israel operates across multiple borders without consequences. Each actor has discovered that low-cost distributed strike capability—drones, cruise missiles, networked munitions—bypasses traditional air defense and creates political costs that exceed hardware investment. The strategic competition is no longer between tank armies but between drone production capacity and air-defense saturation.

For future entrepreneurs, especially in deeptech: this is the inflection point where defense tech becomes venture-capital-friendly because the economics flipped. A startup that can manufacture AI-guided micro-drones at scale, improve terminal guidance with computer vision, or design distributed swarm coordination will find buyers across NATO, EU, and US allies desperate to offset Russian production rates (estimated 5,000+ drones monthly). The 2026–2030 window is when defense tech venture funding will concentrate heavily on autonomous systems, sensor fusion, and production scalability—not platform capex, but capability density.
