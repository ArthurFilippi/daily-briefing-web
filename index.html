🔍 Signal of the Day

The Infrastructure Layer Is Now Where Value Accrues

The fact: In one week, we've seen $10B+ committed to AI deployment infrastructure (OpenAI JV), a $1.5B consulting services JV (Anthropic–Blackstone–Goldman), a €3M customs automation raise (Nabu), Europe's first drone procurement platform (Intelic BASE), and multiple data center IPOs totaling billions. Concurrently, White House officials met Google to address "insufficient computing power for AI"—treating infrastructure as a national security issue.

The broader pattern: After 18 months of model-centric hype (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini), capital and policy attention have pivoted decisively toward deployment infrastructure. The constraint is no longer "can we build good models?" but "can we deploy them at scale?" This mirrors the internet's evolution: after the web browser matured, value moved to hosting, CDNs, and cloud infrastructure. Enterprises cannot adopt AI at speed without integration services, procurement platforms, and backbone compute capacity.

Implications for you as a future entrepreneur: If you're building in deeptech or tech in 2026, the most defensible positions are not model development (crowded, capital-intensive, dominated by well-funded labs). Instead, pursue: (1) Vertical deployment services (Anthropic's approach—enterprise consulting around specific workflows); (2) Infrastructure abstractions (Intelic's role in defence procurement, or Nabu's regulatory compliance automation—platforms that reduce friction in procurement/deployment); (3) Specialized compute/inference (Cerebras, Ouster's lidar—domain-specific hardware that solves bottlenecks in specific verticals). These are where sustainable moats exist and where mid-market capital (€3–50M) can build defensible, profitable businesses without competing with labs on raw capability.
