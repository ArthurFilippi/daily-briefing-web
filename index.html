    Nepal-Tibet Glacial Flood: 160+ Killed, Hundreds Missing

    An ice avalanche from a Himalayan glacier triggered a catastrophic wall of water through a river valley shared by Nepal and Tibet on August 25–26. At least 160 confirmed deaths; hundreds remain missing, including over 100 Indian pilgrims en route to Kailash. Satellite analysis suggests the event required no heavy rainfall—an unusual trigger for such force. Initial assessment points to climate-driven glacier instability. Source: New York Times

The accord de-escalates immediate shipping risk but conditions its duration on Washington's negotiating stance, preserving leverage for broader nuclear/sanctions talks. Oil markets reacted to reduced immediate supply risk. Source: Foreign Policy

    🔍 Signal of the Day

    Compute Intensity as Venture Moat

    The fact: Anthropic locked $45B infrastructure capacity with Nscale; Amazon is purchasing 2M Nvidia chips; Nvidia projects 70% annual growth through 2028. Simultaneously, smaller AI-software companies face a vise: they cannot afford proprietary training runs, inference scale becomes CapEx-prohibitive, and pre-trained model licensing (OpenAI, Anthropic, Mistral) becomes cheaper than building. The "race to train" has already stratified into mega-cap and unicorn tiers—everyone else is middleware.

    Broader pattern: This mirrors the late 1990s internet infrastructure cycle, when bandwidth hoarding and CDN dominance made edge compute the real business, not web apps. AI is repeating that story: the "moat" moves from algorithm to infrastructure. Regulatory capture (Meta's $18B settlement) + compute scarcity + power/cooling bottlenecks create barriers that favour consolidated, capital-rich players. European startups are learning this painfully—€8.7B in deep-tech VC raised in 2025 pales against €115B in state defence procurement, signalling capital markets price small AI software at near-zero while states bet on hardware/sovereignty.

    Implication for an aspiring founder: If you cannot command $1B+ of capital or a defensible data/regulatory moat (think: domain-specific LLM trained on proprietary enterprise data; medical AI benefiting from FDA breakthrough status; or regulatory-arbitrage plays in regions with lighter AI governance), your AI company is likely a feature, not a platform. The winning venture thesis circa 2026 is: find the constraint (power, cooling, regulatory approval, data) that prevents large competitors from serving a segment—not better algorithms. Daraxonrasib succeeded because it solved a rare-cancer problem via precision, not raw compute.
