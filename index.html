    🌍 Geopolitics & Policy

    Musk alleges US government behind Starlink speed limits to Ukraine

    Ukraine reports improved Starlink access but faces persistent speed constraints disrupting combat operations. Musk publicly attributed the slowdown to US government intervention. This escalates the question of whether satellite internet is geopolitical infrastructure or a commodity utility—a critical dependency for Kyiv now uncertain. Source: BBC

    North Korea tests submarine-launched cruise missiles

    Pyongyang conducted successful test-fires of strategic cruise missiles designed for submarine launch, with state media releasing photos of horizontal flight over open water. The escalation reflects accelerating weapons development amid rising regional tensions and underscores the peninsula's role in the broader US-China-Russia strategic triangle. Source: DW

    Ukraine resumes strikes on Russian refineries as US intelligence sharing weakens

    Zelenskyy expressed public anger in an FT interview at the "dangerous" withdrawal of US intelligence-sharing and degradation of Starlink service at a critical moment. Ukraine now plans to resume refinery strikes independently, signaling a pivot toward self-sufficiency even as Western support softens. Source: FT

    Indonesia's megafires blanket region in toxic haze; health crisis mounts

    Millions across Indonesia and neighboring countries face severe air pollution from ongoing wildfires exacerbated by El Niño and human deforestation. Authorities face mounting criticism over inadequate fire prevention despite months of dense smog and documented health impacts. The crisis reveals governance failures in managing transnational environmental risks. Source: Le Monde

    🔍 Signal of the Day

    Geopolitical infrastructure now trumps neutral-platform ideology

    The convergence of three events Thursday reveals a structural shift: Musk publicly blamed the US government for Starlink throttles to Ukraine; OpenAI's fired engineer advised Saudi Arabia's government on AI policy; and India's telecom regulator blocked Starlink via political pressure from Ambani-backed competitors. Simultaneously, North Korea tested submarine-missile capability and the US announced an execution livestream.

    These are not isolated incidents—they reflect a hardening norm that critical infrastructure (satellite, compute, energy networks) is now treated as geopolitical leverage, not neutral plumbing. Silicon Valley's decade-long narrative that technology transcends borders has collided with state power reasserting control over connectivity and compute.

    For entrepreneurs: this creates both risk and opportunity. Risk is obvious—regulatory capture, forced localization, export controls on chips and models. But opportunity lies in the European and allied-nation pivot toward sovereign AI infrastructure—domestically hosted models, European satellite networks (OneWeb, etc.), and defense-grade compute. French and European deeptech founders building in semiconductors, quantum, space, and secure comms now have explicit policy backing (see Nvidia's €250M commitment, UK sovereign VC fund backing Augur, DARPA-style European initiatives). The 2026–2028 window may be peak funding for "strategic autonomy" infrastructure plays—before geopolitical fragmentation becomes fully priced into venture markets.
