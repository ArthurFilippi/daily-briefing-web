🌍 Geopolitics & Policy

Israel Election Yields Hung Parliament; Coalition Building Uncertain

Netanyahu's Likud-led bloc secured 53 seats in the Knesset, falling short of the 61-seat majority required to form government. Opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot's camp won 48 seats, with neither grouping commanding control. President Isaac Herzog, historically cautious, may push toward a unity coalition—a scenario his father, President Chaim Herzog, orchestrated 42 years prior. Coalition negotiations are expected to be complex and protracted. Source: DW, NYT

Iran Signals Readiness for Confrontation Despite Diplomatic Channels

Tehran has instructed defense officials to prepare for potential strikes from the US and Israel, while maintaining backchannel diplomatic contact through regional intermediaries. The messaging reflects a dual-track posture: preparing for military escalation while keeping negotiation pathways open. This stance underscores heightened regional volatility ahead of potential policy shifts under a new US administration. Source: Le Monde

China Accelerates Anti-Corruption Purges; 33 Officials Expelled

Xi Jinping's enforcement of party loyalty intensified with the expulsion of 33 officials, including prominent diplomat Liu Jianchao, as Xi prepares for an unprecedented fourth term. Observers characterize the purges as efforts to consolidate authority rather than genuine anti-corruption measures. This concentration of power signals Xi's intention to tighten control over both civilian and military institutions. Source: BBC, NYT

EU and Canada Explore "Associated Membership" Model

Ursula von der Leyen and Canadian officials maintained discussions on making Ottawa an "associated member" of the EU during a summit in Montreal. Germany expressed clear reservations about the terminology, creating divisions within the bloc. The initiative reflects EU efforts to deepen North American ties amid geopolitical flux. Source: Le Monde

EU Releases 42.6 Million Barrels From Strategic Reserves Ahead of Winter

Eleven EU member states are drawing down strategic petroleum reserves to stabilize diesel prices entering the winter heating season. The coordinated move addresses energy price volatility while the bloc affirms that supply security remains intact. This action signals energy security concerns persist despite assumed supply normalization. Source: Le Monde

🔍 Signal of the Day

Malaysia Becomes Unlikely Hub for US-China AI Data Center Race

The US-owned startup planning a $600M rare-earth refinery in Malaysia, combined with Envision's multi-GW data center build and existing Nvidia/US chip company operations, reveals Malaysia as a critical chokepoint in the AI infrastructure Cold War. Both Washington and Beijing are deploying capital into the same geography—competing for land, power, and regulatory favor—precisely because Malaysia sits outside direct US export controls on advanced chips to China.

This bifurcation within a single country creates two overlapping patterns: (1) supply chain localization—US firms are pre-positioning rare-earth processing and data center capacity closer to Asian demand, reducing China transit; (2) regulatory arbitrage—Malaysia's relative neutrality and abundant hydropower make it attractive to both blocs, but that same neutrality is now under stress as Washington tightens chip export policy. Malaysia will face mounting pressure from Washington to restrict Chinese access to US technology that flows through its territory.

For a 21-year-old deeptech founder, this signals an opportunity window: regional governments (Vietnam, Thailand, Indonesia, Philippines) are competing to attract AI infrastructure capex, and most lack the policy frameworks to manage US-China demands coherently. Building compliance/localization SaaS for data center operators navigating divergent export rules, or infrastructure-as-a-service platforms that can shift workloads between geographies as policy changes, offers asymmetric returns before the market hardens around a stable geopolitical equilibrium.
