    🔍 Signal of the Day

    "Return on Investment" Now Trumps "Return on Impact" in AI Capital Allocation

    This week's market collapse exposes a structural flaw in the AI capex narrative: investors conflated transformational technology potential with guaranteed financial returns. David Allen (Plato Investment Management) observed that Thursday's selloff stemmed from recognizing that token prices face competitive downward pressure from open-source models, slowing revenue growth at leading AI labs just as financing costs rise and IPO calendars slip.

    The political catalyst is real—Democratic victory makes data center permitting slower and costlier—but the underlying issue is older: no hyperscaler has yet demonstrated that $100+ billion annual capex on inference infrastructure generates acceptable returns at scale. Asian markets bore this reckoning first: Nikkei, Kospi, and Taiwan indices all hit fresh lows as foreign capital exited leveraged tech positions. South Korea's $1.7 trillion equity rout is now politically toxic for the Lee administration, which had weaponized stock market gains as a governance legitimacy metric.

    For an entrepreneur, this moment clarifies a durable lesson: venture capital does not reward "future disruption" narratives—it rewards demonstrable unit economics and path-to-cash. The AI founders and infrastructure teams that survive the next 18 months will be those who obsess over cost-per-inference, customer concentration risk, and working-capital cycles, not those betting on a perpetually expanding capex frontier. This applies equally to deeptech: FluoRok (€26.64M Series A for hazard-free fluorochemical synthesis) and VÆRIDION (€124.5M for electric aviation) both attract capital because they solve high-margin, defensible production problems—not because the technology is "transformational."
