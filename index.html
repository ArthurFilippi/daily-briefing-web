    🌍 Geopolitics & Policy

    Trump waives Russia sanctions as geopolitical consensus splinters

    Trump signed a national-interest waiver exempting Russia from congressionally-mandated sanctions tied to Ukraine aid, specifically targeting energy measures tied to diesel sales. Macron responded publicly, labeling the accord "inappropriate and inefficacy," while Italy's Defence Minister Guido Crosetto told the BBC that restricting US support prolongs—rather than shortens—the war. This marks the sharpest transatlantic rupture on Ukraine since 2022, with Europe signaling it will not follow US lead on Moscow dealmaking. Source: BBC, DW

    UN Secretary-General succession deadlocked; Grynspan leads non-binding vote

    All seven candidates for the UN Secretary-General role received vetoes from at least one permanent Security Council member (China, US, France, UK, or Russia). Costa Rican diplomat Rebeca Grynspan holds a narrow lead in an indicative ballot, but consensus remains absent. The stalemate reflects deeper fractures between Washington, Beijing, Moscow, and Europe—none willing to endorse rivals' preferences. Source: Le Monde

    Brazil records Amazon deforestation at 40-year low

    Brazil's National Institute reported 3,335 km² cleared in the 12 months ending July—the lowest since 1988. Incoming presidential elections loom as observers track whether the trend reversal reflects durable policy or cyclical commodity prices. This signals a potential reputational reset for Brazil's environmental governance ahead of COP deliberations. Source: DW, BBC

    Pakistan school roof collapse triggers nationwide safety inspections

    A roof collapse killed 32 children and one teacher in Peshawar, prompting emergency structural audits across northwest Pakistan. The incident exposes enforcement gaps in building codes and raises questions about infrastructure accountability in conflict-affected regions. Source: NYT

    🔍 Signal of the Day

    When Venture Capital Reprices: The Death of "Move Fast" in AI

    The convergence of Anthropic's double-digit debt yields, CoreWave's 25% weekly collapse, Netflix's layoff-via-success, and Replit's cost-driven exit from Anthropic tells a single story: capital markets are recalibrating AI assumptions downward. Six months ago, "scale at any cost" remained the Silicon Valley default. Today, investors demand proof of unit economics and clear paths to profitability before committing nine-figure cheques.

    This repricing is structural, not cyclical. The AI narrative bump (2023–2025) rested on two assumptions: (1) that large-model inference would yield immediate $100B+ revenue streams, and (2) that first-mover moat effects would sustain venture returns. Both assumptions are now contested. Inference margins compress as models commoditize. The moat story collapses once multiple viable open-weight and closed-source alternatives exist. Debt markets, which cannot be spun with narrative alone, are signaling this first.

    For you as a Polytechnique founder, this moment is clarifying. Your deeptech startup no longer competes primarily on capital velocity or narrative sophistication. You compete on unit economic clarity. Investors will ask: What is your cost per inference? Per training run? Can you demonstrate customer lock-in before spending your Series B? The founders who thrive in 2026–2028 will be those who treat capital as a scarce input to be optimized, not a renewable resource to be burned. French and European deeptech ecosystems, structurally smaller than Silicon Valley's, may actually benefit from this repricing—constrained capital forces discipline earlier, selecting for founders who can build sustainable unit economics from day one.
