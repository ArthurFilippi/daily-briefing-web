    🌍 Geopolitics & Policy

    Ukraine Under Sustained Missile Assault

    Russian ballistic missiles struck Kyiv overnight Friday, injuring at least three people, one a child. Ukrainian intelligence had warned of coordinated attacks on energy infrastructure, Dnipro bridges, and civilian targets. The tempo of strikes reflects Moscow's winter campaign strategy targeting Ukrainian supply chains rather than tactical gains. Source: Le Monde

    Washington Escalates Iran Nuclear Intelligence Bid

    The US military command for the Middle East is offering $20 million and potential relocation to informants with details on Iranian nuclear facilities, equipment, and personnel. This marks an intensification of efforts to obtain actionable intelligence on Tehran's weapons program beyond public monitoring. Source: Le Monde

    Houthis Systematize Red Sea Passage Control via Email

    Yemeni pro-Iranian fighters are now issuing passage permits by email to merchant vessels, operationalizing de facto choke-point authority in the Red Sea. Wall Street Journal obtained correspondence showing conditional clearance negotiations. This signals consolidation of irregular maritime governance independent of international law. Source: Le Monde

    Paraguay Executes Banking-Scandal Impeachment, Arrests President

    President Santiago Peña was removed by Senate vote and confined to house arrest after a bank collapse froze accounts for nearly 3 million depositors, including public pension and health funds. Vice President Pedro Alliana assumes office. The financial implosion exposed governance failure in Latin America's second-largest economy by GDP per capita. Source: Le Monde

    🔍 Signal of the Day

    Stephen Miller Promotion Signals Hard-Right Pivot in US Labor & Immigration Policy

    Donald Trump promoted Stephen Miller to Chief of Staff on Saturday, replacing Susie Wiles days after Republicans' midterm rout. Miller is a white nationalist ideologue with documented ties to conspiratorial movements and has long advocated mass deportation, visa restrictions, and labor market skepticism toward immigrant workers. His elevation to top administrative rank—over experienced operatives—indicates Trump intends to use his second term to reshape immigration enforcement and labor supply dynamics regardless of congressional opposition or business lobbying.

    Pattern significance: This reflects the broader Trump 2.0 doctrine of ideology-first personnel decisions. The replacement of Wiles (a competent pragmatist) with Miller (an uncompromising true believer) mirrors the broader power consolidation among MAGA hardliners after the GOP's failure to extend House majorities. For venture capital and tech labor markets, the move implies intensified scrutiny of H-1B visa processing, possible restrictions on remote work for non-US citizens, and enforcement actions against undocumented workers in Silicon Valley and biotech clusters. European deeptech founders and Indian engineering talent will face elevated friction in US hiring and fundraising.

    Founder implication: If you are scaling a tech/deeptech team, expect 2027 H-1B visa caps to tighten and wait times to lengthen. Consider doubling down on European talent acquisition pipelines and establishing subsidiary R&D in allied jurisdictions (Germany, Netherlands, Canada). For US-founded startups with non-citizen technical staff, legal audit of visa sponsorship exposure is now urgent.
