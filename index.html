    Israel's Kingmaker Moment: Arab Lawmakers Enter Coalition Math

    Neither Benjamin Netanyahu nor challenger Gadi Eisenkot secured a parliamentary majority in Israel's October 28 election, leaving Arab parties—historically sidelined from coalition talks—in unexpected negotiating power. The rare surge in Arab voter turnout has transformed the political calculus, offering both leaders a path to government but at the cost of engaging Arab lawmakers directly. This represents a structural shift in Israeli politics and complicates regional security decisions, particularly regarding Iran. Source: NYT

    Localized Ukraine Ceasefire Signals Diplomatic Momentum

    Melania Trump announced a rare localized peace accord in Kherson, granting civilian relief in a contested city on the Dnipr's western bank. President Zelensky framed the halt to Russian drone and artillery strikes as an "immense success" and called for trilateral talks (Moscow–Kyiv–Washington) in November. The move—brokered by the US administration—reflects a shift toward compartmentalized ceasefires rather than comprehensive settlements. Source: Le Monde

    🔍 Signal of the Day

    The AI Capex Trap: Spending Without Monetization Proof

    Google's revelation that its AI-powered "search mode" has reduced traditional search revenue—despite $51 billion in annual infrastructure spending—exemplifies a widening gap between technological capability and economic return. Across the sector, Samsung (sold-out chips but no buyback; stock declines), Tencent (Pony Ma called AI a "leaky ship"), and infrastructure players (Equinix betting on scarcity premium rather than efficiency gains) all show the same pattern: massive capital deployment is not yet translating to margin expansion or shareholder returns.

    This divergence reveals a structural reality: AI infrastructure has become a capital-intensive, defensive investment—necessary to remain competitive but insufficient alone to drive enterprise or consumer revenue growth. Startups and mature tech firms are discovering that compute availability does not automatically unlock new markets; it merely shifts the competition upstream (to chip design, data-center location, supply-chain control).

    For an early-stage founder, the implication is clear: the next wave of venture returns will accrue not to generalist AI companies (which face commoditization pressure and margin compression) but to vertical applications solving time-critical, high-stakes problems where AI reduces cost or risk measurably. French deeptech—rocket engines (MuRay), fusion (Proxima), healthcare logistics (Alan)—is correctly positioned in this window; European investors backing industrial AI and precision instrumentation will outpace those chasing consumer-grade generative chat.
