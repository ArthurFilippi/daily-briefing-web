Volatility compounds valuations for firms pricing power consumption (Apple's memory shortage forecast) and for those exposed to emerging-market currency instability (rupee strength, Philippine peso weakness).

For your portfolio, this signals: deeptech plays tied to compute infrastructure (memory, power, cooling) outpace software; jurisdictional fragmentation creates trapped value for firms that can't export models.

      🌍 Geopolitics & Policy

      US-Iran Ceasefire Stabilizes, But Leverage Remains Fragile

      The US and Iran announced agreement to halt attacks in the Strait of Hormuz and resume peace talks, ending tit-for-tat strikes that threatened a two-week-old truce. However, Iran has not yet confirmed, and analysts assess Tehran is deliberately withholding endorsement to maintain its chokepoint leverage over 20% of global oil transit. The ceasefire signals both sides see escalation as economically ruinous—particularly for the US, which also faces negotiation pressure from the Ukraine conflict where Putin says he awaits American negotiators "once Washington is less occupied with Iran." Source: Bloomberg, NY Times

      Russia Deploys "Project 2026" Disinformation Campaign Targeting LLMs and Search

      A leaked Kremlin program aims to infiltrate search engines and large language models to flood the internet with state narratives. The operation abandons earlier spray-and-pray disinformation for surgical embedding into AI systems themselves—a pivot that reveals Moscow's assessment that information warfare now centers on controlling the infrastructure through which populations access facts rather than competing for belief in the open. This escalation suggests Russian strategic documents now treat LLM providers as critical battlefield terrain. Source: Le Grand Continent

      China Blacklists 20 Japanese Firms; Escalates Asymmetric Economic Pressure

      Beijing placed twenty Japanese entities—including defense-sector suppliers and tech firms—on a sanctions list barring access to Chinese military-use goods, citing "remilitarization." The blacklist targets engineering and component suppliers to Japan's defense industrial base, reversing the logic of US export controls: rather than blocking sales to China, China now blocks sales from Japan. This signals a shift toward economic retaliation as a substitute for military escalation in the region. Source: Le Monde

      Pakistan Conducts Cross-Border Strikes; Afghanistan Taliban Condemns "Cowardly Aggression"

      Pakistan carried out intelligence-led airstrikes and ground operations along its Afghan border, killing at least 25-29 militants (figures vary by source) in response to a Karachi attack on paramilitaries on June 27. Afghanistan's Taliban condemned the strikes as violation of sovereignty, though specifics of coordination remain opaque. The incident illustrates how fragile ceasefire agreements are when non-state actors (Taliban factions) operate across borders and conventional deterrence breaks down. Source: Le Monde, DW

      🇫🇷 🇷 French/European Entrepreneurship

      European Deeptech Hits Annual Record Investment in Six Months

      European deeptech fundraising reached an annual record figure in just the first half of 2026, signaling accelerating capital concentration in hard tech (semiconductors, biotech, quantum, energy). No figures were disclosed in the source, but the milestone reflects investor flight from consumer software toward infrastructure and IP-defensible physics-based businesses—a structural reallocation favorable to founders with manufacturing or supply-chain moats. Source: Sifted

      AI's Memory Appetite Triggers Supply Shocks; Apple Forecasts Sustained Scarcity

      Apple and other AI infrastructure players are bracing for multi-year memory (DRAM/HBM) shortages as generative AI training and inference workloads consume packaging capacity globally. GPU demand has already strained advanced packaging; memory now faces a second-order supply squeeze. The dynamic echoes the 2021-23 chip shortage but with a critical difference: unlike pandemic-driven supply disruptions, this shortage is structural—driven by architectural changes in compute that shift demand faster than foundries can scale. Source: French Web

      💼 Business & Finance

      Shipping Costs Spike as Companies Race Tariff Deadlines

      Container freight rates reached their highest levels since the 2024 Red Sea crisis as importers accelerate cargo to beat anticipated new Trump tariffs. The rush echoes supply-chain behavior from 2021-22 but with a key difference: then, scarcity drove rates; now, policy uncertainty does. This signals a structural shift in how corporate capex decisions get made—no longer anchored to demand forecasts but to political calendars. Source: Financial Times

      Goldman Sachs Reports $200B M&A Boom in US Power Sector; AI Demand Drives Infrastructure Deals

      US power companies have entered a record $200 billion dealmaking blitz, buying generation and transmission assets to build the energy infrastructure for data centers. A single data center can demand 100+ megawatts; hyperscalers (Google, Meta, Microsoft, Amazon) are now direct buyers of power infrastructure rather than customers of utilities. This vertical integration reflects the inelasticity of power supply relative to AI capex growth—a structural advantage for firms that control generation. Source: Financial Times

      PBOC Sets Overnight Rate Below Forecasts, Signaling De Facto Easing

      China's central bank set its new overnight liquidity tool rate below market expectations, interpreted by economists as a soft rate cut aimed at reducing borrowing costs without triggering headlines. The move suggests PBOC awareness that a hawkish stance could deepen Chinese tech underperformance in global AI competition (a risk explicitly flagged in recent policy documents). De facto easing into economic headwinds typically supports risk assets, but duration and effectiveness remain uncertain. Source: Bloomberg

      Copper Declines as Fed Maintains Hawkish Stance; Strong Dollar Weighs on Metals

      Industrial metals declined as the Federal Reserve signaled no imminent rate cuts, sustaining a strong dollar that makes dollar-denominated commodities more expensive for foreign buyers. The dynamic creates a macro drag on EM recovery narratives (India's rupee strength notwithstanding) and suggests central bank divergence—PBOC easing while Fed holds—will remain a structural feature of H2 2026. Source: Bloomberg

      🚀 Tech / Deeptech / VC

      China's Zhipu AI (GLM-5.2) Matches Closed-Model Rivals in Cybersecurity Benchmarks

      Zhipu AI released GLM-5.2, an open-weight model that researchers say matches Anthropic's Mythos in bug-finding and code-security tasks despite lagging in general reasoning. The milestone signals narrowing capability gaps in specialized domains—a pattern that pressures the current "closed model moat" strategy of OpenAI and Anthropic. If open-weight models continue to close gaps in high-value domains (cybersecurity, bioinformatics), margin compression for proprietary APIs becomes a medium-term risk. Source: The Verge, Semgrep

      China Reclaims World's Fastest Supercomputer; US Export Controls Prove Porous

      China's LineShine supercomputer displaced the US El Capitan from the TOP500's #1 rank—the first time since 2018 that China leads. The achievement comes despite US export restrictions on advanced chips, underscoring that compute leadership no longer depends on proprietary silicon alone; architecture, software optimization, and domestic chip design (increasingly capable) now matter. Strategic implication: US semiconductor dominance is eroding faster in compute-intensive domains than headline narratives suggest. Source: The Verge, Chips and Cheese

      South Korea Announces ₩1.3 Trillion (10-Year) Memory and Robotics Investment

      Samsung and SK Hynix are expected to announce a decade-long commitment to memory chip, data center, and robotics spending—a strategic response to both China's supercomputer milestone and the structural memory shortage driven by AI. The scale and duration signal South Korean acceptance of a permanent shift in capex allocation toward AI-adjacent infrastructure. For investors in memory suppliers or industrial robotics, this anchors decade-long demand visibility. Source: Bloomberg

      Momenta (GM-Backed Autonomous Driving Firm) Seeks $752M in Hong Kong IPO

      Autonomous-driving startup Momenta Global filed for Hong Kong listing to raise HK$5.9 billion ($752M), capitalizing on a multiyear high in IPO activity in the city. The move reflects capital repatriation away from US listings amid regulatory scrutiny of Chinese tech firms and a relative reopening of Hong Kong capital markets. For early-stage deeptech founders, this signals non-US equity venues are recapitalizing. Source: Bloomberg

      German AI Adoption Cuts Administrative Labor by 50%; ₹300B Productivity Opportunity

      A German homebuilder reduced invoice processing from ~4 working days per week to ~2 using AI, exemplifying a ₹300 billion (~€300B equivalent) productivity opportunity across German manufacturing and services. The datum matters because it's concrete (not speculative) and early-stage adoption, suggesting AI's ROI in operational efficiency remains underpriced relative to hype cycles. For founders selling B2B automation tools, this validates market appetite and pricing power. Source: Bloomberg

      🔍 Signal of the Day

      China's Supercomputer Reclamation Signals Compute Decoupling Has Already Happened

      The headline—China reclaims #1 on TOP500—masks a deeper structural shift. LineShine's rise doesn't reflect a breakthrough in proprietary chip design or a breakthrough in US export-control evasion. Rather, it demonstrates that once a nation achieves a baseline of chip design competence (which China did in 2023-24), compute leadership depends more on algorithm optimization, software architecture, and manufacturing scale than on raw semiconductor IP. The US export control regime was designed to slow China's capability development; instead, it's accelerated China's independence from US supply chains—and thus China's willingness to invest in parallel ecosystems that, while temporarily inferior in peak performance, are resilient to future restrictions.

      This pattern replicates across deeptech: memory shortages (driven by AI demand) are pushing South Korea and Japan to announce decade-long capex commitments independent of US market signals. Russia's "Project 2026" diverts resources into LLM poisoning because it can no longer compete on model quality—but doesn't need to, if it can own the input layer (language models trained on its data, filtered for its narratives).

      For an entrepreneur building deeptech: the era of single-source-of-truth compute or supply is over. The next decade rewards founders who can operate across jurisdictions, build resilient supply chains that survive sanctions, and own layers (not just endpoints) of the stack. If you're building AI applications, assume you'll eventually need two implementations—one for markets where US chips are available, one for markets where they aren't. If you're building chips or power infrastructure, geography and jurisdictional moats matter more than raw performance benchmarks.
