      🔍 Signal of the Day

      Why Compute Scarcity Matters More Than AI Company Valuations

      The paradox exploded today: Cohere's $25B valuation sits atop a confession that GPUs are the real constraint, not capital or talent. Meanwhile, CoreWeave—a "boring" infrastructure company—is raising forecast on hardware rental while frontier labs scramble to build. OpenAI's $10B quarterly revenue is impressive; Anthropic's $1.5T IPO valuation is newsworthy. But neither solves the underlying physical constraint: there are not enough high-end chips to go around.

      This reveals a structural shift in AI economics. For the first time, the scarcity is not innovation (LLMs work), not talent (pools exist), not money (trillions available)—it is electricity and semiconductor manufacturing. The implications are profound. A 21-year-old entrepreneur considering where to build will find that investing in frontier AI model research is oversaturated; the real defensible moats are in power infrastructure, chip design, and data center operations. Mistral's copyright fight, Cohere's GPU shortage, and SoftBank's data center writedown are all expressions of the same problem: the economics of training and serving LLMs at scale require re-architecting energy supply and silicon allocation, not just software.

      For your portfolio, this trend supports deeptech energy plays (fusion, next-gen power), semiconductor manufacturing (not fabless design), and infrastructure-as-a-service in AI—sectors where scarcity creates margin durability. Avoid unprofitable AI SaaS that relies on spot market compute access.
