      Macro trends:

      1. Ceasefire fatigue is reshaping defense spending architecture. Frozen front lines in Ukraine have pivoted military economies toward maritime attrition (port strikes, grain blockades, tanker attacks in the Arabian Sea) rather than territorial conquest. Simultaneously, drone and AI-enabled defense tech fundraising has shifted leverage to founders—they now dictate term sheets, not VCs. This bifurcation (kinetic stalemate + tech-driven transformation) is creating asymmetric investment flows away from legacy defense contractors toward deeptech. For NASDAQ/SP500-tilted portfolios: expect outperformance in applied AI infrastructure (compute, chips) and defense tech, underperformance in traditional heavy manufacturing.

      2. AI valuation stratification is collapsing cost of capital for European deeptech. Anthropic's $2tn IPO speculation, Lovable's $13.3bn doubling, Legora's $10bn+ private valuation, and Multiverse Computing's emergence signal that AI-adjacent startups have decoupled from traditional fundraising mechanics. European funds (Accel's $800m Series IX, EU's €5bn Scaleup Fund) are now competing on volume not stage-gating. This floods capital into quantum error correction (QuBriC), biotech (Again/Geno merger), and infrastructure plays. Result: barrier to Series C/D for European founders has materially lowered; runway extension now crowns winners, not innovation.

      🌍 Geopolitics & Policy

      Russian and Ukrainian aerial campaigns intensify civilian toll amid frozen front lines

      Sustained air strikes on both sides—despite territorial stalemate—are driving cumulative civilian casualties upward. This reflects a shift from conventional warfare toward attrition of civilian infrastructure and morale. The pattern mirrors broader maritime warfare: Russia's blockade of Ukrainian Black Sea ports and strikes on grain terminals directly threaten global food security, while cargo ship attacks near the Arabian Sea (Russian tanker spilling crude off Oman) compound regional instability. Source: Le Grand Continent, NYT

      Zelensky signals critical Patriot missile shortage to CNN

      Ukraine's president disclosed that Kyiv currently receives only 1% of US Patriot inventory needed for ballistic defense, and requires at least 5% to survive winter and prevent further civilian casualties. This quantification—moving from rhetorical appeals to specific asset requests—indicates NATO logistics planning is entering a new phase. Source: Le Monde

      Trump administration cancels benchmark Arctic climate report

      The White House withdrew the annual Arctic Report Card—published since 2006—marking another institutional breach in US climate science. The move signals that climate-skeptic policy will extend to data suppression, not just regulatory rollback. Source: Le Monde

      AI adoption in oil extraction could unlock billions of barrels, worsening emissions trajectory

      A Nature-published study warns that AI-driven optimization will extract profitable reserves from depleted fields and unlock previously uneconomical sites, potentially adding billions of barrels to global production. This paradox—AI enabling efficiency while increasing absolute extraction—creates a structural headwind for net-zero targets. Source: Le Monde

      🔍 Signal of the Day

      AI founders are now dictating term sheets; the power inversion reflects structural scarcity of execution talent.

      Three signals converged today: (1) Lovable's $400M Series C at 2.4x prior valuation in 4 months; (2) Legora's $10B+ ask after being valued at €4.8B in May; (3) Anthropic's $6B bid for Decart, and $2T IPO whispers. These moves share a common mechanic: VCs are bidding against each other for proven founders, not the reverse. Series C/D terms now favor founders—lower dilution, founder-friendly liquidation preferences, and board seats guaranteed.

      This inversion stems from two scarcities. First, proven execution at scale in AI is rarer than capital. Every VC has dry powder; few founders have shipped a product that reaches $100M+ ARR in 18 months. Second, regulatory/policy risk has created founder optionality. Anthropic can negotiate raw economics because it owns the Claude moat; smaller competitors cannot. European founders (Lovable, Legora, Multiverse) benefit because EU capital (€5B Scaleup Fund, Accel's $800M) is locally constrained and must deploy locally.

      For an aspiring entrepreneur: this is the moment to raise at premium multiples if you have a measurable moat (proprietary data, proven unit economics, defensible IP). Conversely, if your startup is pre-product or in a crowded vertical (e.g., generic AI SaaS, undifferentiated fintech), term sheets will compress. The bifurcation is widening: winners raise at 2-3x multiples, losers raise at 0.5-0.8x or not at all. Survival favors founders who focus on scarcity—not talent, not capital, but the specific technical problem no one else can solve faster.
