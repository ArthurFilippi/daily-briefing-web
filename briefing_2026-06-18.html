      🌍 Geopolitics & Policy

      US-Iran War Ends; Hormuz Reopens With Caveats

      Trump and Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian signed a 14-point Memorandum of Understanding at a post-G7 dinner at the Palace of Versailles on Wednesday evening, formally ending hostilities and triggering 60 days of nuclear negotiations. The agreement includes a $300 billion reconstruction package for Iran and commitments to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, a critical conduit for ~21% of global seaborne oil trade. However, Goldman Sachs projects oil throughput will recover to only ~70% of pre-war levels, as regional producers lean on alternate routes and supply-chain diversification persists. Trump defended the deal against critics citing Obama's 2015 nuclear accord, insisting he secured stronger concessions; detailed text remains interim. Source: NYT, BBC, Le Monde

      Macron Pursues Ukraine Leverage With Trump at Versailles Dinner

      Following the G7 summit in Évian, French President Macron hosted Trump at an elaborate state dinner to deepen bilateral alignment on Ukraine strategy. The gambit signals European determination to keep the US engaged in continental security even as Iran deal dominates headlines. Diplomatic choreography suggests Franco-American coordination on Eastern European deterrence may yield marginal concessions from Trump on NATO burden-sharing. Source: Le Monde

      Ukraine Reports $918 Cost Per Eliminated Russian Soldier Using Drones

      Colonel Ihor Romanenko, commander of Ukraine's unmanned systems forces, disclosed that eliminating a Russian soldier via drone strike averages approximately $918 in hardware and logistics costs. The metric underscores the economic inversion of modern proxy warfare: low-cost autonomous swarms neutralize high-cost conscription. This cost calculus has become a strategic lever in European defense policy design. Source: Le Grand Continent

      Southeast Asia Pivots to Solar Amid Persistent Energy Crisis

      Homeowners and businesses across Southeast Asia—a region heavily dependent on imported oil—are accelerating rooftop solar panel installations to cushion against soaring energy costs driven by the Iran-US conflict. The shift reflects rational hedging against commodity-linked inflation even as geopolitical settlement begins. Regional governments face pressure to decouple energy security from crude supply chains. Source: NYT

      🇫🇷 🇷 French/European Entrepreneurship

      France-Ukraine Launch €20M Defensetech Fund (BRAVE FRANCE)

      Ukraine's state defense innovation cluster (BRAVE1) and the French Defence Innovation Agency (AID) jointly established BRAVE FRANCE, a €20 million initiative to accelerate battlefield-proven technology commercialization and dual-use innovation. The fund represents a structural shift: wartime constraints are being weaponized as entrepreneurial advantage, positioning European defensetech startups to capture both state procurement and NATO allied demand. Source: tech.eu

      Conduct (Palantir Ex-Team) Raises €51M Series A for Enterprise AI OS

      London-based Conduct, founded by three former Palantir analysts, closed a €51 million ($60 million) Series A co-led by Index Ventures, Iconiq, and SAP Ventures. The platform modernizes ERP systems by making legacy enterprise software AI-ready, enabling automated workflow interpretation and system optimization. Demand surge reflects CIO appetite for GenAI-native data integration without full platform replacement. Conduct targets Salesforce, Oracle, SAP, and manufacturing execution systems. Source: eu-startups.com, tech.eu

      Microamp (Warsaw) Raises €6.5M for European 5G/6G Infrastructure

      Polish 5G/6G innovator Microamp secured €6.5 million to accelerate development of its Any-G mmWave AI-RAN platform—a software-defined, reconfigurable wireless system enabling seamless cross-generation adaptation. The funding underscores European institutional commitment to telecom sovereignty and supply-chain decoupling from US-China duopoly. Source: eu-startups.com

      Green-Got Raises €8M in 52 Minutes for Sustainable Banking

      French fintech Green-Got closed an €8 million seed round in under an hour via crowdfunding, signaling retail appetite for climate-aligned financial infrastructure. The sprint-closing suggests European retail investors are pricing ESG-linked services at premium valuations. Source: Maddyness

      French Defensetech Comand AI Raises €32M From Blossom Capital, Saab

      Comand AI, a Paris-based battlefield command software platform, closed €32 million in Series A funding led by Blossom Capital and Swedish defense major Saab. The round validates thesis that real-time battle-space coordination software commands venture + strategic industrial backing. Source: Sifted

      💼 Business & Finance

      Goldman Sachs Warns on Hormuz Recovery; Maersk Treads Cautiously

      Goldman projects Strait of Hormuz throughput recovering to only 70% of pre-war capacity despite the Iran-US ceasefire. Maersk CEO cautioned that allowing Iran to levy transit fees would set a "dangerous precedent" for maritime governance. Supply-chain managers are pre-positioning alternate routing to hedge against renewed disruption, implying persistent structural cost inflation in container shipping. Source: Bloomberg, NYT

      NSE (India) IPO Unlocks $53B Valuation; Could Be Subcontinent's Largest Listing

      India's National Stock Exchange filed for IPO, valued at ~$53 billion in unlisted markets. Early institutional backers (Morgan Stanley, Temasek, State Bank of India) could see 6,400x returns if listing clears at rumored valuations. NSE listing would eclipse all prior Indian IPOs and signal macro confidence in equity capital formation. Source: Bloomberg

      Czech Central Bank Mulls First Rate Hike Since 2022

      Czech policymakers signal first interest rate increase in four years as domestic inflation pressures outweigh receding energy crisis tailwinds. Decision reflects divergence in European monetary policy: energy-intensive economies stabilizing faster than anticipated. Source: Bloomberg

      Rio Tinto Resumes Copper Exports From Oyu Tolgoi (Mongolia)

      Rio Tinto recommenced copper concentrate shipments from its vast Oyu Tolgoi mine following a brief labor protest. The restart signals resilience in Mongolia's mining licensing environment and continuity in global copper supply chains. Source: Bloomberg

DeepL and Mistral gain relative advantage in EU deployment. Source: FT

      BWX Technologies Licenses Small Modular Reactor Design After Activist Push

      Nuclear technology firm BWX agreed to license its small modular reactor (SMR) design following pressure from activist investor Ananym Capital Management. The licensing deal unlocks commercialization pathway for advanced nuclear and addresses US grid capacity constraints driven by AI compute demand. Source: Bloomberg

      🔍 Signal of the Day

      Why Defensetech Funding Outpaces Consumer AI Venture Capital in 2026

      The €20M BRAVE FRANCE fund launch—alongside simultaneous Series A closes for Conduct (€51M), Comand AI (€32M), and PDKINEMATICS (€2M drone guidance)—reveals a structural inversion in European venture appetite. Defense-adjacent technology now absorbs institutional capital faster than pure consumer AI. Three drivers converge:

      First, geopolitical urgency collapses risk-return asymmetries. The $918-per-kill drone metric transforms hardware economics; Ukrainian battle data becomes product validation; procurement guarantees (NATO, EU defense budgets) replace VC-dependent demand forecasting. A defensetech startup at Series A has higher probability of $500M ARR than a B2B SaaS platform chasing ad-tech optimization.

      Second, geopolitical fragmentation is fragmenting inference. Anthropic's ban in Hong Kong; Mistral positioning open-source as "outside state control"; Microsoft selling OpenAI models into China despite policy pressure—AI architecture itself is becoming politicized. European founders building sovereign, exportable alternatives to US/China duopoly gain both ESG premium and policy tailwinds (EU AI Act exemptions for critical infrastructure, defense).

      Third, macroeconomic scarcity revalues tangible assets. When energy costs remain structurally elevated (70% Hormuz recovery = persistent oil risk premium), capital gravitates toward defensetech, nuclear, grid resilience, and industrial automation—domains delivering measurable unit economics. Consumer AI startups chasing engagement metrics compete for finite risk capital; infrastructure plays attract strategic + institutional LPs.

      For a 21-year-old with deeptech ambitions: the next 18–24 months will favor founding teams with (a) engineering depth + geopolitical context, (b) access to government procurement feedback loops, (c) ability to export to NATO allies. VC-pure narratives (scale-at-all-costs) are losing to moat-first, customer-concentration models.
