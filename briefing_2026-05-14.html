🌍 Geopolitics & Policy

Trump and Xi Meet Amid Trade Stalemate and Taiwan Tensions

President Trump and Xi Jinping opened two days of talks in Beijing on Thursday, with both leaders emphasizing partnership over rivalry. Xi urged the US to treat China as a "partner, not rival," while Trump struck an optimistic tone on collaboration. However, multiple unresolved issues loom: trade tariffs (especially on rare earths and energy), Taiwan's status, and ongoing Middle East instability tied to the Iran war. The summit represents a test of whether the "uneasy truce" on trade can hold or if tensions will escalate. Source: Le Monde, Bloomberg, New York Times

Israel-Lebanon Third Round of Ceasefire Talks Begins in Washington

Israeli and Lebanese delegations commenced a two-day negotiation on Thursday aimed at ending the Hezbollah-Israel conflict, marking the third formal round of talks in Washington. Both sides face significant domestic pressure: Israel has little incentive to compromise, while Lebanon cannot compel Hezbollah disarmament. Killing and escalation continue on the ground even as diplomats meet, signaling deep structural obstacles to any durable settlement. Source: Le Monde

US Judge Blocks Sanctions on UN Investigator Francesca Albanese

A US federal judge suspended sanctions imposed by the Trump administration against Francesca Albanese, a UN investigator critical of US Gaza policy. The ruling signals pushback against executive overreach on speech and retaliation against international human rights advocacy. Source: Le Monde

Russia Launches Fresh Attacks on Kyiv; At Least One Killed

Russian forces conducted a large overnight drone and ballistic missile assault on Kyiv and its region, killing at least one person and wounding 16. Mayor Vitali Klitschko urged residents to shelter in place. The attack underscores ongoing attrition on the Ukrainian front despite diplomatic activity elsewhere. Source: Le Monde

🇫🇷 🇷 French/European Entrepreneurship

Mantle8 Raises €31M Series A for Natural Hydrogen Exploration

Grenoble-based Mantle8 closed €31 million in Series A funding (total capitalization: €37M / $44M) to fund advanced natural hydrogen exploration and drilling campaigns. Lead investor Sandwater, alongside Breakthrough Energy Ventures and the Ecotechnologies 2 fund, backs the company's bet on geological hydrogen as a new European energy sovereignty frontier. This move reflects growing recognition of hydrogen's role in decarbonization and industrial resilience. Source: EU-Startups, FrenchWeb

Fractile Raises $220M Series B for AI Inference Hardware

UK-based Fractile secured $220 million in Series B funding (led by Accel, Factorial Funds, and Peter Thiel's Founders Fund) to build next-generation inference chips addressing AI compute bottlenecks. The raise signals investor confidence in specialized hardware plays rather than relying solely on Nvidia's generalist approach. Source: Tech.eu, Sifted

Barcelona's Dolfin Raises €2.1M Seed for AI Sales Compensation Platform

Dolfin, an AI-native platform for sales compensation management, closed a €2.1 million ($2.5M) Seed round led by Swanlaab. The startup is accelerating product development and expansion into the US market, targeting the under-digitized enterprise sales operations space. Source: EU-Startups

Italy's Gyver Raises €1.4M Pre-Seed to Upskill Industrial Workers

Brescia-based Gyver closed €1.4 million in pre-seed funding (led by Brighteye) to build workforce infrastructure for Europe's industrial and energy sectors. The startup equips electricians and other blue-collar workers with AI tools, positioning itself as a solution to labor shortages and automation anxiety in skilled trades. Source: EU-Startups, Tech.eu

💼 Business & Finance

Trump-Xi Beijing Summit Opens with Trade Truce Fragile But Holding

Trump and Xi convened at the Great Hall of the People with ceremonial pomp, striking an optimistic public tone on shared interests. Behind the scenes, both sides have significantly lowered expectations: the Iran war, Taiwan tensions, rare earths tariffs, and liquified natural gas pricing remain live flashpoints. Energy markets are watching closely for any breakthrough on US oil/gas tariffs, given China's recent restrictions on American imports. Source: Bloomberg, Financial Times

Japan's Eneos Acquires Chevron's Asia-Pacific Assets for $2.17B

Japanese refiner Eneos agreed to purchase Chevron's refining and retail operations across Asia-Pacific for $2.17 billion, significantly expanding its regional footprint. The deal reflects ongoing energy market consolidation and Japan's strategic pivot to secure downstream assets amid geopolitical volatility. Source: Bloomberg

ECB June Rate Hike Becomes Less Certain as Inflation Softens

Two weeks after Christine Lagarde signaled an imminent rate increase, ECB officials are backing away from a June hike as inflation data ease. Geopolitical uncertainty (Iran war, energy volatility) and softer eurozone growth estimates are driving a more cautious stance. This represents a sharp pivot from hawkish messaging and signals growing anxiety about recession risk. Source: Bloomberg

Copper Retreats from Record High as Chinese Demand Slows

Copper fell from record highs as purchase momentum in China eased, with investors cautious ahead of the Trump-Xi summit. The retreat signals wariness about trade escalation and demand destruction if tariffs spike. Source: Bloomberg

🔍 Signal of the Day

AI Infrastructure Bubble vs. Real Productivity Gains: A Polaroid Moment

The facts are stark: Blackstone raised $1.75B for a data center REIT, AirTrunk is borrowing $2.3B for Malaysia expansion, Cisco is cutting 5% of staff while beating guidance, and Fanuc surges on a Google robotics deal. Simultaneously, the market concentration—with "Magnificent Seven" proxies dominating returns—is worse than during the Nifty Fifty era. Capital is flooding into AI infrastructure at a pace unseen since the 2000 tech peak.

The broader pattern: we are witnessing a bifurcation between hardware/infrastructure (data centers, chips, power infrastructure) and software consumption (apps, SaaS, enterprise tools). The physical AI wave—Fanuc-Google, robotics, embodied AI—is real and defensible. But pure software plays and inference-heavy generative AI services remain unproven at scale on unit economics. We're in a "prove-it" phase where capital is abundant but confidence is fragile.

Implication for a future entrepreneur: the window to build infrastructure—power, cooling, specialized chips, energy logistics—is opening now, but it will close within 24 months as capital gets disciplined. Conversely, consumer/SMB AI tooling is commoditizing fast. If you're at Polytechnique with deeptech ambitions, the bet to place is not "another Claude competitor," but rather: How do I own a chokepoint in the physical AI supply chain? Natural hydrogen (Mantle8), inference chips (Fractile), industrial AI (Gyver)—these are the defensible positions. The Polaroid moment is now: film studios thought they'd be safe forever; Polaroid won anyway by owning instant. Own the infrastructure, not the content.
