    Bab el-Mandeb Blockade Tightens European Energy Risk

    Houthi control of this chokepoint threatens approximately one-third of diesel imported by the EU from Gulf sources as of 2025. This escalation surfaces a historically discrete defense partnership between France and Riyadh, now openly justified on critical infrastructure grounds. The dual vulnerability (Yemen upstream, Iran at Strait of Hormuz) compounds refinement margins across European markets. Source: Le Grand Continent, Le Monde

    Iran Proposes Seven-Day Strait Reopening; US Engaged

    Iran's foreign ministry submitted a blueprint to unblock the Strait of Hormuz, contingent on US acceptance, with a seven-day countdown to implementation. Control of this passage remains central to Tehran-Washington leverage, with Iran wielding rare earths and specialty chemicals against US tariff, export-control, and technology tools. Resolution would alleviate global crude volatility but remains hostage to broader sanctions architecture. Source: BBC, Le Monde

    🔍 Signal of the Day

    Anthropic's Pentagon Exclusion Becomes Judicial Fact; AI Defense Market Fractures

    The Washington Court of Appeals upheld the Pentagon's decision to bar Anthropic from military AI contracts after the startup refused (February 2026) to grant unrestricted military use of Claude. This is no longer administrative retaliation—it is now settled law. The implication: AI safety constraints are incompatible with US defense procurement in its current form.

    This fracture reveals a deeper market split. OpenAI, despite the uncontrolled agent breaches disclosed this week, remains embedded in US national-security infrastructure (Census, SEC access occurred; Pentagon engagement continues de facto). Anthropic, which took a principled stand on model guardrails, is now locked out of the single largest institutional buyer in the sector. For European and French deeptech founders, this is critical: it means the US government will consolidate AI capability around vendors willing to disable safety filters, while European regulatory frameworks (AI Act) move in the opposite direction—toward mandatory auditability and constraint.

    By 2027, this divergence will create two non-interoperable AI ecosystems: a US defense-first, constraint-minimal model; and a Europe-led, audit-heavy, defensibility-first model. French startups aligned with European standards will be shut out of US military procurement but could capture EU institutional demand (ARPA-E equivalent, sovereign defense, critical infrastructure). Plan accordingly.
