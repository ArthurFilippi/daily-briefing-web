🌍 Geopolitics & Policy

Russia's Oil Paradox: Volume Gains Mask Revenue Collapse

Following the US–Iran peace agreement, the price of Urals crude has plummeted 62%, drastically eroding Moscow's hydrocarbon revenues despite successful efforts to increase export volumes. This dynamic reflects the broader oil market shift: Strait of Hormuz transit recovery and Iranian re-entry into global supply chains are outpacing OPEC+ production discipline. Goldman Sachs now forecasts a swing into global oversupply as the Iran war's scarcity premium fully dissipates. Russia faces a structural revenue challenge that cannot be solved by volume alone, pressuring its defense budget and capital flows into deeptech.
Source: Le Grand Continent

US Supreme Court Bars Trump's Birthright Citizenship Rule; Expands Presidential Power Elsewhere

The Supreme Court ruled against Trump's attempt to revoke birthright citizenship for children of undocumented immigrants—his second major judicial defeat this term alongside February's reciprocal tariff ban—yet simultaneously expanded executive authority in other domains. This mixed verdict constrains anti-immigration action but leaves broad presidential scope intact, signaling court fragmentation rather than unified opposition to the administration.
Source: Le Monde

Anthropic Clears US Export Ban; Fable 5 & Mythos 5 Restored

The US government lifted foreign-access restrictions on Anthropic's two most advanced models after the startup resolved Trump administration safety concerns. The models will resume availability across Claude platforms, AWS, and Google Cloud starting July 1st. This move signals a deliberate policy recalibration: advanced AI export controls remain selective rather than blanket, targeting specific architectures rather than broad capability tiers.
Source: BBC

Iran–US Peace Talks Resume in Qatar; Mediation Track Uncertain

American and Iranian officials are meeting with Qatar mediators, but no direct high-level talks or bilateral negotiations are officially scheduled. Despite recent attacks threatening the accords, diplomatic channels remain open, suggesting de-escalation is incremental and fragile rather than conclusive.

🔍 Signal of the Day

Oil Oversupply and Peace Accords Reshape Geopolitical Risk Premia

The fact: Goldman Sachs now forecasts a global oil surplus by late 2026 as Iran's re-entry into crude markets and Strait of Hormuz traffic recovery overwhelm OPEC+ production discipline. Simultaneously, Russian crude revenues have collapsed 62% despite higher export volumes, and US policy has pivoted from AI restrictions toward selective, negotiated controls. These three moves—commodity deflation, revenue collapse for a major revisionist power, and de-escalatory regulatory recalibration—are tightly coupled.

Broader pattern: The post-2022 geopolitical risk premium that spiked energy prices and justified defense spending reallocation is unwinding faster than consensus assumed. Markets are repricing the probability that US–China tensions and regional conflicts will remain "managed" rather than exploding into full supply disruption. This makes energy an unfavorable hedge for portfolios, favors developed-market asset valuations (where inflation was the core shock), and creates capital redeployment pressure toward AI and semiconductor infrastructure—exactly where Trump's policy is now permitting exports.

Implication for entrepreneurs: Founders building in energy efficiency, advanced nuclear, and critical-infrastructure defense will face a narrative shift. For 2022–2025, scarcity and energy security underpinned deeptech funding; now, the policy conversation is shifting back to cost reduction and operational leverage. European founders should expect accelerated consolidation in renewable-adjacent plays and a repricing of "energy independence" narratives. The window for commanding premium valuations on 10-year infrastructure bets (unless backed by anchored government offtake) is closing.
