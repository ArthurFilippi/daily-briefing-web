🔍 Signal of the Day

The Rare-Earth Pivot: Beijing Weaponizes Supply Chain While Trump Talks Trade

China controls 60%+ of global rare-earth extraction and processing, critical for advanced semiconductors, defense systems, and clean energy. As Trump arrives in Beijing, the central question is whether China extends a temporary freeze on tighter export controls or escalates restrictions. This is not theoretical leverage—rare-earth constraints already limit Taiwan semiconductor capacity and US defense production.

Broadly, we are watching the collision of three dynamics: Trump's need for a trade war "win" (requiring Chinese concessions on tariffs or IP), China's geopolitical confidence (buffered against Hormuz crisis energy shocks, positioned to absorb sanctions), and the reality that neither side can afford escalation while the Iran war destabilizes global supply chains.

For an entrepreneur building deeptech in France or Europe, this signals two things: (1) supply chain resilience is now a venture capital due diligence question—founders must map semiconductor, rare-earth, and critical material dependencies, particularly if targeting US defense/climate customers; (2) European strategic autonomy in semiconductors and materials processing is becoming a state priority (CHIPS Act equivalents, critical minerals strategies), opening public-private partnership opportunities for startups willing to delocalize manufacturing or build redundancy. The next 18 months will see capital flowing into European hard tech founders solving supply-chain fragility.
