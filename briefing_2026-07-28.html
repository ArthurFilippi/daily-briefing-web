🔍 Signal of the Day

Semiconductor Capex Detaching from Utilization Reality

The $570 billion selloff in SK Hynix within 6 weeks—from hottest AI trade to portfolio red flag—reveals a structural break in venture logic. Investor enthusiasm for AI chipmaking pivoted on expected compute shortage; reality is showing abundant capacity deployment (Nvidia's $50bn lease locks in oversupply hedges; Chinese competitors tightening performance gaps). The market is repricing: capex must now justify actual customer ROI, not just supply-constraint scarcity.

Broader pattern: Deeptech founders have long conflated "hard to build" with "hard to compete against." Memory chips, like many infrastructure layers, suffer from commoditization risk once scale is proven. The winners aren't the 5nm pioneers; they're the efficiency players (Multiverse's sovereign AI, Beelzebub's defense-grade cyber, Nuclear Turbines' cost reduction). This explains why European deeptech now clusters around defensible efficiency gains rather than raw capability.

Implication: If you're evaluating a deeptech fund or direct investment, scrutinize the margin-expansion thesis, not the technology-leadership narrative. A startup that reduces compute cost per inference by 40% has a 10-year moat; one that achieves 5% speed gains faces commoditization within 18 months. The AI capex boom is real, but it's now selecting for where capital flows—and that's efficiency, not hype.
