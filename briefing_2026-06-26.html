🔍 Signal of the Day

The "IAflation" Problem: When AI Productivity Gains Become Consumer Price Hikes

Microsoft and Apple have begun raising prices sharply to fund AI infrastructure and licensing costs, contradicting the productivity-driven deflation thesis that has underpinned AI valuations for 18 months. The term "IAflation"—intelligence-artificial-induced inflation—captures a paradox: the technology that was supposed to lower marginal costs and unlock deflationary gains is instead creating pricing pressure on end consumers and squeezing margins across the value chain.

This is not merely a pricing anomaly. It reflects structural mismatches between AI's current real-world deployment costs (datacenter capex, training compute, licensing fees to foundation model providers) and its actual productivity gains in consumer-facing applications. When consumers see higher prices for devices and services before perceiving equivalent capability improvements, public trust erodes—precisely when only 16% of Americans see AI positively.

For entrepreneurs building in this environment, the lesson is surgical: focus on vertical-specific efficiency (manufacturing, healthcare, logistics) where AI productivity is already measurable and can justify pricing, rather than broad-platform consumer plays where the value narrative is still being written. The gap between AI hype and consumer tangibility is where regulatory intervention and popular backlash are sharpening. Startups that can quantify ROI—like Almetra in industrial data, or Leyden Labs in pandemic prevention—outpace those betting on consumer adoption curves that may never materialize at the scale venture funding assumes.
