🌍 Geopolitics & Policy

Iran Consolidates Strait of Hormuz Control; US Pauses Military Response

Iran is implementing a durable apparatus to maintain control over the Strait of Ormuz, deploying mine-laying and traffic monitoring systems even as uncertainty about their presence deters shipping. The US demands unconditional passage, but Tehran's strategy relies on ambiguity and infrastructure denial rather than overt blockade. Trump announced he has paused a planned strike scheduled for Tuesday after UAE, Saudi Arabia, and Qatar requested a 2-3 day suspension "for serious negotiations." Drone minesweepers are being considered as a potential response mechanism. Source: Le Monde | Source: DW

Putin to Negotiate Power of Siberia 2 Pipeline at Beijing Summit

Russian President Vladimir Putin is traveling to China to discuss a major gas pipeline project with Xi Jinping, a meeting taking place days after Trump's own China visit. The pipeline deal signals Russia's need for deeper energy partnerships to offset Western sanctions, though the Moscow-Beijing relationship remains "profoundly imbalanced," with China holding structural economic leverage. Earlier, Xi indicated to Trump that Putin might "regret" the Ukraine invasion. Source: Financial Times

🔍 Signal of the Day

"The Supply Chain Chokehold: How Agentic AI is Reshaping Corporate Structure"

The fact: In a single week, Meta reassigned 7,000 workers into AI roles, Dust raised €40M to coordinate multi-agent workflows for enterprises, Google partnered with Blackstone to vertically integrate AI cloud infrastructure, and Australia's largest pension fund warned agentic AI will disrupt its operational model. These are not isolated announcements—they reveal synchronized recognition that enterprise work organization must restructure around AI agent orchestration, not human supervision.

Broader pattern: The gap between LLM capability and enterprise deployment has always been organizational, not technical. For two years, companies deployed single-model chatbots (copilots) while keeping existing workflows intact. Now, the constraint has shifted: companies realize that to capture AI's productivity dividend, they must redesign process architecture to let agents operate autonomously across sequences of tasks. This is why Dust—which explicitly brands itself around "multiplayer" agents rather than single assistants—is raising at Series B scale with top-tier VC backing. It is why Meta is moving 7,000 people out of traditional product roles. It is why pension funds are already bracing for disruption.

Implication for future entrepreneurs: The next 18 months favor founders who obsess over workflow coordination, agent reliability in constrained domains, and the organizational change management required to transition from human-centric processes to autonomous multi-agent execution. Technical AI chops matter less than deep domain knowledge of how insurance underwriting, supply chain planning, or financial compliance actually runs—because the real moat is embedding agents into customer workflows too complex for competitors to dislodge. The capital is flowing to those who understand that agentic AI is fundamentally a supply chain and organizational redesign play, not a new UI on top of existing processes.
