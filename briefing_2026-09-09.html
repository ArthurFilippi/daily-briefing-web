    🌍 Geopolitics & Policy

    China's Military Humanoid Push

    China's People's Liberation Army has accelerated development of armed humanoid robots since 2024, transitioning from laboratory prototypes toward field-ready combat deployment. The scope signals Beijing views robotic soldiers as force-multipliers for contested terrain operations. This contrasts sharply with Western regulatory caution and reshapes military procurement calculus across Indo-Pacific alliance structures. Source: Le Grand Continent

    Trump-Canada Trade War Escalates to Import Bans

    US President Trump imposed 50% tariffs and outright bans on Canadian dairy, alcoholic beverages, and motorcycles following Ottawa's €20 billion retaliatory tariffs on US goods. Prime Minister Mark Carney framed the response as defending against American "dependency" rather than "economic partnership." The escalation has moved beyond agricultural products into manufactured goods, signaling structural decoupling rather than negotiating theater. Source: DW, BBC

    US-Iran Escalation: Tanker Strikes, Missile Retaliation

    The US struck Iranian oil tankers near the Kharg Island export hub; Iran responded by launching missiles at Jordan, escalating Middle East military tensions. Iranian military leadership warned further US actions against tankers would trigger strikes on American regional bases. Brent crude pushed toward $100/barrel in response, with analysts noting Tehran is increasingly aggressive as its Strait of Hormuz control weakens amid economic pressure. Source: NYT, Le Monde

    Hungary Expels 10 Russian Diplomats

    Hungary's new government moved 10 Russian diplomats, signaling a shift toward alignment with NATO allies on Moscow relations—a departure from Viktor Orbán's previous proximity to the Kremlin. The action reflects Budapest's recalibration under fresh leadership, affecting Central European geopolitical positioning. Source: NYT

    The Exploration Company Lands €387M Series C

    Europe's leading reusable space company closed a €387 million ($450 million) Series C to advance its Nyx orbital capsule toward ISS docking and accelerate Storm high-thrust rocket engine development. Co-led by major institutional backers, this marks the largest Series C by a European space firm and positions TEC as a direct SpaceX challenger in the commercial space infrastructure race. The round reflects investor confidence in European access-to-space as geopolitical hedge against US export restrictions. Source: Maddyness, TechCrunch

    Strong by Form Raises €5.8M for High-Performance Wood Materials

    Chilean-founded, Madrid-headquartered Strong by Form closed a Series A to commercialize engineered wood as a structural alternative to concrete and steel. The deeptech play addresses European construction sustainability mandates while competing against traditional building materials. Capital follows rising European appetite for climate-aligned industrial infrastructure plays. Source: Tech.eu

    Techshop II Closes €43M First Close, Targets €100M Final

    Milan-based VC firm Techshop announced a €43 million first close for its second fund, focusing on pre-Seed and Seed AI B2B startups. CDP Venture Capital joined as anchor investor; the firm targets €100M by 2027. This signals consolidation in European early-stage AI funding around established operators. Source: EU-Startups

    Ground A Raises €9M+ for Counter-Drone Laser Systems

    German defensetech startup Ground A raised a pre-seed financing round to scale laser-based anti-drone technology. The capital injection reflects rising demand for counter-unmanned systems across NATO borders as drone proliferation accelerates. Source: Tech.eu

    🔍 Signal of the Day

    The De-Globalization Tariff Cascade: How Supply Chain Risk Rewrites Capital Allocation

    Today's 50% US tariffs on Canadian goods, coupled with Iran-US oil tanker strikes and Chinese AI model theft allegations, illustrate a deeper structural shift: multinational capital is repricing the risk of geopolitical supply chain rupture. This isn't 2018-style trade theater—it's foundational reordering.

    Canada's response (€20bn retaliatory tariffs effective immediately) and Treasury Secretary Bessent's public yen-defense gambit signal that currency and tariff policy are now synchronized instruments of geopolitical positioning. The yen surge erodes Japan's export buffers; Canadian retaliation forces US importers to absorb price shocks or relocate sourcing. These frictions compound.

    What this means for entrepreneurs: Vertical integration and nearshoring are no longer cost-optimization plays—they're survival imperatives. Startups building software to automate supply chain transparency (inventory tracking, supplier diversification algorithms, tariff-scenario modeling) will capture outsized value. Similarly, any deeptech or manufacturing innovation that reduces dependence on contested geographies (China's AI talent, rare earth suppliers, semiconductor fabs) now attracts premium capital multiples. The Exploration Company's €387M raise reflects this: European space access is de facto geopolitical hedging for industrial democracies.

    Your portfolio's exposure to manufacturing automation (CloudNC), materials science (Strong by Form), and orbital infrastructure (TEC) is now better-positioned than pure SaaS or consumer-facing bets vulnerable to tariff pass-through.
