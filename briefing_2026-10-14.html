      🌍 Geopolitics & Policy

      Houthi siege of Taïz threatens Yemen's fragile authority

      The Iran-backed militia is tightening control of Taïz, Yemen's third-largest city. Coalition airstrikes continue (fuel depot near Sanaa targeted; ballistic missiles intercepted over Riyadh, four civilians injured). Aramco's Riyadh headquarters was struck in a coordinated attack after Houthi warnings. If Taïz falls, the path to Aden—and effective control of Yemen's coast—opens significantly. This is the most direct threat to Saudi energy infrastructure this year; sustained disruption will keep oil >$100 and pressure global manufacturing costs. Source: Le Grand Continent, BBC, Financial Times

      Russia arrests Japanese journalist on espionage allegations

      A Hokkaido Shimbun reporter was detained in Moscow on charges of collecting information on Russian defense capabilities for Tokyo. The arrest signals rising friction between Russia and US-aligned intelligence partners, even as diplomatic channels remain technically open. Source: BBC

      Ethiopia and Eritrea edge toward conflict despite recent alliance

      Two years after fighting alongside each other in Tigray (which killed hundreds of thousands), both nations are now mobilizing forces. This regional destabilization, paired with Yemen escalation, creates a multipolar crisis environment that diverts US and European attention from Asia. Source: New York Times

      French climate policy faces structural deadlock ahead of 2027 election

      Engineer and Shift Project founder Jean-Marc Jancovici warned that without broad public consensus on climate urgency, political action will stall. This matters for EU green capex and carbon pricing—expect volatility in ESG-tilted holdings if France shifts rightward (Le Pen is running; RN now supports Lecornu's budget). Source: Le Monde

      🔍 Signal of the Day

      The bifurcation of European tech: why geographic arbitrage no longer works

      Today's cluster—Nvidia's €1B+ anchor investment, HyLight/Epyr/Undecimal's €13–16M raises, and Anthropic's legal stand against US security designations—reveals a structural shift in European tech funding. A year ago, European startups could raise at 0.7x US multiples and ship globally. That arbitrage is collapsing.

      Why? Three pressures converge: (1) US capital is now directly entering Europe (Nvidia, tier-1 VCs), eliminating the discount; (2) EU regulatory complexity (AI Act, data residency, antitrust) makes European startups more expensive to scale, not cheaper; (3) geopolitical fragmentation means US and EU tech stacks are slowly decoupling (Anthropic's security row exemplifies this).

      For an entrepreneur, this means: if you're building in Europe, you either need US-tier defensibility (proprietary data, regulatory moats like Doctolib, or deeptech IP) or you must accept being an acqui-hire target for US players. Mid-tier European SaaS founders—the ones who raised $5–20M expecting a 5–7 year exit to a US acquirer—are now crowded out. The winners are either (a) European founders raising Series A+ from US VCs at US valuation, or (b) founders solving EU-specific problems (climate, healthcare, financial regulation) with deep local networks. Geographic arbitrage is dead; defensibility is everything.
