      🔍 Signal of the Day

      AI Capital Concentration: From Software Scarcity to Infrastructure Desperation

      Three distinct funding patterns converge on October 22 to reveal a deeper supply-chain crisis beneath the AI boom narrative. First: DeepSeek's $15B round and Legora's $550M Series E reflect mature venture-stage capital flowing into AI companies at scales that used to require 7–10 years of revenue traction. Second: Photon Bridge's €10M seed and Kinder Morgan's $4.2B Texas gas pipeline both target the same problem—not chips or models, but the "boring" infrastructure to connect chips and power facilities. Third: across equity markets, traditional tech stocks retreated as long-end Treasury yields fell, suggesting investors are pricing in that AI capex may not translate to earnings within investment horizons.

      The pattern: capital is flowing backward through the stack. Six months ago, venture focused on AI applications (reasoning models, multimodal agents). Now, every major VC is racing to own the optical networks, energy pipelines, and data-center real estate that sit beneath compute clusters. This mirrors 2010–2015 infrastructure plays (CDNs, colocation) but with 3x the capital intensity because AI workloads demand continuous power and sub-microsecond latency.

      For an aspiring deeptech founder: this is a warning and an opportunity. Aspiring to build an AI model or applications layer now means competing for capital against $15B incumbents. But building the unglamorous infrastructure to power AI—cooling systems, optical interconnects, modular nuclear plants, carbon-neutral energy—faces less competition and commands structural pricing power. The bottleneck has moved from software to physics.
