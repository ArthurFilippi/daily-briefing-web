      🌍 Geopolitics & Policy

      Trump, Xi, and Putin Converge in Shenzhen for APEC Summit

      China orchestrates a three-way meeting between the US, Russia, and itself on 18–19 November, positioning Beijing as indispensable broker. Xi seeks to frame the summit around stability and openness while constraining conversations on points of friction; Putin promotes "Eurasian" order de-linked from Western institutions. Trump's presence—including floated discussions with Kim Jong-un—injects unpredictability into an otherwise choreographed event. The summit's outcome will signal whether US-China competition remains manageable or escalates into sectoral decoupling (trade, tech, defence). Source: Le Monde, NYT

      Russia Conducts Shadow-War Operations Across Europe

      Covert attacks attributed to Moscow—notably a drone strike on Leipzig/Halle airport in eastern Germany—are escalating against Ukraine's NATO allies. These raids, occurring below the threshold of treaty violation, indicate a shift toward indirect destabilization that bypasses formal diplomatic channels. Villages near the airport now live with elevated security risks; Germany's response framework remains unclear, testing NATO cohesion. Source: NYT

      Democrats Clinch Senate Control; Alaska Seat Finalizes 56–44 Majority

      Mary Peltola's victory in Alaska gives Democrats their 56th Senate seat (Republicans hold 44), securing chamber control despite midterm headwinds. This outcome locks in legislative leverage for the remaining Trump administration and shapes 2027 confirmation battles. Source: Le Monde

      🔍 Signal of the Day

      The "Bank of Nvidia" Collapses; Disciplined Deployment Era Begins

      For 18 months, Nvidia under Jensen Huang became a quasi-sovereign wealth fund for AI startups—deploying billions into robotics, language models, chip startups, and infrastructure with minimal public scrutiny. Huang's October exit (Chinese spy scandal) and the board's refusal to reinstate him has triggered a sharp reversal: Bloomberg reports the "Bank of Nvidia" is now "becoming more discriminating" and "disciplined," with deal velocity expected to slow materially.

      This matters because it unmasks a hidden subsidy structure. When a chip monopolist can backstop funding for downstream customers (robotics, cloud, model labs), it distorts capital allocation—startups optimise for Nvidia endorsement rather than unit economics. Huang's departure removes the charismatic founder effect that justified outsized bets; new leadership requires ROI discipline on a $65 billion portfolio. The spillover: second-tier AI startups that banked on Nvidia largesse face a sudden funding drought. Series B/C rounds will compress; only startups with independent revenue or marquee co-investors (Sequoia, A16Z) survive the crunch.

      For you as a 21-year-old deeptech founder: this is a window to hire talent cheaply. Robotics engineers, ML systems architects, and chip designers from failed Huang-backed startups are now available. The flip side: if you pitch VCs in 2027, they will demand 18-month unit economics proofs and customer concentration risk analysis—the "moonshot" era is over. Your NASDAQ/SP500 portfolio (heavy ETFs like WPEA, PAEEM) benefits from this discipline: capital gravitates toward profitable enterprises and away from cash-sink AI labs, supporting sector rotation into profitability.
