    Twin earthquakes devastated Venezuela; official death toll now tops 1,700, though undercounts are probable. In La Guaira, families continue manual excavations with crowbars and bare hands after formal rescue operations halted, citing unsafe conditions. Venezuelan state capacity shows no signs of coordinating international aid. Source: BBC News, New York Times

    🔍 Signal of the Day

    Why AI Adoption Data Is Contradicting the Narrative

    The headline from Tuesday's employment data should surprise anyone who spent 2024–2025 reading "AI will automate away all jobs" pieces: firms most aggressively deploying generative AI actually hired faster than peers, and entry-level positions grew faster than senior roles. A 10.2% headcount increase among high-intensity AI users versus smaller gains elsewhere inverts the expected displacement pattern.

    What's happening beneath this trend is a genuine reallocation. AI is being deployed as a leverage multiplier for existing staff—allowing junior analysts to produce work that previously required mid-level oversight, which compresses certain roles but expands hiring in complementary areas (data annotation, model refinement, domain-specific prompt engineering). European startups, notably, are losing narratively to US competitors not because their product is inferior but because they underinvest in founder storytelling and omit founder thesis depth in pitches. This parallels the AI adoption pattern: execution and specific value delivery now matter more than aspirational framings.

    For a 21-year-old founder evaluating deeptech ideas, the implication is stark: build for measurable productivity gains in specific workflows (Archimede's remote infrastructure monitoring, Base44's coding assistance), not for "transformative AI." Investors are rotating capital toward firms that quantify impact in operational hours saved or cost per inference. Vague AI narratives now signal weakness, not innovation.
