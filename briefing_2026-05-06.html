    🔍 Signal of the Day

    The Quantum-Deeptech Capital Inflection: Europe's Hardware Moment

    The fact: In 48 hours, European quantum/deeptech companies secured €199.3M in mega-rounds: QuantWare's €152M Series B (backed by Intel), Silo's €25M "quantum Palantir" raise, and Lithosquare's €21.3M for AI mineral discovery. Simultaneously, SAP spent €1.16B acquiring Prior Labs, an 18-month-old German AI lab. These aren't scattered exits—they're proof European founders can now access scale capital for hardware/physics problems that required US West Coast networks 18 months ago.

    Broader pattern: The 2024-2026 period marks structural shift in VC capital allocation toward "atoms not bits." US AI enthusiasm (Anthropic, OpenAI, xAI) consumed most venture oxygen through 2025, but mega-fund LPs now demand differentiation. Quantum computing, battery tech, and mineral discovery offer defensible moats via physics and geopolitics rather than pure compute. European quantum startups benefit from 15-year EU research investment (Horizon Europe, EIC Pathfinder), creating talent/IP density US competitors lack. Intel's QuantWare backing signals semiconductor giants treating quantum not as R&D sidecar but as existential capex.

    Implication for future entrepreneur: If you're a Polytechnique founder with deeptech thesis (fusion, quantum, advanced materials, robotics), 2026 is inflection moment to move fast. Capital now flows to founders who can translate physics into $150M+ funding rounds—no longer gatekept by Sand Hill Road. The moat isn't just your thesis; it's ability to attract tier-1 infrastructure partners (Intel, SAP, major OEMs) early. Build relationships with Fortune 500 CTO offices before you fundraise; they now co-invest venture rounds. European location is feature, not bug—access to subsidized research clusters and regulatory sandboxes (IPCEI frameworks) compounds your advantage. Quantum/materials founders who raise Series A before end-2026 will likely see 3-5x capital efficiency vs. 2024 peers.
