🌍 Geopolitics & Policy

Xi's Shanghai Summit Asserts Counter-Bloc to US

At the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation summit in Kyrgyzstan, Chinese President Xi Jinping reasserted opposition to American "imperialism" weeks before his scheduled meeting with Trump in Washington. The display—flanked by Russia and Iran—signals Beijing's strategy of consolidating an alternative security and trade architecture to challenge US-led multilateralism. This follows G20 deadlock where China blocked consensus on trade language, preventing a joint communiqué. Source: Le Monde

Iran–US Escalation Widens as Retaliatory Strikes Reported

Iran launched attacks on US military bases in Iraq and Jordan after Washington struck targets near Iranian airports and the Strait of Hormuz, killing four civilians at a wedding party. Treasury Secretary Bessent stated the Hormuz route will eventually be "bypassed and become worthless," signalling long-term energy infrastructure realignment. The tit-for-tat cycle reflects breakdown of diplomatic channels and raises energy price volatility. Source: BBC/DW

Germany Confirms Russian Drone Attack on Leipzig Airport

German authorities attributed an August 4 drone strike on Leipzig/Halle Airport—targeting Ukrainian cargo planes—to Russia. The incident underscores infrastructure vulnerability and correlates with INLEAP Photonics' €20M funding for laser-based counter-drone defence systems, signalling hardened market demand for critical infrastructure protection. Source: BBC

UN Climate Report: 1.5°C Threshold Already Breached; Reversal Strategy Proposed

UNEP confirmed the 1.5°C warming target will be exceeded imminently, shifting policy focus to "overshoot" mitigation—cooling the planet below this threshold later this century. This represents abandonment of the 2015 Paris Agreement's primary ambition and requires "radical transformations" in energy and land systems. The admission reshapes decarbonisation economics and carbon-credit markets. Source: Le Monde

🔍 Signal of the Day

From Consensus Breakdown to Bifurcated Supply Chains: Why Today's Geopolitical Frictions Matter for Tech Founders

Today's G20 stalemate—where Beijing blocked joint language on trade and China–US yield divergence widened—crystallises a structural shift: the post-1990s assumption of integrated global commerce is fracturing into competing, parallel ecosystems. Xi's Shanghai summit rhetoric, combined with German confirmation of Russian drone strikes and Iran–US retaliation, signals that state actors are now explicitly building redundant infrastructure to de-risk from adversary disruption.

For deeptech and hardware entrepreneurs, this creates both tailwinds and friction. The €100M Uplift Ventures fund and PLD Space's €288M Series C reflect LP conviction that European independence in critical technologies (launch vehicles, counter-drone systems, secure communications) is now a geopolitical necessity—not just commercial virtue. Venture capital is pricing in a 10–15 year cycle where companies that can navigate dual-supply-chain complexity (EU + US sourcing, decoupled certification paths) and sell into government procurement alongside commercial channels will command premium valuations.

Conversely, founders betting on frictionless global scaling face headwinds. NAND shortages by 2027, rising bond yields strangling refinancing for capital-intensive hardware plays, and tariff uncertainty (Trump coalition polling weakness suggests trade policy volatility) mean that capital-efficient, locally-optimised solutions outcompete globally-distributed ones. Early-stage teams should de-risk by: (1) frontloading EU government contracts (defence, space, critical infrastructure); (2) designing supply chains for dual-sourcing from day one; (3) building margin buffers into hardware economics to absorb tariff shocks.
