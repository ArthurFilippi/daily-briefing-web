    🔍 Signal of the Day

    Why Startups Are Clamping Down on Internal "AI Slop"

    European and North American startups are now actively restricting employee use of generative AI for internal communications, product drafts, and knowledge sharing—a behavioral reversal from six months ago. The rationale: quality attenuation and knowledge loss when LLM-generated content becomes a default input into future training loops. What started as cost-saving and velocity often produces degraded output after two or three generations of AI-on-AI composition.

    This reveals a broader pattern: enterprise AI adoption is bifurcating into two tiers. Tier 1 is high-signal use (legal review, code generation with human validation, customer analysis). Tier 2 (commodity text generation, report writing, internal documentation) is being actively quarantined because marginal returns have turned negative. Companies are learning, post-hype, that not all AI use is additive. This has profound implications: the "AI productivity crisis" that economists worried about six months ago was real—much of deployed AI is value-neutral or negative when measured at the firm level, not the task level.

    For a 21-year-old entrepreneur, this signals that boring, defensible AI winners will be those solving specific, measurable problems (chip design iteration, drug screening, financial compliance validation) rather than replacing generic knowledge work. Consumer-facing "AI copilots" are collapsing in unit economics; B2B infrastructure (inference routing, data pipelines, domain-specific fine-tuning) is where capital is consolidating.
