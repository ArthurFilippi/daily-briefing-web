🔍 Signal of the Day

Why European deeptech capital is clustering around energy-efficient infrastructure, not model training

The fact: In a 48-hour window, three mega-rounds landed in European infrastructure and compute: Nscale's €670M Norway data centre, Algorithmiq's €18M quantum software headquarters relocation to Milan, and eyeo's €40M nanophotonics Series A. All three bypass traditional GPU-centric training and target efficiency, sovereignty, and post-Nvidia alternatives.

Broader pattern: European policy and capital are tacitly abandoning the race to build frontier AI models (where US and China lead on talent, data, and scale). Instead, the continent is doubling down on the infrastructure layer—compute as a commodity, energy as the constraint, and quantum/photonics as asymmetric bets. This mirrors Europe's historical playbook: lose the CPU wars to Intel, own the fab (ASML) and process nodes instead.

Implication for future entrepreneurs: If you're building in Europe, the margin pool is not in language models. It's in making AI runnable cheaply—energy efficiency, sovereign cloud, quantum advantage, edge inference, supply-chain security, and regulatory moats (EU AI Act compliance tooling). Capital will flow toward founders who solve the second-order problem: not "how do we train GPT-7?" but "how do we run GPT-7 for €0.001 per inference without US semiconductors?" That's where European venture returns will accrue over 2026–2028.
