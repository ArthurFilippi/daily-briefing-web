      🌍 Geopolitics & Policy

      Nearly half of global oil sourced from conflict zones; US captures margin

      Almost 50% of planetary petroleum supply originates from countries engaged in active conflict, structuring windfall gains for non-combatant producers like the United States, which enjoys both lower-cost energy feedstock and geopolitical arbitrage. This concentration risk—particularly Iran, Iraq, and maritime choke-point dependencies—amplifies volatility for downstream energy pricing and industrial competitiveness. The dynamic explains both Bessent's willingness to deploy Treasury purchases and broader US eagerness to weaponize sanctions; control over oil flows underwrites monetary and diplomatic leverage simultaneously. Source: Le Grand Continent

      Treasury escalates Iran sanctions to gold, digital assets, shipping, aviation

      Scott Bessent's "Economic D-Day" targets Iran's largest lender (Bank Melli) with full foreign-branch closure mandates and extends coercion across gold reserves, crypto, shipping logistics, and aviation sectors—the broadest sectoral blockade since the current US administration began office. Every foreign entity engaging Iran across these domains faces secondary sanctions, forcing institutional capital and sovereign wealth funds to retreat. This represents a doctrine shift: weaponizing Treasury beyond energy to strangle state access to hard assets and reserve accumulation. Source: New York Times

      SpaceX announces Louisiana megalaunch facility; construction begins 2027

      SpaceX will construct the world's largest orbital launch complex in Louisiana (2.5× Washington DC's footprint) beginning next year, anchoring US spaceport capacity in the Gulf of Mexico and signaling long-term private capital commitment to infrastructure outside traditional Kennedy/Vandenberg sites. The facility's scale underscores trajectory toward reusable-launch cost reduction and Mars mission cadence; political competition with China's expansion justifies accelerated capex. Source: Le Monde

      Syrian Kurdish forces announce dissolution; geopolitical realignment signals

      Kurdish militias formed in 2015 to counter ISIS under US backing formally disband, marking retreat of American proxy infrastructure in Syria and recalibration of Middle East alignment toward Turkish and Russian interests. The move reflects Washington's resource reallocation away from Middle East stabilization toward Pacific deterrence. Source: Le Monde

      🔍 Signal of the Day

      The Great Diversion: Why Europe's Deeptech Boom Looks Different Than VC Hype Suggests

      European founders and venture firms are raising more capital than ever—€50M+ landed this week alone (MAASH €12.15M, eComID €14.6M, Neno €6.6M, ArcSpace €2M+, Solace Care €2.1M)—yet the median deal size, focus, and exit thesis differ sharply from the US mega-round playbook. Instead of betting on winner-take-all AI races or moonshot deeptech (which requires $billions), European capital is clustering around vertical automation, regulatory-arbitrage fintech, and climate-infrastructure plays where regulatory tailwinds and fragmented local markets provide defensibility that algorithms alone cannot buy.

      The pattern reveals a deeper divergence: US venture has bet its return profile on hyperscale and eventual IPO exits (or acquisition by mega-cap tech); Europe is optimizing for steady-state profitability, regional expansion into adjacent EU markets, and eventual strategic acquisition by corporates seeking to offshore compliance automation or reduce supply-chain carbon. French e-invoicing adoption chaos (50% of firms still unregistered 5 days before Sept 1 deadline) and German defense-industrial expansion both underpin this reality: European entrepreneurs win by being essential to regulatory execution, not by disrupting it globally.

      Implication for your future career: If you're considering founding in France post-graduation, the capital is plentiful but the expectation has shifted. Investors want defensible regulatory moats (not just AI feats), unit economics discipline (not hockey-stick projections), and demonstrated customer willingness to pay (not Twitter virality).
