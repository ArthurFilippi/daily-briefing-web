    🌍 Geopolitics & Policy

    Nvidia CEO Huang's affair escalates into national-security probe

    Nvidia Chief Executive Jensen Huang admitted to an extramarital relationship with a woman identified by US intelligence as potentially connected to Chinese intelligence recruitment. Secretary of State Marco Rubio had drafted a classified memo last spring flagging the entanglement. Nvidia's board launched a formal investigation; Yale management professor Jeffrey Sonnenfeld estimates Huang's tenure at roughly 50–50 odds. At $4.5 trillion market cap, this implicates AI infrastructure at the heart of US-China competition. Source: Bloomberg, Le Monde

    Paris convenes Chernobyl remediation donor conference; Ukraine settlement pressure mounts

    France hosts a donor conference Monday to secure remaining funding for repairs to the Chernobyl nuclear sarcophage, damaged by Russian drone strike last year. Timing coincides with G7 ministers' talks on Ukraine as Washington prepares bilateral negotiations with Moscow and Kyiv. European leaders fear premature settlement will force Ukraine into unfavorable terms; France's independent fundraising on nuclear infrastructure signals European stakes in post-war reconstruction and energy security. Source: Le Monde, DW

    Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu begins negotiations Monday with social partners, then high school students, and public-sector unions later in the week. If responses fall short of demands, sustained strikes could damage sitting government credibility ahead of the 2027 presidential election. Communist party chief Fabien Roussel (polling 2–3%) remains defiant despite defections to Mélenchon's France Unbowed faction, fragmenting left opposition. Source: Le Monde, Le Monde

    💼 Business & Finance

    China suspends additional 24% US tariff hike through January; trade truce holds

    China formally extended its pause on an additional 24% tariff on US imports to January 2027, maintaining the existing 10% rate. The decision, formalized by the two nations' leaders, signals appetite for deal-making before US policy potentially hardens. Separately, China is marketing green bonds in Canada for the first time, expanding offshore financing footprint as bilateral ties warm. Source: Bloomberg, Bloomberg

    Chinese producer inflation hits 5.7%—highest since mid-2022; domestic demand remains weak

    Producer prices accelerated to 5.7% year-over-year, the fastest since June 2022, driven by surging energy costs. Consumer inflation, however, decelerated, reflecting persistent weakness in end-demand despite rising factory input costs. The gap signals stagflationary pressure: manufacturers cannot pass costs downstream as consumption slows, squeezing margins amid geopolitical and trade uncertainty. Source: Bloomberg, FT

    Mitsui Chemicals acquires JSR for $12 billion with state-bank backing; chip-materials consolidation accelerates

    Japan's Mitsui Chemicals, with support from the Development Bank of Japan, is acquiring chip-materials specialist JSR Corp. for ¥1.84 trillion ($12 billion). The deal strengthens Japan's grip on a linchpin in semiconductor supply chains at a moment of heightened US-China tech decoupling and regional security competition. Tokyo's use of state capital underscores economic-security strategy reshaping Asia's industrial architecture. Source: Bloomberg

    Soybean futures rise as Trump administration pressures China on farm purchases

    Soybean prices advanced most in two weeks as the US pressed Beijing to fulfill commitments to buy American agricultural goods. The rally reflects ongoing trade-truce mechanics: Beijing signals willingness to purchase commodities as a confidence-building measure, offsetting tariff uncertainty. India, meanwhile, attracted close to $5 billion in foreign stock inflows last week as AI-sector volatility redirected capital flows. Source: Bloomberg, Bloomberg

    🔍 Signal of the Day

    US Intelligence Now Openly Names State-Sponsored Sabotage; Nvidia Is the Flagship Incident

    The Rubio memo, Huang's affair, and the board investigation represent a threshold event: Washington is no longer concealing Beijing's targeting of individual executives as a compartmentalized concern. Instead, it has elevated the story to public-market scale, forcing board-level governance and investor re-pricing. This signals a structural shift from espionage-as-tradecraft to espionage-as-policy instrument—coordinated, high-profile, and designed to raise the cost of hiring or retaining key executives with China exposure.

    What this reveals more broadly is that AI supply-chain security is now understood as leadership-contingent, not just technical. The Huang probe isn't really about one relationship; it's a forcing function that will compel boards to adopt counterintelligence vetting as a standard governance layer. Berkshire Hathaway is raising yen bonds; Mitsui is consolidating JSR with state backing; Japan-Vietnam talks are formalizing tech partnerships. Each reflects a pivot toward trusted, state-aligned technology ecosystems rather than globally optimized supply chains.

    For a young entrepreneur in deeptech, the implication is stark: your future cap table and board composition will be hostage to geopolitical alignment. If you're building in semiconductors, compute infrastructure, or quantum, expect investor diligence on your executive team's foreign relationships and clearance status to become as routine as tax compliance. Raising from US limited partners (pension funds, endowments) will require certifiable distance from Chinese connections. Conversely, raising from Singapore or Tokyo will demand EU-US partnership alignment language. Geopolitics is no longer a tail risk; it's an underwriting criterion.
