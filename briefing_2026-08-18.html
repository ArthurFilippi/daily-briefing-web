      Macro trends:

      1. Great Power competition is reshaping military/tech supply chains. Russia constructs NATO-range drone launchers while South Korea absorbs Trump's exercise cuts; simultaneously, China's AI models (DeepSeek, Qwen) undercut US competitors on cost and adaptability. This bifurcation—geopolitical de-risking on one flank, technological leapfrogging on another—pressures allied defense spending and narrows margins for non-US-aligned vendors. For NASDAQ/deep-tech ETF holders, expect structural tailwinds for US-headquartered AI, defense, and autonomous systems, but margin compression in cost-sensitive segments.

      2. Capital is retreating from political/macro uncertainty into proven revenue models. France's bond market shows short-seller activity on budget anxieties; private equity made zero China deals in July; yet Gravis Robotics hit €862M valuation on $200M SoftBank backing, and Anthropic's annualized revenue surged to $65B. The pattern: flight from sovereign/geopolitical risk toward founders with repeatable unit economics and defensible moats. Early-stage generalist VCs face headwinds; capital clusters around proven revenue or hardware + defense/infrastructure themes.

      🌍 Geopolitics & Policy

      Russia Expands Drone Strike Range Into NATO Territory

      Russia is constructing unmanned launch sites positioned to reach territory within 11 NATO member states, according to analysis from Le Grand Continent. The infrastructure suggests a shift toward sustained pressure on the alliance's eastern flank. This move parallels Moscow's integration of drone warfare into frontline tactics and signals intent to extend operational reach beyond Ukraine. Source: Le Grand Continent

      Trump Cuts South Korea Joint Drills; Opens Door to North Korea Talks

      The US administration announced plans to "substantially reduce" annual military exercises with Seoul, a move Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth attributed to Trump's "very good relationship" with Kim Jong Un. South Korean officials are rattled; the decision undercuts the alliance's deterrent posture and risks emboldening Pyongyang amid wider Pentagon redeployments to the Middle East. Source: Le Monde, FT

      Canada Racing Against Midnight Tariff Deadline

      With a 12-hour window before new US tariffs take effect, Canadian negotiators are seeking a deal to avert further economic damage. The Trump administration has pressured Ottawa into concessions on trade terms, leaving policymakers to weigh short-term pain against longer-term dependency. Source: NYT

      Japan-Russia Diplomatic Spat Over Kuril Islands

      Russia summoned Japan's ambassador after Vladimir Putin visited the southern Kurils (disputed territory) last week. The maneuver reinforces Moscow's territorial claims and escalates Cold War–style messaging as tensions over post-war settlements harden. Source: Le Monde

      🇫🇷 🇷 French/European Entrepreneurship

      Gravis Robotics Hits €862M Valuation; SoftBank Backs $200M Series A

      Swiss construction robotics startup Gravis Robotics closed a $200 million (€172 million) Series A at €862 million post-money valuation, becoming Europe's latest robotics unicorn. SoftBank led the round, betting on autonomous heavy-machinery tech for construction. The deal signals investor appetite for hardware-plus-AI plays with clear paths to scale in infrastructure-heavy markets. Source: Sifted, Tech.eu

      Nik Storonsky's VC Fund Closes $500M for Second Vehicle

      Revolut's founder launched QuantumLight Capital and closed its second fund at $500 million, targeting early-stage deep-tech across quantum, semiconductors, and robotics. The fundraise underscores founder-led VC vehicles' durability despite macro volatility, particularly in Europe where limited partners remain selective. Source: Sifted

      Nebex Launches €100M Initiative for French Space Startups

      Space trading platform Nebex announced a €100 million initiative to convert global space agency spending into revenue for French founders. CEO Tejpaul Bhatia framed the move as a counterweight to SpaceX's dominance, emphasizing that "only a few" founders (besides Musk) capture outsized returns from public budgets. The program targets government procurement and export pathways for French scale-ups. Source: Tech.eu

      Amber Raises €7M Series A for SME Knowledge AI

      Aachen-based AI platform amber closed €7 million Series A co-led by Ventech (doubling down on its seed ticket) and NRW.Venture. The round funds expansion across Europe for business knowledge operationalization—helping SMEs unlock internal data via LLM interfaces. Source: Tech.eu

      💼 Business & Finance

      French Government Bonds Under Short Pressure as Budget Crisis Looms

      Investors are shorting French sovereign debt ahead of contentious 2027 budget negotiations, signaling concern over fiscal discipline before next year's presidential election. Yield spreads have widened as traders anticipate political gridlock and spending escalation. Source: Bloomberg

      Anthropic's Revenue Surge to $65B Annualized

      The AI model company added $18 billion in annualized revenue over two months, now running at $65 billion annual rate. The acceleration reflects explosive adoption of Claude for enterprise and consumer use cases, validating OpenAI's competitive pressure and justifying the 2-3 year timeline to AGI-adjacent systems. Source: TechCrunch

      Private Equity Makes Zero China Deals in July

      Foreign PE firms recorded no new equity investments in China during July, the starkest sign yet of capital flight as Beijing tightens scrutiny over foreign involvement in sensitive sectors. The freeze reflects both regulatory risk and collapsing return expectations. Source: FT

      Copper Supply Squeeze Intensifies; LME Spread Flares

      A key short-term tightness indicator (cash-to-three-month spread) on the London Metal Exchange spiked, signaling acute supply pressure. Zambia's re-election of Hakainde Hichilema (who won >60% in the first round) adds political clarity for the copper-rich nation, but Trump administration pressure for resource access may complicate investment decisions. Source: Bloomberg

      🚀 Tech / Deeptech / VC

      Chinese AI Models (DeepSeek, Qwen, Moonshot) Pose Cost and Capability Threat

      Studies suggest Chinese LLMs now approach US platforms' proficiency while remaining substantially cheaper and more adaptable to regional use cases. DeepSeek, Qwen (Alibaba), and Moonshot (Beijing) have narrowed the capability gap by 12–18 months compared to 2024 benchmarks. The cost advantage (often 50–70% lower inference pricing) erodes margins for US-headquartered vendors and threatens OpenAI/Anthropic's pricing power in Asia. Source: Bloomberg

      South Korea Eliminates Startup From Sovereign AI Competition

      Seoul axed Motif Technologies Co. from its high-stakes sovereign AI contest, narrowing the race to three remaining teams. The move reflects government pressure to consolidate around domestically controlled champions and signals skepticism toward foreign-backed startups in strategic AI infrastructure. Source: Bloomberg

      Relay AI Automation Startup Shuts Down; Team Joins Google Chrome

      AI automation startup Relay ceased operations and transferred its engineering team to Google's Chrome division. Founder Jacob Bank indicated the move aligns with "ambitious plans to help you work with AI in Chrome to get things done," suggesting Chrome OS is becoming a primary testbed for consumer AI agents. Source: TechCrunch

      Meta Faces Social Media Addiction Trial Over Youth Design Practices

      Meta Platforms heads to court Tuesday in a high-stakes showdown with coalition of state attorneys general claiming Facebook and Instagram were deliberately engineered to foster compulsive use among minors. The trial will likely produce discovery on internal design decisions and may set precedent for regulating engagement mechanics. Source: Bloomberg

      🔍 Signal of the Day

      The Great Talent Vacuum: Where Relay's Engineers Are Headed

      When Google Chrome absorbed the engineering team from failed AI automation startup Relay, it signaled something quieter but more significant than another acqui-hire. The move reflects how large-cap tech platforms—faced with competition from cheaper Chinese models and constrained by public scrutiny—are consolidating AI talent directly into consumer-facing products rather than funding independent ventures.

      This pattern extends beyond Google. We see it in SoftBank's €172M bet on Gravis Robotics (not a greenfield VC play, but construction-grade industrial robotics), Nik Storonsky's QuantumLight fund (targeting deep-tech with defined commercial pathways), and the near-total retreat of PE from China. Capital is no longer funding moonshots in uncertain regulatory zones; it's clustering around founders with (a) repeatable revenue models, (b) defensible technical moats, or (c) alignment with geopolitical suppliers (defense, semiconductors, robotics for NATO-adjacent infrastructure).

      For an aspiring entrepreneur at École Polytechnique, the implication is stark: standalone AI agent companies face wind-down risk if they cannot achieve $10M+ ARR within 18 months (see Anthropic's $65B run rate as the exception proving the rule). The real capital today flows to (1) founders solving specific vertical problems (construction robots, medical imaging, defense tech), (2) those with geographic arbitrage into underserved markets (French space, Indian rupee-denominated services), or (3) teams who can credibly control supply chains (semiconductors, energy infrastructure). Broad-platform plays are Google's, Meta's, and OpenAI's game now. Build defensible, specific, or get acquired early.
