    🌍 Geopolitics & Policy

    China's Expanding Military Footprint in the South China Sea

    Satellite imagery shows Beijing expanding its largest artificial island on Antelope Reef, a move analysts assess as materially strengthening China's military capabilities across the disputed waterway. The construction underscores a long-term strategy to entrench territorial claims without triggering overt conflict escalation. Vietnam, the Philippines, and US Indo-Pacific allies view this as part of a broader gray-zone competition over resource access and chokepoint control. This trend accelerates military modernization spending by regional allies and raises insurance costs for critical shipping corridors. Source: Le Grand Continent

    Trump Administration Escalates "Economic D-Day" Rhetoric Against Iran

    President Trump has signaled willingness to impose "economic penalties" on third countries doing business with Iran, framing this as an alternative to kinetic escalation. The language—deliberately invoking military terminology—aims to shape behavior without committing to direct military intervention. However, no specific tariff rates or enforcement mechanisms have been announced, leaving markets pricing in ambiguity. Iran's trading partners, particularly China, are now calculating exposure; Beijing has historically circumvented sanctions through front companies and barter arrangements. Source: NY Times

    Hong Kong Activists Convicted Under National Security Law

    Lee Cheuk-yan (69) and Chow Hang-tung (41), former leaders of the Hong Kong Alliance, were found guilty of "inciting subversion" for organizing annual Tiananmen vigils. Both face up to 10 years imprisonment. The verdict signals that Beijing's tolerance for commemorative activism has narrowed further, reinforcing capital flight concerns among Hong Kong's professional class and deterring civil society organizing around historical grievances. This precedent will likely ripple through regional protest movements. Source: BBC

    Panama Canal Traffic Curtailed by El Niño-Driven Drought

    Record precipitation deficits linked to El Niño have depleted freshwater reserves in the Gatun and Alajuela lakes, forcing authorities to reduce daily transit volumes. The canal typically processes ~14,000 transits annually; reduced lockage capacity compounds existing supply-chain friction and increases shipping insurance premiums. This hydraulic constraint will persist through Q4 2026 unless rainfall normalizes, adding cost pressure to Asia-US trade flows and marginal inflation to downstream logistics. Source: Le Monde

    🇫🇷 🇷 French/European Entrepreneurship

    Callosum Raises €85.4M for AI Compute Heterogeneity Infrastructure

    London-based Callosum closed an exceptional seed round of €85.4M ($100M) led by Atomico to build software enabling AI workloads to run across heterogeneous chip architectures and models. The round, completed in two weeks, signals institutional conviction that standardization of AI compute is a critical middleware problem. This addresses an underexplored gap: as frontier labs diversify away from NVIDIA-only deployments (cost, supply, performance heterogeneity), infrastructure-layer plays command premium valuations. Source: EU-Startups

    Pixelgen Technologies Secures €13.26M for Cell Protein Mapping

    Stockholm-based Pixelgen closed an oversubscribed Series B of €13.26M ($15.5M) to advance spatial mapping of cell-surface proteins—a foundational capability for drug discovery and immunotherapy development. The round was led by new investor Flat Capital, signaling growing regional appetite for structural biology infrastructure plays. This positions Northern European biotech alongside UK/Swiss hubs as a meaningful frontier for computational biology. Source: EU-Startups

    Solinide Photonics Raises €4M for Silicon-Nitride AI Datacentre Chips

    Swedish deeptech company Solinide closed a €4M seed round to commercialize siliconnitride photonic integrated circuits targeting AI datacentre interconnect. The funding reflects structural demand for analog/photonic compute solutions as power consumption of digital inference becomes untenable at scale. This sits within a broader EU deeptech thesis: photonics and analog compute are capability gaps versus US/China competitors, making them a legitimate policy funding target. Source: Tech.eu

    Velatir Secures €5M for European AI Infrastructure Expansion

    Odense-based AI infrastructure startup Velatir raised €5M in seed (co-led by Spintop Ventures) to scale its platform across Europe and compete for talent. The round closed in two weeks, underscoring appetite for EU-based compute abstraction layers. Velatir aims to reduce lock-in to US cloud hyperscalers by providing federated, cost-optimized inference infrastructure—a strategic play given EU regulatory momentum around data sovereignty and AI Act compliance. Source: EU-Startups

    💼 Business & Finance

    US Treasury's Bond Buyback Plan Meets Skepticism from "Bond Vigilantes"

    Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced elevated buybacks of long-dated US debt to manage refinancing costs amid elevated yields and AI-driven capex competition for capital. Wall Street strategists are describing the move as "a band-aid on a bullet hole," noting that $32tn in outstanding Treasuries require structural fiscal restraint, not tactical repositioning. Gold rallied on the announcement as investors hedged duration risk; Australian bond futures positions hit record highs as foreign buyers hedged currency exposure. Source: FT, Bloomberg

    Alphabet Issues A$5.5B in Kangaroo Bonds to Fund AI Capex

    Google's parent company tapped Australia's bond market for the first time, raising A$5.5B ($3.9B) via so-called Kangaroo bonds to finance AI infrastructure spending. The move signals both the competitive intensity of AI capex (requiring diverse funding sources) and relative yield attractiveness in non-US developed markets. This diversification away from USD financing reflects macro stress: US rates have remained sticky despite Powell's earlier signaling, creating refinancing drag across tech mega-caps. Source: Bloomberg

    Alibaba Profit Collapses 75% as Quarterly AI Spending Nears $10B

    Chinese tech giant Alibaba reported quarterly profit decline of over 75% after increasing capex to nearly $10B to defend its position in global AI competition. The earnings miss underscores the margin compression cycle afflicting megacap tech: frontier AI development is capital-intensive, has no near-term revenue offset, and crowds out shareholder returns. Samsung responded by pledging up to ¥110T ($79B) in shareholder buybacks—an attempt to restore investor confidence amid similar capex pressures. Source: Bloomberg

    RBI Hawkish Signals Unsettle Indian Bond Markets

    Reserve Bank of India minutes released this week signaled a more restrictive policy stance than consensus expected, particularly as foreign currency deposit scheme inflows begin to fade. Indian bond traders repriced duration risk sharply; yields are drifting higher. Meanwhile, India's securities regulator issued record-speed interim orders against a JPMorgan Chase unit for alleged auction manipulation, signaling regulatory vigilance on market integrity as capital inflows accelerate. Source: Bloomberg

    🔍 Signal of the Day

    AI Capex Arms Race Inverting the Profitability Equation

    Alibaba's 75% earnings collapse despite record revenues and Samsung's €79B shareholder return pledge are not independent noise—they are symptoms of a structural phase transition in tech competition. Frontier AI development requires capital outlays ($10B+/quarter for hyperscalers) that have no near-term revenue model and systematically depress reported profitability. This creates a paradox: the most competitive firms (those investing heaviest in AI) report the weakest earnings, inverting traditional equity valuation frameworks.

    The broader pattern: infrastructure-layer plays are attracting disproportionate venture capital precisely because they decouple from this capex drag. Callosum's €85.4M seed, Velatir's €5M expansion, Pixelgen's €13.26M Series B, and Solinide's €4M all target the middleware of AI—heterogeneous compute, spatial biology mapping, photonic interconnect—rather than the frontier models themselves. These companies will benefit from capex intensity (their customers are capex-constrained megacaps seeking efficiency) without bearing the full burden themselves.

    For an early-stage deeptech founder, the implication is stark: if your company's unit economics or go-to-market depend on selling to megacap tech buyers, you've just been handed a 24-36 month window of exceptional demand and compressed competition for attention. Megacaps are simultaneously desperate for efficiency gains and willing to write large checks to avoid building infrastructure in-house. But this window is time-bound—once the capex cycle normalizes (2027-2028), margin recovery will resume, and internal R&D will crowd out external procurement. Build for optionality: either achieve 10x efficiency on critical paths, or develop a defensible moat that persists beyond the capex supercycle.
