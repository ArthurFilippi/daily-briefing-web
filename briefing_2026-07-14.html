      🌍 Geopolitics & Policy

      US-Iran Open Conflict Resumes; Trump Announces 20% Strait of Hormuz Toll

      On its third consecutive night, the US conducted fresh strikes against Iranian targets as President Trump announced a unilateral 20% levy on all cargo transiting the Strait of Hormuz. Iran responded with cruise missile attacks on tankers. This represents a formal end to the June cease-fire and a direct challenge to international maritime law—Trump's own aides (VP Vance, Secretary of State Rubio) have stated no country can legally impose such fees. The immediate effect: oil prices spike, Chinese crude imports collapse to decade lows, and the Philippine peso approaches record weakness. Sources: NYT Live, BBC, DW

      France Deploys Record Military Presence for July 14 Parade

      Paris' annual Bastille Day procession featured ground troops and aircraft at double the scale of Washington's 2025 parade, reflecting France's assertion of military posture amid broader NATO repositioning. The spectacle coincided with opinion pieces questioning whether Trump's symbolic appropriation of French republican iconography signals ambitions for executive presidency beyond constitutional norms. Sources: Le Grand Continent

      India-Bangladesh Border Tensions Escalate Over Deportation Policy

      India is unilaterally deporting Bangladeshi migrants classified as undocumented, leaving hundreds stranded at the border and triggering retaliatory pressure from Dhaka. This regional migration crisis underscores deepening South Asian instability and signals risk for cross-border labor markets. Source: NYT

      Hungary Parliament Removes Presidential Loyalist; UK Designates IRGC as Terrorist Entity

      Hungary's parliament voted to remove President Tamás Sulyok, an Orbán ally, marking the first major institutional challenge to the former autocrat's influence post-April election. Concurrently, the UK formally designated Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps as a terrorist organization, enabling enhanced counterintelligence operations. Sources: NYT, BBC

      💼 Business & Finance

      China Car Exports Surge Past 1M Monthly; Yuan Remains Undervalued

      China exported over 1 million vehicles in June—a record—while becoming the world's second-largest importer. Yet Deutsche Bank assesses the yuan as still cheap versus the euro despite recent appreciation, contributing to widening EU trade deficits. This asymmetry reflects state-managed currency policy and export-driven policy, not market equilibrium. Sources: FT, Bloomberg

      China's Crude Oil Imports Plunge to Near-Decade Low

      Chinese crude purchases fell sharply in June, hampered by Persian Gulf warfare and collapsing domestic demand. This signals synchronized macro weakness: both supply shock (Hormuz blockade) and demand destruction (China slowdown) compressing oil markets. Portfolio implication: energy volatility will persist but longer-duration headwinds suggest lower-for-longer crude. Sources: Bloomberg

      Paramount-Warner Merger Faces 12-State Antitrust Challenge

      🔍 Signal of the Day

      Japan's 90% Lithium Recovery Breakthrough Reshapes EV Battery Circularity

      Japan announced a novel method to recover up to 90% of lithium from spent EV batteries—a critical step toward closing the supply chain loop in electrified transport. This is not incremental: current recycling rates hover around 50%, and lithium scarcity remains a hard constraint on global EV scale-up. The breakthrough sidesteps geopolitical dependency on Chilean, Argentine, and Australian mining while reducing environmental footprint.

      Why it matters: As US-China chip wars restrict access to rare earth and semiconductor inputs, battery recycling becomes strategic infrastructure. This Japanese development signals that deeptech founders focusing on circular industrial processes—not just novel materials—now have policy-backed tailwinds. Japanese state backing, coupled with rising EV demand across Asia, will attract capital and talent into battery-tech startups within 18 months. The implication for a young entrepreneur: circular supply chains (lithium, cobalt, rare earths) are becoming as venture-able as primary production. Consider founding teams with mining engineering + chemistry expertise as founders-in-residence for future VCs.
