🔍 Signal of the Day

Energy Infrastructure as AI's Binding Constraint

The fact: NextEra Energy is in merger talks to acquire Dominion (€400B+) explicitly to meet data center power demand. Simultaneously, NATO's Brussels summit next week will push European defense contractors to boost arms production (FT), and Hantavirus in France plus Ebola in DRC are exposing state health infrastructure gaps. These are separate crises—except they're not.

Broader pattern: AI deployment, geopolitical rearmament, and pandemic preparedness all face the same bottleneck: energy infrastructure built for 20th-century baseload demand cannot sustain 21st-century power-hungry compute, defense systems, or distributed health surveillance. The US is consolidating utilities; Europe is scrambling to coordinate NATO arms procurement; Africa has no surplus grid capacity. This isn't a technology problem—it's a capex sequencing problem.

Implication for a future entrepreneur: If you're building in deeptech, energy, or critical infrastructure, the next 24 months will be characterized by structural capital flows toward boring infrastructure. Unsexy sectors (power transmission, grid modernization, modular nuclear) will see 10x capital multiples vs. software. European founders should focus on distributed energy solutions (local microgrids, modular storage, demand-side flexibility) rather than centralized cloud/AI platforms—EU policy now incentivizes grid resilience, not density. The venture window for AI-native energy optimization closes when utilities' capex pivots from software to concrete.
