    🔍 Signal of the Day

    European Deeptech's Escape from Nvidia Dependency

    The cluster of €26–28M raises in Zurich (Enact—tactile robotics), Lisbon (Beyond Vision—drones), and Amsterdam (Indurex—industrial cyber-security) in one week reveals a deliberate repositioning by European VCs and corporates. Rather than betting on LLM commoditization, founders are raising at seed/pre-Series A scale for physical embodiment and industrial robustness—layers that require domain expertise and regulatory compliance, not just compute scaling.

    This matters because it inverts the 2023–25 playbook: instead of chasing API access to Nvidia GPUs and training tokenizers, 2026's winners are building moats in perception (tactile sensing), autonomy (swarm logic, defensive UAVs), and compliance (CPS security for grid/manufacturing). The US Treasury's new China tech restrictions and NATO's pivot toward allied supply chains create regulatory tailwinds for European players who aren't purely reliant on Nvidia's margins.

    For a 21-year-old founder: this signals a 3–5 year window to build in physical AI, defense-adjacent robotics, and industrial automation before US hyperscalers (Tesla, Boston Dynamics spin-offs, etc.) consolidate. You'd need 18–24 months to raise €5–10M, hire PhDs, and ship a credible prototype. The capital is flowing. The regulation is forcing diversification away from pure compute. But the window is finite—China will replicate within 18 months if you don't.
