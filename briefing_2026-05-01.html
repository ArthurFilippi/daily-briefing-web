🔍 Signal of the Day

The Inference Revolution: Compute Efficiency Becomes the New Moat

Nebius's acquisition of Eigen AI, Apple's chip-shortage admission, and OpenAI's CFO rhetoric on "vertical wall of demand" collectively signal a structural shift in AI's competitive landscape.

The fact: A year ago, the race was for "bigger models, more tokens." Today, the bottleneck is inference cost at scale. Eigen AI's valuation reflects investor belief that making LLM inference 2–3x cheaper or faster unlocks entirely new commercial tiers (real-time applications, edge deployment, lower-margin verticals). Simultaneously, memory-chip shortages are making compute infrastructure scarce—a reversal of 2023's token-counting abundance.

Broader pattern: This mirrors the GPU duopoly collapse in 2024–25. When one resource (tokens, GPUs, memory) becomes the scarce constraint, startups that optimise around that constraint capture disproportionate value. We saw it with Anthropic's Constitution AI (efficiency), now we're seeing it with inference-cost optimisation. The next wave will be power/thermal efficiency for on-device AI.

Implication for a future entrepreneur: If you're building deeptech or AI infrastructure, the question is not "can we train bigger models?" but "can we deploy at 1/10th the cost or latency?" Founders who obsess over inference unit economics, edge deployment, or memory efficiency will find venture and enterprise appetite far exceeds those chasing raw model size. France/EU has a structural advantage here: energy costs and supply-chain fragility incentivise efficiency-first engineering cultures. Position yourself accordingly.
