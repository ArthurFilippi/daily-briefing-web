      🌍 Geopolitics & Policy

      AfD Dominance Scenario Advances in Saxe-Anhalt Election

      Germany's far-right Alternative for Germany party would govern under 60% of coalition scenarios in next week's Saxe-Anhalt election if the BSW (Bündnis Sahra Wagenknecht) abstains, according to exclusive modeling by Le Grand Continent. Former Chancellor Angela Merkel has publicly urged voters to defend democracy, signaling establishment alarm. The September 6 vote will determine 83 Landtag seats; AfD leads consolidated polling. Source: Le Grand Continent

      Venezuela Oil Pact Roils Political Spectrum

      President Trump announced the US will control majority stakes in 65 billion barrels of Venezuelan reserves under a partnership with a private Caracas-linked entity. Interim President Delcy Rodriguez insists Venezuela retains sovereignty and ownership; both hardliners and opposition leaders denounce the deal as colonial overreach or Washington meddling. The accord's opacity—lacking published terms—has fueled speculation it favors US extraction at expense of Venezuelan state revenues. Source: BBC, Le Monde

      Niger Junta Quashes Mutiny With Russian Wagner Support

      Renegade Nigerien troops attacked Niamey's presidential palace and air base on August 29; the junta's presidential guard and Russia's Africa Corps retook a key military installation adjacent to the capital airport, killing several mutineers. The episode signals fragility within Niger's military governance and deepening dependency on Moscow for internal security. Source: Le Monde, NYT

      Somalia-Turkey Piracy Operation Kills 14; 15 Attacks Recorded This Year

      Turkish and Somali forces freed the hijacked MV LUTUF after a 10-day operation that resulted in 14 pirate deaths and destruction of four attack vessels. At least 15 piracy incidents have been recorded off Somalia's coast since January 2026, per International Maritime Organization data—a resurgence that threatens Red Sea trade and insurance costs. Puntland regional authorities alleged ransom was paid despite official denials. Source: Le Monde, DW

      Iceland EU Referendum Tightens; Yes Camp Leads 51.2% in Early Count

      Icelanders voted on whether to resume EU membership talks; early tallies showed a razor-thin lead for the "yes" camp at 51.2%, though election officials warned final results may not arrive until later Sunday. The outcome will pivot Iceland's strategic alignment and fiscal policy for years. Source: BBC, DW

Source: Le Monde

      Catastrophic flooding along Nepal's China border left survivors sifting through thick mud in devastated towns including Bidur. Rescue operations face fresh threats from rising water levels in key rivers, risking further disruption. The vulnerable nation faces an immense recovery challenge. Source: NYT, Bloomberg

      💼 Business & Finance

      G7 Debt Servicing Costs Surge Tens of Billions on Rising Bond Yields

      Since the outbreak of the US-Iran conflict, rising yields have added substantial financing pressure to the world's largest developed economies. Higher yields compound deficit pressures amid aging populations and infrastructure needs, constraining fiscal space for stimulus or growth investment. Central banks face a dilemma: tighten further to control inflation, or risk currency depreciation and import-driven price shocks. Source: FT

      Fed Chair Warsh Signals Inflation Persists; Rate Hikes Remain On Table

      Kevin M. Warsh stated at Jackson Hole that inflation is not slowing and the Fed retains a "firm, fixed" 2% target. He indicated "we have work to do" if price growth does not normalize quickly, implying readiness to raise rates despite Trump administration sensitivity. This messaging conflicts with market expectations of rate cuts and signals a prolonged high-rate regime. Source: NYT, Bloomberg

      Treasury Restricts Journalist Credentials at G20 Finance Meeting

      The US Treasury barred select reporters from The New York Times, Wall Street Journal, and Bloomberg from covering the September G20 financial leaders meeting in Asheville, North Carolina. The move raises press freedom concerns and signals selective information control during a period of heightened geopolitical and economic tension. Source: NYT

      Acrisure Credit Suffers as Guggenheim Ties Drag High-Yield Markets

      Acrisure, the fintech-insurance hybrid, cut 11% of its workforce (2,250 people) in May as part of a modernization overhaul; its bonds and loans have fallen, though remaining above prior lows. Guggenheim Partners' financial distress is now contaminating credit sentiment for affiliated firms, highlighting concentration risk in private equity-backed debt. Source: Bloomberg

      Music Labels Divide on AI Training: Lawsuits vs. Partnerships

      Sony Music and Warner Chappell sued Anthropic in the US District Court for the Northern District of California, alleging a "brazen" intellectual property theft campaign and seeking up to $150,000 per work plus $25,000 per identifiable copyright violation. Parallel AI music generators like Suno have also faced legal action from some labels while striking deals with others, creating a fragmented market response to AI-generated content licensing. Source: TechCrunch, The Verge

      🔍 Signal of the Day

      Quantum's IPO Problem: Valuation vs. Viability

      PASQAL's Nasdaq debut—doubling from SPAC close yet triggering 90%+ redemptions—encapsulates a widening credibility gap in European deeptech exits. The French quantum firm raised cash for industrialization but failed to convince early investors (Bleichroeder shareholders) that the business model justified staying in. This is not equity volatility; it's a structural mismatch between IPO timing and commercial proof points.

      The pattern spans quantum, fusion, and synthetic biology: founders and VCs have become adept at narrating exponential technical progress, but markets now demand evidence of revenue, unit economics, or binding customer contracts before public listing. PASQAL has none of these anchors yet. Instead, it inherits a $19+ share price and must now deliver chip shipments, customer wins, and positive cash-flow milestones in a macro environment where debt is expensive and enterprise capex scrutiny is tightening.

      For an aspiring deeptech founder, the lesson is blunt: IPO optionality is no longer a viable exit story in a rising-rate regime. The venture path now requires earlier customer validation (ideally paying pilots or LOIs from tier-1 accounts), clearer path to gross margin, and defensible IP that justifies the capital intensity. The SPAC craze masked this for 18 months; PASQAL's public market debut signals that era is over.
