    🔍 Signal of the Day

    Orange Cable Theft Spikes on Copper Price Surge; Infrastructure Vulnerability Exposed

    French telecom Orange is losing kilometers of legacy copper cable to organized theft as spot prices for the metal hit multi-year highs. Small criminal networks are sectioning and stripping cables from Orange's historical network, triggering widespread broadband and phone outages. No figures on total losses released, but disruptions are frequent enough to warrant executive acknowledgment.

    This exposes a structural fragility in aging telecom infrastructure: as energy transition and EV adoption push copper demand skyward, previously-worthless scrap becomes theft targets. The incentive to strip undefended outdoor plant is now material. Telecom operators globally—especially in markets with underinvested rural plant—face similar pressure. German carriers reported similar trends in 2024-25.

    For a deeptech founder, this signals opportunity in two directions: (1) remote cable-integrity monitoring systems using IoT + ML to detect theft in real time, and (2) materials science solutions for copper-alternative conductors in low-criticality rural loops. Both address a growing externality cost that telecom capex budgets will soon internalize as formal line items. Regulatory pressure to maintain universal service makes this a defensible market.
