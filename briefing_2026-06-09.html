Ukraine War Now Equals WWI Duration

From tomorrow (June 10), the large-scale Russian invasion will have lasted 1,566 days—matching the entire duration of World War I. Including the 2014 Crimea annexation, Ukraine has endured 4,492 days of Russian aggression—twice the length of WWII. This milestone underscores the conflict's historical magnitude and raises questions about sustainability of military aid, domestic political fatigue, and reconstruction timelines. Source: Le Grand Continent

🔍 Signal of the Day

Capital Is Fleeing Europe for US Tech Public Markets

Three signals converged on June 8–9: OpenAI and Anthropic filed for US IPO; Bending Spoons (Milan-based) filed for Nasdaq listing; and FrenchWeb reported European VCs increasingly finance US growth rather than building locally. Separately, PhysicsX ($2.4bn) and OQC (€299m) raised later-stage deeptech rounds but notably in offshore structures or with Asian leads (Temasek).

This pattern reflects a structural arbitrage: US public markets price AI/deeptech at 3–5x European multiples, and US venture dollars are cheaper than European capital once you account for governance and exit optionality. European founders and investors face a binary choice: 1) list in the US early and capture valuation premium, or 2) accept lower European multiples and slower exit timelines. The Paris-London-Amsterdam tech corridor is generating world-class founders (OpenAI's Anthropic board has European roots; Bending Spoons is Italian), but exit mechanisms and capital structure economics push them toward US markets.

For a future polytechnicien entrepreneur: if you're building AI/deeptech with >€20M fundraising needs, structure early for US market access. The cost of capital arbitrage alone justifies Delaware incorporation, even if your team and initial market are European. Conversely, if you're building Europe-specific infrastructure (energy, telecom, defense) or B2B SaaS with <€50M revenue ceiling, stay in Europe—regulatory moats and local anchor customers favor proximity.
