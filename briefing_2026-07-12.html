      🌍 Geopolitics & Policy

      US launches strikes as Iran closes Strait of Hormuz

      After Iran fired on a Cyprus-flagged container ship transiting an unapproved route, the IRGC declared the Strait closed; Centcom responded with its third strike package in days. Negotiations between Iranian and Omani foreign ministers on corridor access had occurred hours before the attack, suggesting Iran's closure is a calculated statement, not a breakdown. The US Navy chief subsequently called for expanded warship production and munitions capacity to sustain commitments across multiple theaters. Source: BBC, Financial Times, Bloomberg

      Oil prices spike above prewar levels despite easing crude

      Crude edged lower this week, yet gasoline, diesel, and jet fuel rebounded—a rare divergence that signals terminal bottlenecks and refinery capacity strain rather than commodity shortage alone. Hormuz instability translates into shipping insurance premiums and hedging costs that filter through fuel retail faster than spot prices move. Source: Bloomberg, NYT

      Russia's Tokyo intelligence hub industrializes semiconductor theft

      Operating from a Tokyo high-rise, a Russian military intelligence unit systematically procures advanced electronics and drones needed for Ukraine operations. The operation exploits Japan's historical export controls gaps and leverage Japan's position as a chip-design and manufacturing power. Source: NYT

      Typhoon Bavi displaces 2M+ in China; sequential storm pattern accelerates

      A second typhoon in one week forced nearly two million evacuations from Zhejiang; Bavi weakened to severe tropical storm on Sunday morning but spawned deadly landslides across Southeast Asia. Compounding climate events are eroding supply-chain redundancy in semiconductor, battery, and precision manufacturing hubs. Source: BBC, Bloomberg

      🔍 Signal of the Day

      Long COVID damages vagal nerve signaling; implications for biotech thesis rotation

      Research in Infectious Diseases Journal demonstrates that Long COVID causes measurable nerve-tissue damage along the vagus nerve pathways controlling stomach function, moving etiology from purely inflammatory/viral persistence into structural neurological injury. This finding validates a slow-building biotech thesis: post-viral syndromes require mechanistic drug targets, not immunomodulation alone.

      Broader pattern: Over the past two years, Long COVID research has migrated from academic curiosity into FDA priority review lanes. GLP-1 developers (Novo, Eli Lilly) and neuroimmunology-focused startups (Neurobiological Tech, Neurochem Partners) are now filing INDs targeting vagal dysfunction and neuropathic signaling. Separately, Martha Lillard's recent death—the last US iron-lung polio patient—marks the symbolic end of an infectious-disease era; her passing coincides with resurgent polio vaccine efforts and biotech capital rotating toward rare neurological sequelae.

      Implication: As a 21-year-old deeptech investor, monitor early-stage neuroimmunology and vagal-modulation startups for Series A/B entry points. The window for first-mover advantage in post-viral neuropathy is ~24 months before larger pharma acquires the category. Patent landscapes remain thin; structural nerve-tissue repair (scaffold-based, gene-therapy-adjacent) is underexplored relative to inflammatory cytokine targets.
