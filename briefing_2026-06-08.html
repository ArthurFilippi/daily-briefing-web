🌍 Geopolitics & Policy

Iran and Israel Exchange Missile Strikes; Ceasefire at Risk

Iran fired missiles at Israel in retaliation for Israeli attacks on Lebanese targets, hours after which Israeli forces struck Iranian military installations in Tehran, Tabriz, and Isfahan. The exchange marks the first direct military contact since a 2-month ceasefire begun in April, raising the risk of wider regional escalation. Ceasefire viability now hinges on whether both sides view this as contained retaliation or a trigger for renewed conflict. Source: NYT, DW

UK, Germany, France Endorse Ukraine–Russia Direct Talks

Prime Ministers Keir Starmer, Friedrich Merz and Emmanuel Macron jointly backed President Zelenskyy's proposal for direct dialogue with Putin, with the caveat that current front lines must serve as negotiation baseline and borders cannot shift by force. This coordinated European position signals willingness to engage Russia diplomatically, though it stops short of concessions on sovereignty. The statement was issued following trilateral talks in London with the Ukrainian leader. Source: DW, Le Monde

Armenia's PM Pashinyan Claims Election Victory, Tilts Further West

Prime Minister Nikol Pashinyan's Civil Contract party won decisively in parliamentary elections (partial results showed 43% support), cementing his westward pivot away from Russian influence following Armenia's 2023 military defeat by Azerbaijan. The electoral result consolidates Pashinyan's authority to pursue closer EU and NATO ties, reversing decades of Moscow-centric foreign policy. This represents the first democratic test since the devastating war. Source: DW, Le Monde

Kosovo's PM Wins Snap Vote But Lacks Majority; Political Gridlock Deepens

Albin Kurti's leftist nationalist party won 43% in snap elections (the third ballot in 15 months), but failed to secure a parliamentary majority amid record-low turnout. Kosovo's prolonged political crisis continues to weaken an already ailing economy, leaving coalition negotiations likely to drag into summer. Voter apathy reflects public exhaustion with institutional dysfunction. Source: DW, Le Monde

💼 Business & Finance

Oil Surges to Decades-High Premium After Iran–Israel Strikes

Crude prices jumped sharply following Iran's missile salvo, with Saudi Arabia's official selling price remaining near multi-decade highs despite a second consecutive monthly cut to July Asian pricing. The spike reflects trader anxiety that renewed Middle East conflict could disrupt Strait of Hormuz flows; OPEC+ announced a symbolic 188,000 barrels-per-day production increase despite vast swaths of output already stranded by effective closure of the strait. Geopolitical risk now dominates energy fundamentals. Source: FT, NYT, Bloomberg

Asian Tech Rout Accelerates: Seoul Triggers Circuit Breaker

South Korean equities plunged Monday, triggering automatic trading halts, as investors reversed blistering AI-sector gains from prior weeks. SK Hynix and Samsung led declines; the broader KOSPI suffered its worst session in months as momentum shifted from euphoria to profit-taking and valuation concerns. Goldman Sachs' Timothy Moe predicted a near-term rebound, but the volatility signals that leveraged AI positions may face forced liquidation. Source: Bloomberg, FT

Software M&A Collapses to Pandemic Lows; AI Rout Pressures Deal Pipeline

Private equity software acquisitions fell to $50 billion in the first five months of 2026—the lowest level since the pandemic—as the tech selloff eroded buyer confidence and target valuations became unstable. The contraction suggests that dealmaking appetite for traditional software assets evaporates when AI-darling stocks are in free fall, creating secondary-order contagion across the PE ecosystem. Source: FT

China's Xi Visits North Korea; Nuclear Expansion Rhetoric Escalates

Chinese President Xi Jinping arrived in Pyongyang for his first trip to North Korea in seven years, as the regime intensifies nuclear expansion rhetoric and the US deepens sanctions. The summit underscores Beijing–Pyongyang coordination on US containment strategy and signals China's commitment to North Korea as a geopolitical counterweight despite international isolation of the Kim regime. Economic data shows North Korea has recovered post-pandemic by leveraging Russian war demand. Source: FT, NYT

🚀 Tech / Deeptech / VC

China's Moonshot AI Seeks $30B Valuation in Fresh Funding Round

Moonshot AI is raising up to $2 billion in a new financing round at a $30 billion valuation, marking its third capital injection in six months as China's AI race intensifies. The Beijing-based model developer faces mounting pressure to keep pace with rivals (including DeepSeek) in an ecosystem now shaped by state-backed backing and outbound capital restrictions. This signals confidence among Chinese investors despite broader tech volatility. Source: Bloomberg

China Imposes New Outbound Investment Curbs on AI Startups

China's Communist Party officially restricted foreign investment by domestic AI startups, placing national security and US competition above growth imperatives. The regulatory shift—formalized via new outbound investment rules—erects a "wall" around China's AI ecosystem, effectively decoupling it from Western capital and talent flows. This represents an unprecedented pivot from the pre-2020 open innovation stance and signals that Beijing now treats AI as a strategic asset category akin to semiconductors. Source: Le Grand Continent

Nvidia CEO Huang Calls Tech Selloff a "Buying Opportunity"; AI Buildout "Just Beginning"

Jensen Huang dismissed the ongoing tech stock rout in Seoul, arguing that large-scale AI infrastructure deployment is still in early innings and volatility creates entry points for long-term believers. Huang's confidence—backed by Nvidia's blockbuster earnings power—contrasts sharply with panic selling among retail and leveraged traders, though it does not address valuation questions or energy grid constraints in data center buildout. Source: Bloomberg

Nvidia and SK Hynix Seal Multi-Year AI Chip Design Partnership

Nvidia and South Korean memory giant SK Hynix signed a multi-year pact to co-develop next-generation AI memory chips, strengthening SK Hynix's competitive position against Samsung in the high-margin AI accelerator supply chain. The deal represents a vote of confidence in South Korean semiconductor engineering despite Monday's market turmoil and signals long-term commitment to diversified supply-chain relationships for advanced memory. Source: Bloomberg

🔍 Signal of the Day

Beijing's AI Capital Controls Are the Real Tech Decoupling

China's formal restriction on outbound investment by domestic AI startups signals an irreversible shift: Beijing no longer treats artificial intelligence as a growth asset to be maximized globally, but as strategic infrastructure to be hoarded domestically. This is not merely regulatory tightening—it mirrors the semiconductor export restrictions of 2022-2024, but applied retroactively to prevent Western LP capital from flowing into Chinese AI teams and vice versa.

The pattern spans trade, talent, and technology: US curbs on GPU exports + China's ban on outbound AI capital = bifurcated innovation ecosystems with minimal cross-pollination. Unlike Brexit (which was largely self-imposed friction), this decoupling is bilateral and now explicitly codified. Chinese startups will be forced to accept state backing, domestic VC, and Chinese institutional LP capital—all of which come with implicit tech-transfer expectations and political oversight.

For a 21-year-old entrepreneur in deeptech, the implication is stark: the era of borderless VC capital chasing the best AI talent globally is closing. If you're building AI (especially large language models, synthetic data, or autonomous systems), you must now assume that cross-border capital and talent mobility will face friction. European founders should expect US and Chinese VCs to become less available for pure tech plays that touch "dual-use" domains. Build your moat via unfair advantage (proprietary data, regulatory compliance, energy efficiency), not via access to cheap capital that evaporates when geopolitics shifts.
