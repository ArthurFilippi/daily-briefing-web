      🌍 Geopolitics & Policy

      Houthis Command Red Sea Straits; Energy Markets Convulse

      Iran-backed Houthis captured Perim island in Bab al-Mandab and now control the entire Red Sea coastline, cementing dominance over a critical shipping artery. Concurrently, Saudi Arabia announced shutdown of a major oil pipeline following drone attacks launched from Iraqi territory. Oil briefly spiked to $110/barrel amid fears of widening Middle East escalation. This consolidates Iranian leverage: two months after the Hormuz closure, Tehran can now negotiate from a position of dual-strait control, amplifying its hand in future diplomatic talks.
      Source: NYT, FT

      BRICS Summit in Delhi: Fractures Beneath Facade of Unity

      China, Russia, India, Iran, and partners convene in New Delhi this weekend amid soaring energy prices and divergent war interests. Consensus on Western decoupling masks deep tensions: India's trade deficit with China has nearly doubled in five years, straining New Delhi's industrial policy; Middle East conflicts pit members' competing alignments. Finding operational unity remains difficult despite the nominal bloc's expansion rhetoric.
      Source: NYT

      France Mobilizes to Lock In EU Agenda Pre-Election

      EU diplomats confirm Paris has urged the bloc to finalize budget and climate measures before France's presidential election in April 2027. The move reflects anxiety over Le Pen's potential victory and her party's EU-skeptic platform. Early agenda-setting signals institutional risk concentration in the next 7 months.
      Source: FT

      Africa CDC Chief Reshapes Disease Response Model

      Dr. Jean Kaseya, head of Africa CDC, is pushing to move control of Ebola and other epidemic responses from international agencies and foreign donors to African institutions. This represents a structural shift in global health governance—political will and funding continuity remain testing points.
      Source: NYT

      🔍 Signal of the Day

      Energy Chokepoints and AI Capex: Structural Margin Pressure for 2026–2028

      The simultaneous tightening of two supply chains—Middle East oil routes and semiconductor/data center power and cooling capacity—creates a dual margin squeeze for NASDAQ-heavy portfolios. Houthis' capture of Bab al-Mandab and Hormuz closure now impose $50 billion+ in monthly fuel cost externalities across Europe; US tanker routing adds 14–20% to transit time. Simultaneously, Oracle's $700 million layoff acceleration and Larry Ellison's $7.5 billion stock sale signal that frontier AI capex commitments ($500M–$1B+/quarter per lab) have exceeded internal cash generation. Nvidia's $10B Anthropic IPO backing and Mistral's €3B raise reflect capital markets' willingness to fund this infrastructure race, but execution risk persists. A 25 bps Fed hike next week compounds the pressure: higher refinance costs for leveraged buyout lending (KKR's $2.1B Integer loan signals continued LBO appetite, but spreads will widen) and delayed corporate capex payback periods. For entrepreneurs, this creates bifurcation: (1) deep-tech plays with government contracts (Exploration Company's €760M ESA deal) or captive buyers (defense, energy security) enjoy policy tailwinds; (2) software-as-a-service businesses face renewed unit-economics scrutiny as cost-of-capital rises and customer procurement cycles lengthen amid macro uncertainty.
