🔍 Signal of the Day

Memory chip shortage + AI capex = end of "easy money" for diversified semiconductors

The convergence is clear: Kioxia's buy-order freeze and ChangXin's 8x revenue jump signal structural allocation toward AI-enabling memory (DRAM, NAND) and away from generalist chip demand. Samsung's union negotiations underscore rising labor costs in a capacity-constrained environment.

This mirrors a broader pattern in deeptech capex: companies betting on AI infrastructure (data centers, chips, optical networks) are absorbing capital and labor at rates that crowd out adjacent tech sectors. Margins compress for non-essential compute; capex intensity rises everywhere.

For a 21-year-old entrepreneur building in deeptech: this is the inverse of 2020–2022's democratization narrative. The easy path—sell AI software, compile models, train smaller LLMs—now saturates. Capital flows upward into capital-intensive layers (semiconductor fabs, data-center operators, foundational inference platforms). Your venture's leverage comes from enabling that infrastructure, not replicating it. Publicis's LiveRamp play exemplifies this: they're not building another LLM; they're building the identity and data pipes that LLMs need to run on customer data. That's where strategic scarcity and defensibility live now.
