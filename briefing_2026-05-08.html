🌍 Geopolitics & Policy

Strait of Hormuz Closure Collapses Oil Demand by 5 Million Barrels/Day

S&P Global data confirms the Iran war has triggered global oil demand destruction of 5 million barrels/day—the steepest decline since the COVID-19 pandemic. Despite Trump's assertion that the US-Iran ceasefire remains intact after Thursday's exchange of fire in the Strait of Hormuz, Iran accused the US of targeting tankers and coastal areas, creating persistent risk of escalation. Analysts project oil could reach $90/barrel under truce scenarios or spike to $150+ if conflict widens, reshaping energy markets and industrial strategy across NATO Europe. Source: Le Grand Continent

US Court Invalidates Trump's 10% Global Tariff, Second Legal Defeat

The US International Trade Court ruled on Thursday that Trump overstepped constitutional authority when invoking Section 1974 trade law to impose blanket 10% tariffs on all imports. This marks the second judicial nullification of the policy—the Supreme Court previously struck down predecessor tariffs as illegal. The decision constrains Trump's trade leverage precisely as Iran tensions reshape supply chains and energy costs. Source: Financial Times

France Approves €36 Billion Military Spending Increase, Hardening Strategic Autonomy

The National Assembly approved a new military programming law trajectory adding €36 billion to defense budgets, reflecting France and NATO's pivot toward wartime posture. Debates stalled within timeline constraints, deferring final votes until Tuesday—signaling institutional friction as geopolitical pressure mounts against procurement timelines. Source: Le Monde

Russia Escalates Nuclear Rhetoric; Kremlin Experts Warn of "European Nuclear Hydra"

A Russian Foreign Ministry journal published by experts near the Kremlin promoted rhetoric describing a growing "European nuclear hydra"—a propaganda frame amplifying Moscow's perception of NATO expansion. The messaging emerged amid deteriorating Russian economic conditions, internet restrictions, and military strain, signaling Moscow's reliance on narratives over material capability as Victory Day approaches. Source: Le Grand Continent

Paris-based OpsMill closed a $14 million Series A led by IRIS with participation from BGV and existing backers Serena and Partech. Sweden's SWEBAL Raises €30M to Build NATO's First Domestic TNT Facility

Sweden Ballistics announced €30 million in funding to construct Sweden's first trinitrotoluene (TNT) manufacturing plant, directly addressing NATO ammunition supply fragility exposed by the Iran conflict. The strategic infrastructure play underscores how geopolitical hardening is mobilizing deeptech capital into defense-critical manufacturing. Source: Tech.eu

Skeleton Technologies (Tallinn) Announces €33M First Close, Eyes 2027 US IPO

Estonia-based Skeleton Technologies, an AI infrastructure and grid power systems provider, disclosed the opening €33 million tranche of a pre-IPO round, bringing total venture capital to €392 million. The IPO preparation signals European deeptech's confidence in exit markets as energy resilience becomes geopolitically foundational. Source: EU-Startups

Meatly Raises £10.4M Series A for Europe's Largest Cultivated Meat Bioreactor

UK cultivated meat pioneer Meatly closed £10.4 million in Series A funding to build Europe's largest bioreactor facility in London. The company is Europe's first to commercialize cultivated meat and is using this capital to scale manufacturing infrastructure amid growing food security concerns across NATO energy-constrained economies. Source: Tech.eu

🔍 Signal of the Day

The Iran War is Forcing European Energy Autarky—and Reshaping VC Capital Allocation Toward Defense Deeptech

The fact: Strait of Hormuz closure triggered a 5 million barrel/day oil demand collapse; simultaneously, France approved €36B additional military spending, Sweden funded TNT manufacturing, and European grid operators activated wartime sabotage protocols. Venture capital is now flowing aggressively into defense infrastructure (72 European startups mapped), quantum computing, cultivated meat, and power systems.

Broader pattern: Geopolitical shocks have historically compressed venture investment horizons and redirected capital from consumer internet toward state-critical infrastructure. The Iran war, combined with five-year-old energy crisis memory, is triggering a structural reallocation: European founders are pivoting away from SaaS and consumer tech toward physical infrastructure, energy independence, and NATO-adjacent deeptech. Principal Financial's $3B data-center commitment, SWEBAL's €30M TNT facility, and Skeleton Technologies' €33M IPO raise all cluster around the same thesis—that geopolitical fragmentation is making distributed, resilient, sovereign infrastructure investable on risk-adjusted returns.

Implication for a future entrepreneur: If you are building in France or Europe and your pitch centers on cloud SaaS or consumer mobility, you are fighting VC momentum. The capital is flowing toward: (1) energy resilience (solar, heat pumps, grid software), (2) critical manufacturing (ammunition, semiconductors, biotech), (3) quantum/AI applied to defense or supply-chain transparency, and (4) data sovereignty infrastructure. The window for pivoting a consumer play into a strategic one may close within 18 months as geopolitical hardening accelerates budget allocation. If you can credibly connect your technology to NATO resilience, energy independence, or semiconductor supply, the funding environment is shifting in your favor—but only if you can move at scale and with institutional credibility faster than the current capital velocity.
