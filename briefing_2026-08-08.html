    🌍 Geopolitics & Policy

    Ukraine's Warehouse Campaign Escalates Against Russian E-Commerce

    Ukrainian drones have systematically targeted Wildberries warehouses, the largest e-commerce operator in Russia. Co-founder Tatyana Kim, a billionaire whose business empire spans logistics and fashion retail, faces severe asset destruction as strikes accelerate. The campaign reflects Ukraine's shift to targeting civilian supply chains that fund Russian state revenue and household consumption. This marks a departure from traditional military targets and raises questions about the durability of cross-border commerce during prolonged conflict. Source: NYT

    Saudi Arabia, Turkey, Pakistan Forge Tripartite Defense Pact

    The three nations signed a mutual defense treaty in Mecca on August 7, binding signatories to treat an attack on one as an attack on all. The agreement marks a significant rebalancing in Middle Eastern geopolitics, particularly as tensions with Iran intensify. This alliance creates a new power bloc spanning South Asia, Central Asia, and the Levant—one capable of coordinating air defense, intelligence, and military operations without direct US or NATO involvement. Source: Le Monde

    Brazil's Amazon Deforestation Falls 37% Year-on-Year

    Brazilian Amazon clearing dropped to 2,874 km² (August 2025–July 2026), down from 4,563 km² in the prior twelve months and vastly below the 10,278 km² peak under Bolsonaro in 2022. Lula's enforcement strategy—satellite monitoring, increased fines, federal intervention—has delivered measurable results, though sustainability depends on maintaining political will beyond 2026. Source: Le Monde

    Colombia's New President De la Espriella Takes Office; US Pledges $1B Aid

    Conservative lawyer Abelardo de la Espriella (48) was inaugurated as Colombia's president, pledging a "relentless offensive" against narcoterrorism and criminal organizations. Washington announced $1 billion in assistance, signaling alignment with his Trump-friendly stance on drug enforcement and military modernization. The move consolidates US influence in a region destabilized by gang consolidation and cocaine production growth. Source: DW

Markets rallied on the data, pricing in near-term rate relief—a rare sign that growth fears now outweigh inflation concerns in investor calculus. Source: FT

    🔍 Signal of the Day

    Amazon's $7.65 GW Texas Gas Plant Signals Decoupling of AI Energy & ESG Narratives

    Amazon announced it is building the largest natural gas power plant in the US—a 35-turbine, 7.65 gigawatt facility in Texas—explicitly to power its hyperscaler data centers supporting AI workloads. The admission breaks the company's public 2040 carbon neutrality pledge and reflects a hard engineering reality: AI inference and training cannot reliably run on variable renewables at the scale required for competitive model deployment.

    This reveals a broader pattern: every hyperscaler—OpenAI, Meta, Google, Anthropic—has quietly begun either acquiring fossil fuel peaker capacity or signing long-term nuclear deals. The green energy narrative, still dominant in public messaging, has collided with the physics of semiconductor power density. A single modern GPU cluster demands 10–100 MW sustained; renewable grids cannot guarantee that.

    For an entrepreneur betting on deeptech: the infrastructure constraints are not regulatory or financial—they are thermodynamic. Any AI or semiconductor venture that assumes grid parity or rapid renewable scaling is misreading the technical roadmap. Conversely, this creates massive opportunity in: (1) small modular nuclear, (2) hybrid diesel-hydrogen peaker systems, (3) heat recovery and waste-energy recycling in data centers, and (4) geopolitically-resilient energy partnerships (Saudi Arabia, UAE) for cloud regions. The next decade's industrial winners will be those who solve the energy density problem, not those who deny it.
